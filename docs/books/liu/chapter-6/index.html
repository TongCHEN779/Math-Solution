Conventions and scope. Labels refer to the four exercise lists in Chapter 6 of the supplied 2002 text (printed pp. 218–251). Statements are restated, with subparts in running prose. Write \(\Omega_{B/A}=\Omega^1_{B/A}\), and similarly for schemes. A function field means a finitely generated field extension. A regular sequence in a local ring is contained in its maximal ideal. Standard results used below include the conormal and transitivity sequences, the local criterion for flatness, Nakayama's lemma, the Koszul criterion for a regular sequence, and cohomology and base change.

Corrections and limitations. Some requested conclusions are false under the printed hypotheses. 6.1 Kahler differentials

Exercise 1.1

Problem

Let \(B\) be an \(A\)-algebra, \(\rho:B\otimes_A B\to B\) multiplication, and \(I=\Ker\rho\). Give \(I/I^2\) the \(B\)-action from the right factor and put \(db=b\otimes1-1\otimes b\) modulo \(I^2\). (a) Prove that \(d\) is an \(A\)-derivation. (b) Prove its universal property. (c) For a morphism \(f:X\to Y\) and its diagonal \(\Delta\), let \(\mathcal I=\Ker(\mathcal O_{X\times_YX}\to\Delta_*\mathcal O_X)\). Prove \(\Omega_{X/Y}\simeq\Delta^*(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2)\).

Solution

(a) Additivity and \(da=0\) for \(a\in A\) follow from the tensor relations. The difference \(d(bc)-b\,dc-c\,db\) is the product \((b\otimes1-1\otimes b)(c\otimes1-1\otimes c)\), hence is zero modulo \(I^2\).

(b) For an \(A\)-derivation \(D:B\to M\), define \(h:B\otimes_A B\to M\) by \(h(b\otimes c)=cD(b)\). This is well defined and right \(B\)-linear. For \(u,v\in B\otimes_A B\), direct expansion of the Leibniz rule gives \(h(uv)=\rho(u)h(v)+\rho(v)h(u)\). Thus \(h(I^2)=0\) and its restriction gives a map \(I/I^2\to M\) sending \(db\) to \(D(b)\). Every \(u=\sum b_i\otimes c_i\in I\) equals \(\sum (1\otimes c_i)(b_i\otimes1-1\otimes b_i)\), so these elements generate \(I\), proving uniqueness.

(c) On \(U=\Spec B\to V=\Spec A\), the diagonal inside \(U\times_VU\) has precisely this ideal. The isomorphism in (b) is compatible with restriction and localization and therefore glues. Such product opens cover a neighborhood of the diagonal, which suffices for its pullback. No separatedness hypothesis is needed. ∎

Exercise 1.2

Problem

Show that \(\Omega_{A[[T]]/A}\) need not be finitely generated. Prove that formal differentiation induces an exact sequence \(0\to\bigcap_{n\ge1}T^n\Omega_{A[[T]]/A}\to\Omega_{A[[T]]/A}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A[[T]]dT\) with \(\varphi(dF)=F'dT\).

Solution

Put \(R=A[[T]]\) and \(M=\Omega_{R/A}\). Formal differentiation is an \(A\)-derivation, so defines \(\varphi\), which is surjective since \(\varphi(dT)=dT\). Write \(F=P+T^{n+1}G\) with \(P\in A[T]\). Then

\[ dF-F'dT=T^{n+1}(dG-G'dT)\in T^nM. \]

Since \(M\) is generated by the \(dF\), this shows that \(M/T^nM\to(R/T^nR)dT\) is an isomorphism: its inverse sends \(dT\) to \(dT\). Consequently \(\Ker\varphi\subseteq\bigcap_n T^nM\). The reverse inclusion follows from \(\bigcap_nT^nR=0\).

For non-finiteness take \(A=\Q\). Localization identifies \(M\otimes_R\Q((T))\) with \(\Omega_{\Q((T))/\Q}\). The field \(\Q((T))\) has infinite transcendence degree over \(\Q\): any field of finite transcendence degree over a countable field is countable, whereas \(\Q((T))\) is uncountable. In characteristic zero the differentials of a transcendence basis form a basis of the differential module after the remaining algebraic extension. Its dimension is therefore infinite, ruling out finite generation of \(M\). ∎

Exercise 1.3

Problem

For an ideal \(I\subset B\) and its adic completion \(\widehat B\), let \(\rho:\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{\widehat B/A}\). (a) Show \(\Omega_{\widehat B/A}=\rho(\Omega_{B/A})+I\Omega_{\widehat B/A}\) and obtain surjections modulo \(I^n\) with kernels \(K_n\) satisfying \(K_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow K_n\). (b) If \(A\) is Noetherian and \(B\) is a localization of a finite type \(A\)-algebra, identify \(\varprojlim_n\Omega_{\widehat B/A}/I^n\Omega_{\widehat B/A}\) with \(\Omega_{B/A}\otimes_B\widehat B\).

Solution

Scope of (a): assume \(B\) Noetherian. This guarantees \(\widehat B/I^j\widehat B=B/I^j\). Put \(M=\Omega_{B/A}\) and \(N=\Omega_{\widehat B/A}\). In fact \(M/I^nM\to N/I^nN\) is an isomorphism. Here is a useful proof which also controls the kernels. If \(E\) is a \(B/I^n\)-module, any derivation \(D:B\to E\) annihilates \(I^{n+1}\) by the product rule. It therefore extends uniquely to \(\widehat B\) through

\[ \widehat B\longrightarrow B/I^{n+1}, \]

using the derivation on \(B/I^{n+1}\) induced by \(D\). Conversely a derivation on \(\widehat B\) into \(E\) annihilates \((I\widehat B)^{n+1}\), so is determined by its restriction to \(B\). The two differential modules modulo \(I^n\) represent the same functor on \(B/I^n\)-modules. This proves the isomorphism, and in particular \(K_n=0\) and the requested transition surjectivity. At \(n=1\) it also proves the displayed equality: coefficients modulo \(I\) lift from \(B\).

(b) The hypotheses imply that \(B\) is Noetherian and \(M\) is finite. Taking inverse limits gives \(\widehat M\), and the completion theorem for finite modules gives \(\widehat M=M\otimes_B\widehat B\). For a general non-Noetherian \(B\) the identification of completion quotients used above requires additional hypotheses; no proof of that unrestricted case is supplied. ∎

Exercise 1.4

Problem

If \(X\to Y\) is a morphism and \(Y\to Z\) an open or closed immersion, prove that \(\Omega_{X/Z}\to\Omega_{X/Y}\) is an isomorphism.

Solution

For an open immersion work in an open subset of \(Z\) identified with \(Y\). For a closed immersion, write the maps locally as \(A\to A/J\to C\). An \(A\)-derivation on \(C\) is precisely an \(A/J\)-derivation: the images of elements of \(A/J\) are images of elements of \(A\). Thus the two universal differential modules agree, compatibly with restriction. ∎

Exercise 1.5

Problem

For \(S\)-schemes \(X,Y\) with product projections \(p,q\), prove \(\Omega_{(X\times_SY)/S}\simeq p^*\Omega_{X/S}\oplus q^*\Omega_{Y/S}\). (The left-hand base subscript is omitted in the print.)

Solution

On affine charts set \(R=B\otimes_A C\). A derivation \(R\to M\) is uniquely specified by derivations \(D_B:B\to M\), \(D_C:C\to M\), and is given by \(D(b\otimes c)=cD_B(b)+bD_C(c)\). Hence its representing module is \((\Omega_{B/A}\otimes_B R)\oplus(\Omega_{C/A}\otimes_C R)\). These isomorphisms are natural and glue. ∎

Exercise 1.6

Problem

Let \(K\subseteq L\) be function fields separable over \(k\). Show that \(\Omega_{K/k}\otimes_KL\to\Omega_{L/k}\) is injective if and only if \(L/K\) is separable.

Solution

Write \(a=\operatorname{trdeg}_kK\), \(b=\operatorname{trdeg}_kL\). By Prop. 6.1.15 the first two modules have dimensions \(a,b\). Transitivity shows that injectivity is equivalent to \(\dim_L\Omega_{L/K}=b-a=\operatorname{trdeg}_KL\). By the same proposition this is precisely separability of \(L/K\). ∎

Exercise 1.7

Problem

If \(K\) is perfect of characteristic \(p>0\) and \(L/K\) is any field extension, prove \(K\subseteq L^p\) and \(\Omega_{L/K}\simeq\Omega_{L/L^p}\).

Solution

Every element of \(K\) is a \(p\)th power in \(K\), hence in \(L\). Every derivation in characteristic \(p\) kills \(p\)th powers. Thus a derivation on \(L\) kills \(K\) if and only if it kills \(L^p\): one direction follows from the containment, the other from the product rule. Apply the universal property. ∎

Exercise 1.8

Problem

Let \(X\) be smooth of dimension \(d\) over \(K\). (a) For a function field \(L/K\) put \(r=\dim_L\Omega_{L/K}\); show that \(L\) can be generated over \(K\) by \(r+1\) elements. (b) Deduce that \(k(x)\) can be generated by \(d+1\) elements for every \(x\in X\).

Solution

(a) Choose \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) with \(du_i\) a basis and set \(E=K(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\). Transitivity gives \(\Omega_{L/E}=0\). A finitely generated field extension with zero differential module is finite separable (Prop. 6.1.15 followed by Lemma 6.1.13(c)). The primitive element theorem gives \(L=E(v)\), proving the assertion, without requiring that the \(u_i\) be algebraically independent.

(b) The conormal sequence for \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\to k(x)\) surjects \(\Omega_{X/K}\otimes k(x)\) onto \(\Omega_{k(x)/K}\). The source has dimension at most \(d\), so (a) applies with \(r\le d\). ∎

Exercise 1.9

Problem

Let \(S=\Spec\Z_p\) and \(X=\Spec\Z_p[u,v]/(uv^2+p)\). Prove that \(X\) is regular and integral and that \((\Omega_{X/S})_{\rm tors}\) is nonzero coherent with support \(V(v)\).

Solution

Put \(R=\Z_p[u,v]/(uv^2+p)\). Gauss's lemma applied over \(\Z_p[v]\) shows that the primitive linear polynomial \(v^2u+p\) is irreducible, hence prime in this UFD; thus \(R\) is a domain. On \(D(v)\), \(R_v=\Z_p[v,v^{-1}]\) is regular. A prime containing \(v\) contains \(p\); in the regular ambient local ring of \(\Z_p[u,v]\) at that prime, \(p\) is part of a regular system of parameters and \(uv^2\in\fm^2\). Thus \(p+uv^2\notin\fm^2\), and its quotient is regular. This covers \(X\).

Now

\[ \Omega_{R/\Z_p}=(R\,du\oplus R\,dv)/R\,v(v\,du+2u\,dv). \]

Set \(w=v\,du+2u\,dv\). The saturation of the relation depends on whether \(p=2\), so we separate the cases.

If \(p\ne2\), the vector \(w\) is primitive at every height-one prime: the unique height-one prime containing \(v\) is \((v)\), and \(2u\) is a unit there. Since \(R\) is normal, its saturated span in \(R^2\) is \(Rw\). Consequently the torsion is \(Rw/Rvw\simeq R/(v)\). If \(p=2\), the relation becomes \(v^2(du-u^2v\,dv)=0\), since \(2=-uv^2\). The vector \(du-u^2v\,dv\) is part of a basis of \(R^2\), so the torsion is \(R/(v^2)\) times this vector. In both cases it is nonzero, coherent, and has exactly the asserted support. ∎

6.2 Differential study of smooth morphisms

Exercise 2.1

Problem

If \(X\to S\) is smooth and \(x\in X_s\) is \(k(s)\)-rational, prove \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,s}[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]\) as complete base algebras, with \(n=\dim\mathcal O_{X_s,x}\).

Solution

Choose relative functions vanishing at \(x\) whose differentials form a basis. They define an etale map from a neighborhood of \(x\) to \(\mathbf A^n_S\), taking \(x\) to the origin over \(s\) (Cor. 6.2.11). An etale local map with identical residue fields induces an isomorphism on completed local rings: successive lifts across powers of the maximal ideal are unique by formal etaleness. The completed local ring at that origin is \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,s}[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]\). This also respects the base map. ∎

Exercise 2.2

Problem

For an algebraic variety \(X/k\), the print asks to prove: \(X\) is smooth if and only if \(\Omega_{X/k}\) is locally free and every generic residue field \(k(\xi)/k\) is separable.

Solution

A generically reduced hypothesis is needed. Liu's varieties are finite type schemes, not necessarily reduced. In characteristic \(p\), \(X=\Spec k[t]/(t^p)\) has free differential module \(\mathcal O_Xdt\), and its only residue field is \(k\), but is not smooth. Thus the printed statement is false.

Here is the corrected assertion for generically reduced \(X\) (in particular for reduced varieties). Smoothness implies both conditions. Conversely, near a point where \(\Omega\) is free of rank \(r\), each irreducible component has dimension \(r\): at its generic point the local ring is a field by generic reducedness, and separability identifies the differential rank with its transcendence degree. Thus \(\dim_xX=r\) throughout this neighborhood. Proposition 6.2.2, the criterion that \(\Omega_{X/k,x}\) be free of rank \(\dim_xX\), now gives smoothness. ∎

Exercise 2.3

Problem

The print asserts that if \(X\to S\) is smooth, then \(s\mapsto\dim X_s\) is locally constant.

Solution

False. Let \(S=\mathbf A^1_k\), \(U=D(t)\), and \(X=S\amalg\mathbf A^1_U\) with its natural smooth surjective morphism to \(S\). The fibre has dimension \(1\) on \(U\) and dimension \(0\) at \(0\).

The valid general statement is that \(x\mapsto\dim_xX_{f(x)}\) is locally constant on \(X\): it equals the rank of the locally free sheaf \(\Omega_{X/S}\). If the morphism is proper and smooth, its nonempty fibre dimensions are locally constant on its image. Indeed, near any base point only finitely many relative-rank strata occur; each is open and closed in \(X\), and its image is open by smoothness and closed by properness. The maximum of the ranks present is therefore locally constant. ∎

Exercise 2.4

Problem

For a smooth connected variety \(X/k\) in characteristic \(p>0\) and relative Frobenius \(F:X\to X^{(p)}\), compute \(\Omega_{X/X^{(p)}}\): (a) for affine space and (b) in general.

Solution

Locally the relative Frobenius homomorphism is \(B\otimes_{k,F_k}k\to B\), \(b\otimes c\mapsto b^pc\). Every \(k\)-derivation kills its image, and every derivation relative to that image kills \(k\). Hence, canonically,

\[ \Omega_{X/X^{(p)}}=\Omega_{X/k}. \]

For affine space the basis is \(dT_1,\ldots,dT_n\). In general smoothness makes this sheaf locally free; on connected \(X\) its rank is constant and equals \(\dim X\). This argument does not require that \(k\) be perfect. ∎

Exercise 2.5

Problem

For \(X\to S\) of finite type and \(x\in X_s\), (a) Prove

\[ \dim_{k(x)}T_{X_s,x}\le\dim_{k(x)}(\Omega_{X_s/k(s),x}\otimes k(x)). \]

(b) Give the exact sequence

\[ \fm_x/\fm_x^2\to\Omega_{X_s/k(s),x}\otimes k(x)\to\Omega_{k(x)/k(s)}\to0. \]

(c) If \(x\) is closed in \(X_s\) with separable residue field, identify the dual of \(\Omega_{X/S}\otimes k(x)\) with \(T_{X_s,x}\).

Solution

(a) By Lemma 6.2.4, near \(x\) there is a closed immersion \(X\to Z\) with \(Z/S\) smooth of relative dimension \(d=\dim_{k(x)}\Omega_{X/S}\otimes k(x)\). Closed immersions inject Zariski tangent spaces. The embedding dimension of the regular local ring \(\mathcal O_{Z_s,x}\) equals its dimension, which is at most \(d\). This proves the inequality, including at nonclosed points.

(b) Put \(R=\mathcal O_{X_s,x}\) and \(\fn=\fm_R\). The conormal sequence for \(R\to k(x)\) is

\[ \fn/\fn^2\longrightarrow\Omega_{R/k(s)}\otimes_R k(x)\longrightarrow\Omega_{k(x)/k(s)}\longrightarrow0. \]

The map \(\fm_x/\fm_x^2\to\fn/\fn^2\) is surjective, giving exactly the requested sequence.

(c) Now \(k(x)/k(s)\) is finite separable. In addition the first map from \(\fn/\fn^2\) is injective: a finite separable residue extension has a coefficient-field lift into \(R/\fn^2\) by formal etaleness, and the resulting split square-zero algebra shows that its relative differentials tensored with \(k(x)\) are \(\fn/\fn^2\). Thus \(\Omega_{X/S}\otimes k(x)\simeq\fn/\fn^2\). Dualizing gives the tangent space. ∎

Exercise 2.6

Problem

Let \(X\to Y\) be of finite type between locally Noetherian regular schemes, and let \(x\in X_y\) be closed with \(k(x)/k(y)\) separable. (a) Locally embed \(X\) into a smooth \(Y\)-scheme of relative dimension \(\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}\). (b) Locally realize \(X\) as a closed subscheme of codimension \(\dim\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) in a smooth \(Y\)-scheme.

Solution

Set \(a=\dim\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) and \(b=\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}\). By 2.5(c), the dimension \(e\) of \(\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes k(x)\) is the embedding dimension of the fibre local ring and is at most \(b\), since that fibre ring is a quotient of \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\). Lemma 6.2.4 embeds a neighborhood in a smooth \(Y\)-scheme of relative dimension \(e\). Take its product with \(\mathbf A^{b-e}_Y\) and embed \(X\) using zero in the new coordinates. This proves (a).

The ambient local ring at \(x\) is regular of dimension \(a+b\), since \(x\) is closed in its fibre and has finite residue extension. Its quotient \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is regular of dimension \(b\). The kernel is therefore generated by \(a\) members of a regular system of parameters: lift a basis of the kernel on cotangent spaces and complete it to a parameter basis. The quotient by those \(a\) parameters is a regular local domain of dimension \(b\), so its further prime quotient of dimension \(b\) has zero kernel. Spreading these generators to a neighborhood proves (b). ∎

Exercise 2.7

Problem

Let \(X\to S\) be finite type and smooth at a point \(x\) closed in \(X_s\). (a) If \(\dim X_s>0\), find a closed subscheme \(Z\) through \(x\), smooth at \(x\), with smaller special-fibre dimension. (b) Find an etale \(T\to S\) such that \(X_T\to T\) has a section whose composite to \(X\) contains \(x\).

Solution

Both parts need a separability hypothesis on \(k(x)/k(s)\). Let \(k=\mathbf F_p(a)\), \(S=\Spec k\), \(X=\mathbf A^1_k\), and take the closed point defined by \(T^p-a\). Its residue field is purely inseparable of degree \(p\). A zero-dimensional closed subscheme through this point cannot be smooth over \(k\): a smooth zero-dimensional \(k\)-scheme has finite separable residue fields. Thus (a) is false. Similarly an etale \(T\to\Spec k\) cannot have a point mapping to \(x\), since that would embed \(k(x)\) in a finite separable extension of \(k\). This disproves (b).

For a corrected version assume \(k(x)/k(s)\) finite separable. Locally at \(x\), if the relative dimension \(n\) is positive, 2.5(c) gives a function vanishing at \(x\) with nonzero relative differential. Its zero locus on a sufficiently small affine neighborhood is smooth at \(x\) of relative dimension \(n-1\), by the Jacobian criterion. This proves the useful local form of (a). No bound on the whole fibre of a global closure is asserted here.

For (b) under this extra assumption choose etale coordinates \(h:U\to\mathbf A^n_S\) near \(x\). Lift the finite separable extension \(k(x)/k(s)\) to an etale neighborhood \(S'\to S\), and lift the coordinates of \(h(x)\) to functions on \(S'\) near its distinguished point. These define a section \(a:S'\to\mathbf A^n_S\) whose distinguished fibre value agrees with \(h(x)\) over \(k(x)\). The fibre product \(T=U\times_{\mathbf A^n_S}S'\) is etale over \(S\) and has a point mapping to \(x\). Its map \(T\to U\to X\) gives the required section of \(X_T\to T\). ∎

Exercise 2.8

Problem

Let \(S\) be Noetherian local with closed point \(s\). If \(X\to S\) is proper and flat and \(X_s/k(s)\) is smooth, prove that \(X/S\) is smooth.

Solution

The nonsmooth locus is closed, since a proper morphism is finite type and smoothness is open in this setting. Its image is closed by properness. It misses \(s\), since flatness and geometric regularity of the special fibre give smoothness there. Every nonempty closed subset of a local scheme contains its closed point; hence the image is empty. ∎

Exercise 2.9

Problem

Let \(f:X\to Y\) be dominant of finite type between locally Noetherian integral schemes. Call it generically separable (respectively generically etale) when \(K(X)/K(Y)\) is separable (respectively finite separable); the print reverses this extension. (a) Find a dense open smooth (respectively etale) locus. (b) If \(f\) is proper and its generic fibre is smooth, find a nonempty open \(V\subset Y\) over which \(f\) is smooth (respectively etale under the corresponding generic assumption).

Solution

(a) At the generic point of \(X\), the morphism is smooth: choose a separating transcendence basis of \(K(X)/K(Y)\) followed by a primitive element for the finite separable part. Clearing denominators gives a presentation etale over an affine space on a nonempty open of \(X\) (also shrink the base to clear coefficients). Smoothness is open, so this yields the assertion. If the field extension is finite separable the affine space has dimension zero and the morphism is etale there.

(b) Let \(C\) be the closed nonsmooth locus. The whole generic fibre is smooth by assumption, so \(f(C)\) is a proper closed subset; take its complement. In the generically etale case the smooth generic fibre is integral and zero-dimensional, hence finite separable and everywhere etale. Use instead the closed non-etale locus. Properness of \(f\) is what makes its image closed. ∎

Exercise 2.10

Problem

For an \(S\)-morphism \(f:X\to Y\) between smooth \(S\)-schemes, prove exactness of \(0\to f^*\Omega_{Y/S}\to\Omega_{X/S}\to\Omega_{X/Y}\to0\) (a) if \(f\) is smooth, and (b) if \(X,Y\) are integral and \(f\) is dominant and generically separable.

Solution

(a) Smoothness gives, locally, an etale factorization through affine space over \(Y\). For a polynomial algebra the sequence splits with complementary basis the relative coordinate differentials. Etale pullback preserves this description, proving left injectivity as well as right exactness.

(b) At the generic point, choose an affine base neighborhood \(\Spec R\subset S\). Its image in \(K(Y)\) is a domain with fraction field \(k\). Since \(X,Y\) are smooth over \(S\), localization and base change identify their differential spaces with those of the separably generated fields \(K(X),K(Y)\) over \(k\). Exercise 1.6 gives generic injectivity. The kernel is a subsheaf of the locally free sheaf \(f^*\Omega_{Y/S}\) on integral \(X\); a section of a locally free sheaf which vanishes generically is zero. Right exactness is transitivity. ∎

Exercise 2.11

Problem

For a finite map of Noetherian rings \(A\to B\) with \(A\) complete local and residue field \(k\), let \(k'\) be the largest separable \(k\)-subalgebra of \(B\otimes_Ak\). Factor the map through a simple finite etale \(A\)-algebra \(C\) with \(C\otimes_Ak=k'\).

Solution

We use two standard properties of finite etale algebras over a complete local ring: reduction modulo the maximal ideal is an equivalence with finite etale \(k\)-algebras, and morphisms from such algebras lift uniquely across nilpotent thickenings. Let \(C\) lift \(k'\). The inclusion \(k'\to B/\fm_AB\) lifts uniquely through \(B/\fm_A^nB\). Taking limits gives \(C\to B\), since the finite \(A\)-module \(B\) is complete. No injectivity of this map is asserted or required: the original \(A o B\) need not be injective. The algebra \(B\) is finite over \(C\) because any finite set of \(A\)-module generators also generates over \(C\).

For the additional monogenicity requirement, first suppose \(k\) is infinite. Then choose primitive elements in its field factors with pairwise coprime minimal polynomials, using translations. If \(k\) is finite, the maximal separable subalgebra can have arbitrarily many identical residue-field factors. Thus monogenicity is not automatic in this case: for \(k=\mathbf F_2\), \(B=k^3\), and \(A=k\), the largest separable subalgebra is \(k^3\), which no single element generates (a single element has only the two possible coordinate values). The finite etale factorization always exists; the additional printed requirement “\(C\) simple” is false without an extra monogenicity hypothesis on \(k'\), for example \(k\) infinite. In that case lift a primitive element; Nakayama shows its lift generates \(C\) over \(A\). ∎

6.3 Local complete intersection

Exercise 3.1

Problem

For a regular sequence \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) in the maximal ideal of a Noetherian local ring, (a) reduce invariance under permutations to two elements, and (b) for \(J=\Ann(a_2)\) show \(J=a_1J\), deduce \(J=0\), and prove \((a_2,a_1)\) regular.

Solution

(a) Adjacent transpositions generate the symmetric group. To interchange positions \(i,i+1\), work modulo the preceding elements; the quotient by the two elements, and hence the regularity of the remaining tail, is unchanged.

(b) If \(a_2x=0\), regularity of \(a_2\) modulo \(a_1\) gives \(x=a_1y\). Since \(a_1\) is a nonzerodivisor, \(a_2y=0\). Thus \(J=a_1J\). This finite ideal is zero by Nakayama. Finally if \(a_1x=a_2y\), reduction modulo \(a_1\) gives \(y=a_1z\), and cancellation gives \(x=a_2z\). Thus \(a_1\) is regular modulo \(a_2\). ∎

Exercise 3.2

Problem

Keep the preceding hypotheses. Put \(I=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) and choose minimal generators \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\). (a) After permuting the \(a_i\), show

\[ b_m=ua_n+\sum_{i<n}c_ia_i \]

with \(u\) a unit and the resulting sequence regular. (b) Prove \(m=n\) and regularity of the \(b_i\). (c) If \((J,T)\) in \(A[T]_{(\fm,T)}\) is generated by a regular sequence, prove the same for \(J\) in \(A\), also for several variables. (d) Prove descent of this property along a faithfully flat Noetherian local extension \(A\to C\).

Solution

By Lemma 6.3.6, \(I/I^2\) is free of rank \(n\) over \(A/I\), so \(\dim_{A/\fm}I/\fm I=n\). Thus \(m=n\), and a minimal generator \(b_m\) has a unit coefficient in an expression in the \(a_i\). Permute that coefficient to the last place using 3.1. Modulo the first \(n-1\) elements, \(b_m\) is a unit multiple of \(a_n\), proving (a). More generally the two minimal systems are related by an invertible matrix over \(A\), since their reductions are bases of \(I/\fm I\). Their Koszul complexes are isomorphic under this change of basis. Positive Koszul homology vanishes for the \(a_i\), hence for the \(b_i\), which proves (b) by the local Koszul criterion.

(c) A minimal system for \(J\) together with \(T\) is minimal for \((J,T)\): reduce its cotangent space first modulo \(T\), and observe that the coefficient of \(T\) cannot be supplied by \(J\subset\fm\). By (b) it is regular, in the order starting with \(T\). Modulo \(T\) the remaining elements form a regular sequence in \(A\). Iterate for several variables.

(d) A minimal system \(j_1,\ldots,j_r\) for \(J\) remains minimal after a flat local extension: \(JC/\fm_CJC\simeq(J/\fm_AJ)\otimes_{k(A)}k(C)\). By (b) it is regular in \(C\). Flat base change of the Koszul complex and faithful flatness imply that its positive homology over \(A\) vanishes. Apply the Koszul criterion again. The zero and unit ideals can be handled separately, with the empty sequence for zero. ∎

Exercise 3.3

Problem

For immersions \(X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gZ\), the print asks: if \(f\) and \(gf\) are regular, show \(g\) regular; give an example where \(g\) and \(gf\) are regular but \(f\) is not.

Solution

The first assertion is local along \(f(X)\). In a local ambient ring write \(Y=V(I)\), \(X=V(J)\), \(I\subseteq J\). Assume \(J\) and \(J/I\) are regular-sequence ideals. Lift a minimal regular system \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) of \(J/I\). Its classes are independent in \(J/\fm J\) and can be extended using elements \(a_1,\ldots,a_c\in I\) to a minimal generating system for \(J\). By 3.2 this system is regular. Since the \(b_i\) are regular modulo \(I\), their Koszul syzygies of their images generate all relations. Applying this to expressions for elements of \(I\) in \((a,b)\) shows

\[ I=(a_1,\ldots,a_c)+I(b_1,\ldots,b_r). \]

Indeed, subtract the lifted Koszul relations to make every coefficient of \(b_i\) lie in \(I\). Nakayama gives \(I=(a_1,\ldots,a_c)\), and this is a regular sequence as a subsequence of a permutable regular sequence. This proves regularity at points of \(f(X)\), hence on a neighborhood of that set.

There is no assertion away from \(f(X)\). For a global counterexample take \(Z=\mathbf A^1_k\amalg\mathbf A^2_k\), \(Y=\{0\}\amalg V(x^2,xy,y^2)\), and \(X=\{0\}\) in the first component. Both \(f\) and \(gf\) are regular, while \(g\) is not regular in the second component, whose defining ideal has height \(2\) and three minimal generators.

For the requested converse counterexample take \(Z=\Spec k[t]\), \(Y=V(t^2)\), \(X=V(t)\). Both immersions into \(Z\) are regular, but \(X\to Y\) is defined by the zero divisor \(t\) in \(k[t]/(t^2)\). ∎

Exercise 3.4

Problem

Let \((A,\fm)\) be Noetherian local and equidimensional. The print asks: (a) a regular sequence of length \(r\) has equidimensional quotient of dimension \(\dim A-r\); (b) if the next element is a zero divisor, any extension to \(m\) elements has quotient dimension at least \(\dim A-m+1\); (c) \(m\) elements cutting the dimension down by \(m\) form a regular sequence.

Solution

Equidimensionality alone is insufficient for (b) and (c). Take

\[ A=k[x,y]_{(x,y)}/(x^2,xy). \]

It has one minimal prime \((x)\) and dimension \(1\), hence is equidimensional. But \(y\) is a zero divisor, since \(xy=0\) with \(x\ne0\), whereas \(A/(y)=k[x]_{(x)}/(x^2)\) has dimension \(0\). This contradicts (b) with \(r=0,m=1\) and also (c).

A valid version of all three parts assumes \(A\) Cohen–Macaulay. A quotient by a regular sequence is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension \(\dim A-r\), and a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is equidimensional, proving (a) in this version. Its associated primes are minimal and their quotients have the full dimension. If \(a_{r+1}\) is a zero divisor it lies in one such prime \(\fp\). The remaining \(m-r-1\) elements decrease \(\dim A/\fp\) by at most that number, by the height theorem. The resulting quotient is a quotient of \(A/(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) and has dimension at least \(\dim A-m+1\). This proves (b). For (c), a first failure of regularity would contradict (b). Equivalently, extend the \(b_i\) to a system of parameters; in a Cohen–Macaulay local ring every system of parameters is regular. The unrestricted assertion (a) is not used in these notes; its equidimensional conclusion also requires care without catenarity hypotheses. ∎

Problem

Let \(f:X\to Y\) be finite surjective and l.c.i. The print asks: (a) \(\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}=\dim\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}\); (b) locally present \(X\) in \(\mathbf A^n_V\) by \(n\) equations; (c) show the fibre equations regular and deduce flatness.

Problem

For factorizations \(X\xrightarrow{i_k}Z_k\xrightarrow{g_k}Y\) with regular \(i_k\) and smooth \(g_k\), put \(\omega_{i_k,g_k}=\det\mathcal C_{X/Z_k}^{\vee}\otimes i_k^*\det\Omega_{Z_k/Y}\). (a) Use the product \(Z_1\times_YZ_2\) and the map \((i_1,i_2)\) from \(X\). Its projections to the two ambients are smooth, and its immersion is regular by Cor. 6.3.22. A triple product gives a common comparison for three choices. It is enough to compute on towers of these smooth comparisons.

(b) The conormal sequence for the two embeddings gives

\[ 0\to\mathcal C_{X/Z_1}\to\mathcal C_{X/Z_2}\to i_2^*\Omega_{Z_2/Z_1}\to0. \]

Locally choose \(\beta_j\) in the second ideal lifting a basis of the right-hand bundle. Their differentials form a relative basis along \(X\), hence near the chosen point by Nakayama. Smooth transitivity shows that \(d\alpha,d\beta\) is a basis of \(\Omega_{Z_2/Y}\) there.

(c) The same exact sequence says that the classes of \(a_i,\beta_j\) are a conormal basis. In the determinant comparison the extra conormal determinant and the extra differential determinant cancel. With the ordered-basis convention in the question the formula is therefore exactly

\[ a^*\otimes d\alpha\longmapsto(a^*\wedge\beta^*)\otimes(d\alpha\wedge d\beta). \]

A change in the complementary basis multiplies one determinant by a unit and the dual determinant by its inverse, so the formula is independent of the lifts.

(d) For a further smooth comparison choose \(\gamma\) in the third ideal lifting its relative differential basis, as in (b). Applying (c) twice yields

\[ (a^*\wedge\beta^*\wedge\gamma^*)\otimes(d\alpha\wedge d\beta\wedge d\gamma). \]

This equals the direct comparison by associativity of exterior multiplication. Thus \(\phi_{13}=\phi_{23}\phi_{12}\); the product reductions in (a) give this for arbitrary triples.

(e) The proposed index category needs a correction. Products of projective spaces are not projective spaces, and their smooth projections do not furnish the required transition maps from projective-space ambients. Indeed over a field a morphism \(\mathbf P^N\to\mathbf P^m\) with \(N>m>0\) cannot be a nonconstant smooth morphism: a basepoint-free positive-degree system of \(m+1\) forms on \(\mathbf P^N\) would have a common zero. Thus one cannot simply call the printed collection directed.

Equivalently take the colimit of this system of canonical isomorphisms after choosing a set of representatives. Restricting the factorizations to an open \(U\subset X\) restricts the same formulas, so \(\omega_{X/Y}|_U\simeq\omega_{U/Y}\). An open subscheme of a quasi-projective scheme is quasi-projective, using the locally finite type notion here. This supplies the intended construction without the unsupported directedness assertion. ∎

Problem

For a finite flat projective morphism \(f:X\to Y\), (a) construct the map \(\lambda:\mathcal O_X\to\omega_f\) from \(b\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}(b\,\cdot)\). (b) For a finite \(k\)-algebra \(E\), compare etaleness, existence of an element of trace \(1\), and perfection of the trace pairing. (c),(d) Prove \(\lambda\) is an isomorphism exactly when \(f\) is etale. (e) If \(X,Y\) are integral and \(f\) generically separable, prove \(\lambda\) injective with cokernel supported on an empty set or a pure codimension-one closed subset.

Solution

(a) On \(\Spec A\subset Y\) put \(B=\mathcal O_X(f^{-1}\Spec A)\), finite locally free over \(A\). Finite duality gives \(\omega_f\) the module \(B^\vee=\Hom_A(B,A)\) with action \((b\phi)(c)=\phi(bc)\). The trace pairing is \(B\)-linear for this action and commutes with localization, so the maps glue to \(\lambda\) and to its pushforward \(\rho\).

(b) The trace-\(1\) condition is too weak. For example over a characteristic-zero field \(E=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) has \(\operatorname{Tr}(1/2)=1\) but is not etale. The correct equivalence is: \(E\) is etale if and only if its trace pairing is perfect. Nilpotents lie in the radical of that pairing since multiplication by a nilpotent has trace zero. Perfection forces \(E\) reduced, hence a product of fields. The pairing is perfect precisely when each field factor is separable, by the field trace theorem. Conversely this condition gives perfection. For nonzero etale \(E\) the trace functional is nonzero and therefore attains \(1\); the converse fails, as above. The zero algebra is a separate trivial case for the perfect-pairing equivalence.

(c) Formation of the pairing commutes with base change. On every fibre of an etale \(f\) it is perfect by (b). Nakayama makes \(\rho\) surjective on each local base ring, and a surjection of finite free modules of the same rank is an isomorphism. The corresponding map of \(B\)-modules, hence \(\lambda\), is an isomorphism.

(d) Conversely an isomorphism remains one on every fibre. By (b) the fibres are etale. Together with finite flatness and finite presentation this says that \(f\) is etale.

(e) Over \(K(Y)\) the pairing is perfect, so \(\rho\) is generically invertible. Both its source and target are locally free over integral \(Y\); thus it is injective, and so is \(\lambda\). To prove purity on \(X\) without assuming \(\omega_f\) invertible, let \(Q=B^\vee/B\) on an affine chart. As an \(A\)-module it has a presentation \(0\to A^n\to A^n\to Q\to0\) with nonzero determinant. At a minimal prime \(\fp\) of its support, the local determinant is a nonunit nonzerodivisor; the principal ideal theorem gives \(\operatorname{ht}\fp=1\).

For purity upstairs one needs to ensure that each minimal support prime of \(Q\) lies over such a height-one prime, rather than just knowing the image. This follows by localizing a finite algebra near its fibre factors after a faithfully flat etale base change: a selected factor is again finite locally free, its trace pairing has its own nonzero determinant, and its non-isomorphism locus on the base is a principal divisor. Each such factor's minimal support points lie over its divisor's generic points. Flat going-down and integrality then give height one upstairs. Equivalently this is the local discriminant argument for finite locally free algebras, applied factor by factor. Thus the closed support is empty or pure of codimension one. ∎

\[ i_*\omega_f=\mathcal E xt^{n-r}_{\mathbf P^n_A}(i_*\mathcal O_X,\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^n_A}(-n-1)), \]

with the trace induced from the projective-space trace. This is the closed-immersion construction of the top dualizing sheaf, not an appeal to Theorem 6.4.32. A flat extension \(A\to A'\) commutes with this Ext sheaf: compute locally by a resolution by finite free modules (which may continue indefinitely); flat tensor product preserves its homology. It also pulls \(\mathcal O(-n-1)\) to the same twist and carries the projective-space trace to its base change. Thus the construction on \(X_{A'}\) is \(p^*\omega_f\) with the base-changed trace. Uniqueness of dualizing sheaves identifies it with the desired one. The identifications agree on base overlaps. For unrestricted non-Noetherian \(Y'\) an extension of this duality construction is needed and is not established here. ∎

Exercise 4.11

Problem

Let \(X=V_+(F_1,\ldots,F_{d-r})\subset P=\mathbf P^d_A\) over Noetherian \(A\), with every fibre pure of dimension \(r\). Assume the homogeneous equations have positive degrees. Without Theorem 6.4.32: (a) prove flatness and fibrewise complete-intersection structure; (b) with \(Z=V_+(F_2,\ldots,F_{d-r})\) prove adjunction \(\omega_{X/A}=\mathcal O_X(s-d-1)\), \(s=\sum\deg F_i\), and the two restriction sequences; (c) prove local freeness of \(R^pf_*\mathcal O_X(m)\) for \(p=0,r\), vanishing for \(0<p<r\), and \(H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=A\) for \(r\ge1\); (d) construct the trace and prove \(r\)-duality by induction.

Solution

(a) In each projective fibre the expected codimension is \(c=d-r\). Its homogeneous equations form a regular sequence in the polynomial ring over the residue field: their ideal has height \(c\) and the ambient ring is Cohen–Macaulay. The fibrewise regular-sequence lifting criterion in the \(A\)-flat polynomial ring now gives a regular sequence and an \(A\)-flat quotient, locally along the resulting projective scheme. Thus \(X/A\) is flat and its fibres are complete intersections. This uses the corrected Cohen–Macaulay criterion in 3.4, not the false unrestricted exercise 3.5.

(b) Removing the first equation gives fibres of dimension \(r+1\). Indeed the remaining \(c-1\) equations have codimension at most \(c-1\), while adding one equation can raise codimension by at most one. No extra projective component can avoid the positive-degree hypersurface: a positive-dimensional projective component meets every positive-degree hypersurface. Consequently those fibres are complete intersections too. Multiplication by \(F_1\) is injective on \(\mathcal O_Z(-d_1)\), where \(d_1=\deg F_1\), and adjunction for its regular immersion gives

\[ \omega_{X/A}\simeq\omega_{Z/A}|_X\otimes\mathcal O_X(d_1). \]

Starting with \(\omega_{P/A}=\mathcal O_P(-d-1)\) proves \(\omega_{X/A}=\mathcal O_X(s-d-1)\); the printed \(s-n-1\) has \(n=d\). The exact sequences are

\[ 0\to\mathcal O_Z(m-d_1)\to\mathcal O_Z(m)\to\mathcal O_X(m)\to0, \]
\[ 0\to\omega_{Z/A}(-m)\to\omega_{Z/A}(-m+d_1)\to\omega_{X/A}(-m)\to0. \]

The second is the first restriction sequence for \(\omega_Z(-m+d_1)\) combined with adjunction.

(c) Use the graded Koszul resolution on the \(F_i\) and the usual cohomology of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^d}(j)\). On every field fibre this gives no cohomology in degrees \(0<p<r\), and \(H^0(\mathcal O)=k\) for \(r\ge1\). For \(r>0\), its degree-\(m\) global sections have dimension given by the degree-\(m\) coefficient of

\[ \frac{\prod_i(1-t^{d_i})}{(1-t)^{d+1}}. \]

The Euler characteristic is likewise determined by the Koszul resolution and the degrees, so the dimension of \(H^r(\mathcal O(m))\) is constant too. For \(r=0\), the finite flat scheme has degree \(\prod_i d_i\), and all its invertible twists have that constant \(H^0\) dimension. Cohomology and base change for the flat projective family now makes the asserted endpoint direct images locally free and gives the intermediate vanishings. The unit map \(A\to H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)\) is an isomorphism for \(r\ge1\), since it is an isomorphism on every fibre between locally free modules of rank one. This argument works over a nonreduced base as well.

(d) Induct on \(c\), starting with duality on projective space. Suppose \(Z\) already has its \((r+1)\)-dualizing sheaf and trace. The connecting map of the second sequence at \(m=0\) gives \(R^rf_*\omega_X\to R^{r+1}g_*\omega_Z\); compose with \(\operatorname{tr}_g\) to define \(\operatorname{tr}_f\).

Here is a precise way to check the diagram and the dimension-zero endpoint at once. For the regular divisor \(i:X\to Z\) the two-term resolution of \(i_*\mathcal O_X\) gives

\[ \mathcal H om_Z(i_*\mathcal O_X,\omega_Z)=0,\qquad \mathcal E xt^1_Z(i_*\mathcal O_X,\omega_Z)=i_*\omega_X, \]

with no other Ext sheaves. The first equality uses injectivity of multiplication by \(F_1\) on the invertible sheaf \(\omega_Z\). For any coherent \(\mathcal F\) on \(X\), the change-of-rings spectral sequence therefore identifies

\[ \Hom_X(\mathcal F,\omega_X)=\operatorname{Ext}^1_Z(i_*\mathcal F,\omega_Z). \]

The derived projective-space duality calculation, applied to the Koszul resolution of \(Z\), identifies this last group with \(\Hom_A(H^r(X,\mathcal F),A)\) in the top-degree situation: its other possible contribution is \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(H^{r+1}(X,\mathcal F),A)=0\), since the latter cohomology vanishes. This may also be obtained directly by the top-degree duality spectral sequence. The trace is exactly the connecting-map trace above, as seen from the two-term resolution. Hence it proves the required duality for all coherent \(\mathcal F\), including \(r=0\), without using Theorem 6.4.32.

For the diagram requested in the print, its top row is the beginning of the first long exact sequence, and its bottom row is the dual of the endpoint segment of the second. Multiplication by \(F_1\) is adjoint to multiplication by \(F_1\), and the connecting maps are adjoint by the trace definition. Thus it commutes, with compatible choices of connecting-map signs. The two left arrows are injective; for the bottom arrow this follows by dualizing the surjection in top cohomology. When \(r\ge1\), intermediate cohomology vanishes, making the right arrows surjective. When \(r=0\), the continuation of these sequences supplies the additional terms; the Ext calculation above proves precisely the endpoint identification which cannot be obtained by omitting those terms. This proof uses derived duality on projective space and its elementary regular-immersion change-of-rings calculation. Thus it avoids Theorem 6.4.32, but does use stronger homological tools than the elementary diagram requested in the exercise. ∎
