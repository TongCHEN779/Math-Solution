Exercise p.~37

Problem

Verify that the definitions of addition and multiplication of fractions \((a/s)+(b/t)=(at+bs)/st\), \((a/s)(b/t)=ab/st\) are independent of the choices of representatives \((a,s)\) and \((b,t)\), and that \(S^{-1}A\) satisfies the axioms of a commutative ring with identity.

Solution

Recall \((a,s)\equiv(b,t)\iff(at-bs)u=0\) for some \(u\in S\). Suppose \(a/s=a'/s'\) (witness \(u\): \((as'-a's)u=0\)) and \(b/t=b'/t'\) (witness \(v\)). Then

\[ \bigl[(at+bs)s't'-(a't'+b's')st\bigr]uv=\bigl[(as'-a's)tt'+(bt'-b't)ss'\bigr]uv=0, \]

so \((at+bs)/st=(a't'+b's')/s't'\). For products,

\[ ab\,s't'-a'b'\,st=(as'-a's)\,bt'+a's\,(bt'-b't), \]

and multiplying by \(uv\) gives \(0\), so \(ab/st=a'b'/s't'\).

Ring axioms. Addition and multiplication are commutative by the formulas. The element \(0/1\) is a zero, \(1/1\) an identity, and \(-(a/s)=(-a)/s\). Associativity of \(+\): both \((a/s+b/t)+c/u\) and \(a/s+(b/t+c/u)\) equal \((atu+bsu+cst)/stu\); associativity of \(\cdot\) is similar. Distributivity: \((a/s)(b/t+c/u)=a(bu+ct)/stu\) while \((a/s)(b/t)+(a/s)(c/u)=(abus+acts)/s^2tu=s\,a(bu+ct)/(s\cdot stu)\), and these agree because \(sx/sy=x/y\) for \(s\in S\) (take \(u=1\): \((sx\,y-x\,sy)\cdot1=0\)). Finally \(f:A\to S^{-1}A\) is a ring homomorphism. ∎

Exercise 1

Problem

Let \(S\) be a multiplicatively closed subset of a ring \(A\), and let \(M\) be a finitely generated \(A\)-module. Prove that \(S^{-1}M=0\) if and only if there exists \(s\in S\) such that \(sM=0\).

Solution

(\(\Leftarrow\)) \(m/t=sm/st=0\) for all \(m/t\in S^{-1}M\). (\(\Rightarrow\)) Let \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) generate \(M\). Since \(x_i/1=0\) there is \(s_i\in S\) with \(s_ix_i=0\). Then \(s=s_1\cdots s_n\in S\) kills every \(x_i\), hence \(sM=0\). ∎

Exercise 2

Problem

Let \(\fa\) be an ideal of a ring \(A\), and let \(S=1+\fa\). Show that \(S^{-1}\fa\) is contained in the Jacobson radical of \(S^{-1}A\). Use this result and Nakayama's lemma to give a proof of (2.5) which does not depend on determinants. [If \(M=\fa M\), then \(S^{-1}M=(S^{-1}\fa)(S^{-1}M)\), hence by Nakayama we have \(S^{-1}M=0\). Now use Exercise 1.]

Solution

\(S^{-1}\fa\subseteq\Jac(S^{-1}A)\). Let \(x/s\) with \(x\in\fa\), \(s\in S\), and let \(b/t\in S^{-1}A\) be arbitrary (\(b\in A\), \(t\in S\)). By 1.9 it suffices to show \(1-(x/s)(b/t)=(st-xb)/st\) is a unit. Now \(st\in1+\fa\) (product of elements of \(1+\fa\)) and \(xb\in\fa\), so \(st-xb\in1+\fa=S\). Hence \((st-xb)/st\) has numerator and denominator in \(S\), and its inverse is \(st/(st-xb)\).

Proof of 2.5 (Cor.): \(M\) finitely generated, \(\fa M=M\Rightarrow\exists x\equiv1\pmod\fa\) with \(xM=0\). Put \(S=1+\fa\). Applying \(S^{-1}\) to \(M=\fa M\) gives \(S^{-1}M=S^{-1}(\fa M)=(S^{-1}\fa)(S^{-1}M)\) (elements of \(\fa M\) are sums \(\sum a_im_i\) and \(S^{-1}\) commutes with this). \(S^{-1}M\) is a finitely generated \(S^{-1}A\)-module and \(S^{-1}\fa\subseteq\Jac(S^{-1}A)\), so Nakayama's lemma (2.6) gives \(S^{-1}M=0\). By Exercise 1 there is \(x\in S=1+\fa\) with \(xM=0\). ∎

Exercise 3

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring, let \(S\) and \(T\) be two multiplicatively closed subsets of \(A\), and let \(U\) be the image of \(T\) in \(S^{-1}A\). Show that the rings \((ST)^{-1}A\) and \(U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\) are isomorphic.

Solution

Let \(g:A\to U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\), \(g(a)=(a/1)/1\). We check the three conditions of Cor. 3.2 for \(ST=\{st\}\).

(i) \(g(st)=((s/1)(t/1))/1\). Here \(s/1\) is a unit of \(S^{-1}A\), hence of \(U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\), and \(t/1\in U\) is a unit of \(U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\). So \(g(st)\) is a unit.

(ii) If \(g(a)=0\), there is \(u=t/1\in U\) with \((t/1)(a/1)=0\) in \(S^{-1}A\), i.e. \(s\,ta=0\) for some \(s\in S\); and \(st\in ST\).

(iii) A general element of \(U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\) is \(\xi=(a/s)/(t/1)\). Then \(\xi\,g(st)=\bigl((a/s)(st/1)\bigr)/(t/1)=(at/1)/(t/1)=(a/1)/1=g(a)\) (as \((at/1)\cdot1-(a/1)(t/1)=0\)). Since \(g(st)\) is a unit, \(\xi=g(a)g(st)^{-1}\).

By Cor. 3.2 there is a unique isomorphism \((ST)^{-1}A\to U^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\) compatible with the maps from \(A\). ∎

Exercise 4

Problem

Let \(f:A\to B\) be a homomorphism of rings and let \(S\) be a multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\). Let \(T=f(S)\). Show that \(S^{-1}B\) and \(T^{-1}B\) are isomorphic as \(S^{-1}A\)-modules.

Solution

\(S^{-1}B\) means the localization of \(B\) regarded as an \(A\)-module (\(s\) acts as \(f(s)\)). Define \(\varphi:S^{-1}B\to T^{-1}B\), \(b/s\mapsto b/f(s)\). For \(b,b'\in B\), \(s,s'\in S\):

\[ b/s=b'/s'\ \text{in }S^{-1}B\iff\exists t\in S:\ t(s'b-sb')=0\iff\exists t\in S:\ f(t)\bigl(f(s')b-f(s)b'\bigr)=0\iff b/f(s)=b'/f(s')\ \text{in }T^{-1}B, \]

because every element of \(T\) has the form \(f(t)\). So \(\varphi\) is well defined and injective; it is clearly surjective, additive, and \(S^{-1}A\)-linear: \((a/s_1)\cdot(b/s)=f(a)b/f(s_1s)\mapsto f(a)b/f(s_1)f(s)\), which is the action of \(a/s_1\) on \(b/f(s)\) via the homomorphism \(S^{-1}A\to T^{-1}B\) induced by \(f\). (In fact \(\varphi\) is a ring isomorphism.) ∎

Exercise 5

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring. Suppose that, for each prime ideal \(\fp\), the local ring \(A_\fp\) has no nilpotent element \(\ne0\). Show that \(A\) has no nilpotent element \(\ne0\). If each \(A_\fp\) is an integral domain, is \(A\) necessarily an integral domain?

Solution

Let \(x\in A\) be nilpotent. For each prime \(\fp\), \(x/1\in A_\fp\) is nilpotent, hence \(0\), so \(sx=0\) for some \(s\notin\fp\), i.e. \(\Ann(x)\not\subseteq\fp\). Thus \(\Ann(x)\) lies in no prime ideal, so \(\Ann(x)=(1)\) and \(x=0\).

No, \(A\) need not be an integral domain. Let \(k\) be a field and \(A=k\times k\). Its primes are \(\fp_1=0\times k\) and \(\fp_2=k\times0\). For \(S=A-\fp_1\ni e_1=(1,0)\) we have \(e_1e_2=0\) with \(e_2=(0,1)\), so \(e_2/1=0\) in \(A_{\fp_1}\) and \(A_{\fp_1}\cong S^{-1}(A/(e_2))\cong S^{-1}k=k\) (the image of \(S\) in \(A/(e_2)\cong k\) consists of non-zero scalars), a field. Likewise \(A_{\fp_2}\cong k\). So every \(A_\fp\) is a domain, but \(A=k\times k\) has zero-divisors. ∎

Exercise 6

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring \(\ne0\) and let \(\Sigma\) be the set of all multiplicatively closed subsets \(S\) of \(A\) such that \(0\notin S\). Show that \(\Sigma\) has maximal elements, and that \(S\in\Sigma\) is maximal if and only if \(A-S\) is a minimal prime ideal of \(A\).

Solution

\(\Sigma\ni\{1\}\) is non-empty, and the union of a chain in \(\Sigma\) is multiplicatively closed and does not contain \(0\); by Zorn's lemma \(\Sigma\) has maximal elements.

First, for \(S\in\Sigma\) we have \(S^{-1}A\ne0\), so it has a maximal ideal, whose contraction is a prime \(\fq\) of \(A\) with \(\fq\cap S=\emptyset\) (3.11(iv)).

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Let \(S\) be maximal and \(\fq\) a prime with \(\fq\cap S=\emptyset\) as above. Then \(A-\fq\) is multiplicatively closed, contains \(S\) and not \(0\), so \(A-\fq\in\Sigma\) and by maximality \(A-\fq=S\), i.e. \(A-S=\fq\) is prime. If \(\fq'\subseteq\fq\) is a prime, then \(A-\fq'\in\Sigma\) contains \(S\), hence equals \(S\), so \(\fq'=\fq\). Thus \(A-S\) is a minimal prime.

(\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(\fp\) be a minimal prime and \(S=A-\fp\); \(S\in\Sigma\) since \(0\in\fp\). If \(S\subseteq S'\in\Sigma\), there is a prime \(\fq\) with \(\fq\cap S'=\emptyset\), so \(\fq\subseteq A-S'\subseteq A-S=\fp\); by minimality \(\fq=\fp\), so \(A-S'\supseteq\fp=A-S\), i.e. \(S'\subseteq S\). Hence \(S'=S\) and \(S\) is maximal. ∎

Exercise 7

Problem

A multiplicatively closed subset \(S\) of a ring \(A\) is said to be saturated if \(xy\in S\iff x\in S\) and \(y\in S\). Prove that

  1. (i)

    \(S\) is saturated \(\iff A-S\) is a union of prime ideals.

  2. (ii)

    If \(S\) is any multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\), there is a unique smallest saturated multiplicatively closed subset \(\bar S\) containing \(S\), and \(\bar S\) is the complement in \(A\) of the union of the prime ideals which do not meet \(S\). (\(\bar S\) is called the saturation of \(S\).)

If \(S=1+\fa\), where \(\fa\) is an ideal of \(A\), find \(\bar S\).

Solution

(i) (\(\Leftarrow\)) If \(A-S=\bigcup\fp_i\), then \(S=\bigcap(A-\fp_i)\) and each \(A-\fp_i\) is multiplicatively closed and saturated (\(xy\notin\fp_i\iff x,y\notin\fp_i\), as \(\fp_i\) is prime). An intersection of saturated multiplicatively closed sets is such a set.

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Let \(S\) be saturated and \(x\notin S\). Then \((x)\cap S=\emptyset\): if \(ax\in S\) then \(x\in S\). By 3.11(ii) \(S^{-1}(x)\ne(1)\), so it lies in a maximal ideal of \(S^{-1}A\) whose contraction \(\fp\) is a prime with \(x\in\fp\), \(\fp\cap S=\emptyset\) (3.11(iv)). Hence \(A-S\subseteq\bigcup\{\fp\text{ prime}:\fp\cap S=\emptyset\}\subseteq A-S\), so \(A-S\) is a union of primes.

(ii) Put \(\bar S=A-\bigcup\{\fp\text{ prime}:\fp\cap S=\emptyset\}\). By (i) it is saturated; clearly \(S\subseteq\bar S\). If \(T\supseteq S\) is saturated, then \(A-T=\bigcup\fq\) with \(\fq\cap T=\emptyset\) (by the proof of (i)), so \(\fq\cap S=\emptyset\) and \(\fq\subseteq A-\bar S\). Hence \(A-T\subseteq A-\bar S\), i.e. \(\bar S\subseteq T\). So \(\bar S\) is the unique smallest saturated set containing \(S\).

The case \(S=1+\fa\): \(\bar S=\{x\in A:\ x+\fa\text{ is a unit of }A/\fa\}\). Indeed, let \(S'\) be this set; it is saturated (a product is a unit mod \(\fa\) iff each factor is) and contains \(1+\fa\), so \(\bar S\subseteq S'\). Conversely if \(x\in S'\) with \(xy\in1+\fa\) and \(x\in\fp\) for a prime \(\fp\) disjoint from \(1+\fa\), then \(xy\in\fp\cap(1+\fa)\), a contradiction; so \(x\in\bar S\). (For \(A=\Z\), \(\fa=(6)\): \(\bar S=\{x:\gcd(x,6)=1\}\).) ∎

Exercise 8

Problem

Let \(S,T\) be multiplicatively closed subsets of \(A\), such that \(S\subseteq T\). Let \(\phi:S^{-1}A\to T^{-1}A\) be the homomorphism which maps each \(a/s\in S^{-1}A\) to \(a/s\) considered as an element of \(T^{-1}A\). Show that the following statements are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    \(\phi\) is bijective.

  2. (ii)

    For each \(t\in T\), \(t/1\) is a unit in \(S^{-1}A\).

  3. (iii)

    For each \(t\in T\) there exists \(x\in A\) such that \(xt\in S\).

  4. (iv)

    \(T\) is contained in the saturation of \(S\) (Exercise 7).

  5. (v)

    Every prime ideal which meets \(T\) also meets \(S\).

Solution

(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii): \(t/1\) is a unit of \(T^{-1}A\) and \(\phi(t/1)=t/1\); \(\phi^{-1}\) is a ring homomorphism, so \(t/1\) is a unit of \(S^{-1}A\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i): By 3.1 applied to \(A\to S^{-1}A\) (which sends \(T\) to units) there is a homomorphism \(\psi:T^{-1}A\to S^{-1}A\), \(\psi(a/t)=(a/1)(t/1)^{-1}\). Then \(\psi\phi(a/s)=(a/1)(s/1)^{-1}=a/s\) and \(\phi\psi(a/t)=a/t\) in \(T^{-1}A\). So \(\phi\) is bijective.

(ii)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(iii): \(t/1\) is a unit in \(S^{-1}A\) iff \((t/1)(x/s)=1\) for some \(x\in A\), \(s\in S\), iff \(u(xt-s)=0\) for some \(u\in S\). Then \((ux)t=us\in S\). Conversely if \(x't=s\in S\) then \((t/1)(x'/s)=1\).

(iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iv): if \(xt\in S\subseteq\bar S\) and \(\bar S\) is saturated, then \(t\in\bar S\).

(iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii): Let \(t\in T\subseteq\bar S\). If \((t)\cap S=\emptyset\), then as in Exercise 7 there is a prime \(\fp\ni t\) with \(\fp\cap S=\emptyset\), so \(t\notin\bar S\), a contradiction. So \(xt\in S\) for some \(x\).

(iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(v): if a prime \(\fp\) contains \(t\in T\subseteq\bar S\), then by definition of \(\bar S\), \(\fp\) meets \(S\).

(v)\(\Rightarrow\)(iv): if \(t\in T\) were not in \(\bar S\), then \(t\in\fp\) for a prime \(\fp\) disjoint from \(S\); \(\fp\) meets \(T\), contradicting (v). ∎

Exercise 9

Problem

The set \(S_0\) of all non-zero-divisors in \(A\) is a saturated multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\). Hence the set \(D\) of zero-divisors in \(A\) is a union of prime ideals (see Chapter 1, Exercise 14). Show that every minimal prime ideal of \(A\) is contained in \(D\). [Use Exercise 6.]

The ring \(S_0^{-1}A\) is called the total ring of fractions of \(A\). Prove that

  1. (i)

    \(S_0\) is the largest multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\) for which the homomorphism \(A\to S^{-1}A\) is injective.

  2. (ii)

    Every element in \(S_0^{-1}A\) is either a zero-divisor or a unit.

  3. (iii)

    Every ring in which every non-unit is a zero-divisor is equal to its total ring of fractions (i.e., \(A\to S_0^{-1}A\) is bijective).

Solution

Minimal primes lie in \(D\). Let \(\fp\) be a minimal prime and \(x\in\fp\). By Exercise 6, \(A-\fp\) is a maximal element of \(\Sigma\); equivalently \(A_\fp\) has exactly one prime ideal (3.13), so \(\fp A_\fp\) is the nilradical of \(A_\fp\) and \(x/1\) is nilpotent: \(s x^n=0\) for some \(s\notin\fp\) and \(n\ge1\) (note \(s\ne0\) as \(0\in\fp\)). Take \(n\) minimal with \(sx^n=0\); then \(y=sx^{n-1}\ne0\) (\(n=1\): \(y=s\ne0\)) and \(xy=0\). So \(x\in D\).

(i) \(f:A\to S^{-1}A\) is injective iff (\(sa=0\) with \(s\in S\Rightarrow a=0\)), i.e. iff no \(s\in S\) is a zero-divisor, i.e. iff \(S\subseteq S_0\).

(ii) Let \(a/s\in S_0^{-1}A\). If \(a\in S_0\), \(a/s\) is a unit with inverse \(s/a\). If \(a\notin S_0\), then \(a\) is a zero-divisor: \(ab=0\) with \(b\ne0\). By (i) \(b/1\ne0\), and \((a/s)(b/1)=0\), so \(a/s\) is a zero-divisor.

(iii) If every non-unit is a zero-divisor then \(S_0\subseteq A^*\) (units). Then \(a/s=(as^{-1})/1\), so \(f\) is surjective; it is injective by (i). ∎

Exercise 10

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring.

  1. (i)

    If \(A\) is absolutely flat (Chapter 2, Exercise 27) and \(S\) is any multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\), then \(S^{-1}A\) is absolutely flat.

  2. (ii)

    \(A\) is absolutely flat \(\iff A_\fm\) is a field for each maximal ideal \(\fm\).

Solution

(i) By Ch. 2, Ex. 27, \(A\) is absolutely flat iff \(x=ax^2\) is solvable for each \(x\). Given \(\xi=x/s\in S^{-1}A\) pick \(a\) with \(x=ax^2\). Then \(\xi^2\cdot(as/1)=ax^2s/s^2=x/s=\xi\), so \(\xi\in(\xi^2)\), i.e. \((\xi)=(\xi^2)\). By Ch. 2, Ex. 27 again, \(S^{-1}A\) is absolutely flat.

(ii) (\(\Rightarrow\)) \(A_\fm\) is absolutely flat by (i) and local, hence a field by Ch. 2, Ex. 28. (\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(x\in A\) and \(M=(x)/(x^2)\). For each maximal \(\fm\), \(M_\fm=(x/1)A_\fm/(x^2/1)A_\fm\) (3.3, 3.4) and in the field \(A_\fm\) the ideals \((x/1)\) and \((x/1)^2=(x^2/1)\) coincide (both are \(0\) or the whole field). So \(M_\fm=0\) for all \(\fm\), hence \(M=0\) by 3.8, i.e. \((x)=(x^2)\). By Ch. 2, Ex. 27, \(A\) is absolutely flat. ∎

Exercise 11

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring. Prove that the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    \(A/\Nil\) is absolutely flat (\(\Nil\) being the nilradical of \(A\)).

  2. (ii)

    Every prime ideal of \(A\) is maximal.

  3. (iii)

    \(\Spec(A)\) is a \(T_1\)-space (i.e., every subset consisting of a single point is closed).

  4. (iv)

    \(\Spec(A)\) is Hausdorff.

If these conditions are satisfied, show that \(\Spec(A)\) is compact and totally disconnected (i.e. the only connected subsets of \(\Spec(A)\) are those consisting of a single point).

Solution

Put \(\bar A=A/\Nil\). Primes of \(A\) correspond to primes of \(\bar A\) (all primes contain \(\Nil\)), maximal to maximal, and \(\Spec A\cong\Spec\bar A\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 21(iv)).

(ii)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(iii): by Ch. 1, Ex. 18(i), \(\{x\}\) is closed iff \(\fp_x\) is maximal.

(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii): Let \(B=\bar A\) be absolutely flat, \(\fq\) a prime of \(B\), \(\fm\supseteq\fq\) a maximal ideal. By Exercise 10(ii), \(B_\fm\) is a field, so it has exactly one prime ideal; by 3.13 the primes of \(B\) contained in \(\fm\) correspond to the primes of \(B_\fm\), so \(\fm\) is the only prime contained in \(\fm\). Hence \(\fq=\fm\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i): Let all primes of \(\bar A\) be maximal. For a maximal \(\fm\) of \(\bar A\), the local ring \(\bar A_\fm\) has a unique prime (3.13, since every prime inside \(\fm\) is \(\fm\)), so \(\bar A_\fm/\Nil(\bar A_\fm)\) is a field (Ch. 1, Ex. 10). By 3.12, \(\Nil(\bar A_\fm)=(\Nil\bar A)_\fm=0\). So \(\bar A_\fm\) is a field for all \(\fm\) and \(\bar A\) is absolutely flat by Exercise 10(ii).

(iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii): Hausdorff implies \(T_1\).

(i)–(iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iv): Replace \(A\) by the absolutely flat \(\bar A\) (same spectrum). Let \(\fp\ne\fq\) be primes; both are maximal, so there is \(x\in\fp-\fq\). By Ch. 2, Ex. 27, \((x)=(e)\) with \(e\) idempotent. Then \(e\in\fp\) and \(e\notin\fq\) (else \(x\in(e)\subseteq\fq\)). From \(e(1-e)=0\) we get \(1-e\in\fq\) and \(1-e\notin\fp\). Thus \(\fq\in X_e\), \(\fp\in X_{1-e}\) and \(X_e\cap X_{1-e}=X_{e(1-e)}=X_0=\emptyset\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 17). So \(\Spec A\) is Hausdorff.

Compact and totally disconnected. \(\Spec A\) is quasi-compact (Ch. 1, Ex. 17(v)) and Hausdorff, hence compact. For \(\fp\ne\fq\) and \(e\) as above, \(X=X_e\sqcup X_{1-e}\): any prime contains exactly one of \(e,1-e\) (their product is \(0\), their sum \(1\)). So \(X_e,X_{1-e}\) are disjoint open sets covering \(X\), with \(\fq\in X_e\), \(\fp\in X_{1-e}\). A connected subset cannot contain both \(\fp\) and \(\fq\), so connected subsets are single points. ∎

Exercise 12

Problem

Let \(A\) be an integral domain and \(M\) an \(A\)-module. An element \(x\in M\) is a torsion element of \(M\) if \(\Ann(x)\ne0\), that is if \(x\) is killed by some non-zero element of \(A\). Show that the torsion elements of \(M\) form a submodule of \(M\). This submodule is called the torsion submodule of \(M\) and is denoted by \(T(M)\). If \(T(M)=0\), the module \(M\) is said to be torsion-free. Show that

  1. (i)

    If \(M\) is any \(A\)-module, then \(M/T(M)\) is torsion-free.

  2. (ii)

    If \(f:M\to N\) is a module homomorphism, then \(f(T(M))\subseteq T(N)\).

  3. (iii)

    If \(0\to M'\to M\to M''\) is an exact sequence, then the sequence \(0\to T(M')\to T(M)\to T(M'')\) is exact.

  4. (iv)

    If \(M\) is any \(A\)-module, then \(T(M)\) is the kernel of the mapping \(x\mapsto1\otimes x\) of \(M\) into \(K\otimes_AM\), where \(K\) is the field of fractions of \(A\).

[For (iv), show that \(K\) may be regarded as the direct limit of its submodules \(A\xi\) \((\xi\in K)\); using Chapter 1, Exercise 15 and Exercise 20, show that if \(1\otimes x=0\) in \(K\otimes M\) then \(1\otimes x=0\) in \(A\xi\otimes M\) for some \(\xi\ne0\). Deduce that \(\xi^{-1}x=0\).]

Solution

\(T(M)\) is a submodule. \(0\in T(M)\) since \(\Ann(0)=A\ne0\). If \(ax=0\), \(by=0\) with \(a,b\ne0\), then \(ab\ne0\) (\(A\) a domain) and \(ab(x+y)=0\); and \(a(cx)=0\) for \(c\in A\).

(i) Suppose \(a\bar x=0\) in \(M/T(M)\) with \(a\ne0\). Then \(ax\in T(M)\), so \(bax=0\) for some \(b\ne0\); \(ba\ne0\), so \(x\in T(M)\), \(\bar x=0\).

(ii) If \(ax=0\) with \(a\ne0\) then \(af(x)=0\).

(iii) Let \(u:M'\to M\), \(v:M\to M''\) be the maps; by (ii) they restrict to \(T(\cdot)\). The restriction of \(u\) is injective. \(vu=0\) gives \(v(u(T(M')))=0\). If \(x\in T(M)\) with \(v(x)=0\), then \(x=u(x')\) for some \(x'\in M'\); if \(ax=0\) (\(a\ne0\)) then \(u(ax')=0\) so \(ax'=0\) (\(u\) injective), i.e. \(x'\in T(M')\).

(iv) Let \(S=A-\{0\}\). Then \(K=S^{-1}A\) and \(K\otimes_AM\cong S^{-1}M\) by 3.5, with \(1\otimes x\mapsto x/1\). Now \(x/1=0\) in \(S^{-1}M\) iff \(sx=0\) for some \(s\in S\) iff \(x\in T(M)\). ∎

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring, and let \(F\) be the \(A\)-module \(A^n\). Show that every set of \(n\) generators of \(F\) is a basis of \(F\). Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring, \(M\) an \(A\)-module. The support of \(M\) is defined to be the set \(\Supp(M)\) of prime ideals \(\fp\) of \(A\) such that \(M_\fp\ne0\). Prove the following results:

    \(M\ne0\iff\Supp(M)\ne\emptyset\).

  2. (ii)

    \(V(\fa)=\Supp(A/\fa)\).

    If \(0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0\) is an exact sequence, then \(\Supp(M)=\Supp(M')\cup\Supp(M'')\).

    If \(M,N\) are finitely generated, then \(\Supp(M\otimes_AN)=\Supp(M)\cap\Supp(N)\). [Use Chapter 2, Exercise 3.]

    If \(M\) is finitely generated and \(\fa\) is an ideal of \(A\), then \(\Supp(M/\fa M)=V(\fa+\Ann(M))\).

    Let \(x\) \((=\fp)\) be a point of \(X\). Show that \(\varinjlim_{U\ni x}A(U)\cong A_\fp\).

The assignment of the ring \(A(U)\) to each basic open set \(U\) of \(X\), and the restriction homomorphisms \(\rho\), satisfying the conditions (iii) and (iv) above, constitutes a presheaf of rings on the basis of open sets \((X_f)_{f\in A}\). (v) says that the stalk of this presheaf at \(x\in X\) is the corresponding local ring \(A_\fp\).

Solution

Key fact. For \(f,g\in A\): \(X_g\subseteq X_f\iff V(f)\subseteq V(g)\iff g\in\bigcap_{\fp\ni f}\fp=\rad((f))\iff g^n\in(f)\) for some \(n>0\) (1.14).

(i) Let \(X_f=X_g\). Then \(g^n=uf\) and \(f^m=vg\) for some \(n,m,u,v\). In \(A_f\) the element \(f\) is a unit, and \(f^m=vg\) gives \(g\cdot(vf^{-m})=1\), so \(g\) is a unit of \(A_f\); symmetrically \(f\) is a unit of \(A_g\). By 3.1 the canonical maps \(A\to A_f,A_g\) induce \(A\)-algebra homomorphisms \(A_f\to A_g\to A_f\) whose composite is the identity (uniqueness in 3.1). So \(A_f\cong A_g\) by a unique \(A\)-algebra isomorphism, i.e. \(A(U)\) is determined by \(U\) up to unique canonical isomorphism.

(ii) \(U'\subseteq U\) gives \(g^n=uf\) by the key fact. Then \(f\cdot u=g^n\) is a unit in \(A_g\), so \(f\) is a unit of \(A_g\) and by 3.1 there is a unique \(A\)-algebra homomorphism \(\rho:A_f\to A_g\), with \(\rho(a/f^m)=(a/1)(f/1)^{-m}\). In \(A_g\), \(f^{-1}=u\,g^{-n}\), so \(\rho(a/f^m)=au^m/g^{mn}\), as stated. Because \(\rho\) is characterized as the unique \(A\)-algebra homomorphism \(A_f\to A_g\), it does not depend on the choices of \(n,u\) (nor, by (i), on \(f,g\) beyond \(U,U'\)).

(iii) If \(U=U'\) the identity is an \(A\)-algebra homomorphism \(A(U)\to A(U)\), so \(\rho=\mathrm{id}\) by uniqueness.

(iv) \(\rho_{U'U''}\circ\rho_{UU'}\) and \(\rho_{UU''}\) are both \(A\)-algebra homomorphisms \(A(U)\to A(U'')\); by uniqueness (3.1) they are equal.

(v) The neighbourhoods \(U=X_f\ni\fp\) (i.e. \(f\notin\fp\)) form a directed system (\(X_f\cap X_{f'}=X_{ff'}\)), with the maps \(\rho\). For \(f\notin\fp\) the canonical \(A_f\to A_\fp\) exists by 3.1 and is compatible with the \(\rho\)'s (all are \(A\)-algebra homomorphisms), so by Ch. 2, Ex. 16 there is \(\lambda:\varinjlim A(U)\to A_\fp\). It is surjective: \(a/s\in A_\fp\) (\(s\notin\fp\)) is the image of \(a/s\in A_s\). It is injective: an element of the limit is the class of some \(\xi=a/f^m\in A_f\) (Ch. 2, Ex. 15), and \(\lambda(\xi)=0\) means \(a/f^m=0\) in \(A_\fp\), so \(sa=0\) for some \(s\notin\fp\). Then in \(A_{fs}=A(X_f\cap X_s)\), with \(X_{fs}\ni\fp\), the image of \(\xi\) is \(a/f^m=0\) (\(s\) is a unit there and \(sa=0\)). So \(\xi\) is \(0\) in the limit. Hence \(\varinjlim_{U\ni x}A(U)\cong A_\fp\). ∎

Exercise 24

Problem

Show that the presheaf of Exercise 23 has the following property. Let \((U_i)_{i\in I}\) be a covering of \(X\) by basic open sets. For each \(i\in I\) let \(s_i\in A(U_i)\) be such that, for each pair of indices \(i,j\), the images of \(s_i\) and \(s_j\) in \(A(U_i\cap U_j)\) are equal. Then there exists a unique \(s\in A\) (\(=A(X)\)) whose image in \(A(U_i)\) is \(s_i\), for all \(i\in I\). (This essentially implies that the presheaf is a sheaf.)

Solution

Write \(U_i=X_{f_i}\), so \(s_i\in A_{f_i}\).

Finiteness. \(\bigcup X_{f_i}=X\) means no prime contains all \(f_i\), so the \(f_i\) generate \((1)\): \(1=\sum_{i\in J}g_if_i\) with \(J\subseteq I\) finite (Ch. 1, Ex. 17(v)). Raising to powers, for every \(N\) the elements \(f_i^N\) (\(i\in J\)) also generate \((1)\) (otherwise a maximal ideal would contain all \(f_i\)).

Normalizing. For \(i\in J\) write \(s_i=b_i/f_i^{n}\) with a common \(n\). The hypothesis says \(b_i/f_i^n=b_j/f_j^n\) in \(A_{f_if_j}\), so \((f_if_j)^m(b_if_j^n-b_jf_i^n)=0\) in \(A\) for some \(m\) (taking the maximum over the finitely many pairs). Put \(h_i=f_i^{n+m}\) and \(b_i'=b_if_i^m\); then \(s_i=b_i'/h_i\) in \(A_{f_i}\) and \(b'_ih_j-b'_jh_i=(f_if_j)^m(b_if_j^n-b_jf_i^n)=0\) for \(i,j\in J\). Choose \(g_i'\) with \(\sum_{i\in J}g'_ih_i=1\).

Existence. Let \(s=\sum_{j\in J}g'_jb'_j\in A\). For \(i\in J\):

\[ s\,h_i=\sum_jg'_jb'_jh_i=\sum_jg'_jb'_ih_j=b'_i\sum_jg'_jh_j=b'_i, \]

so the image of \(s\) in \(A_{f_i}\) is \(b'_i/h_i=s_i\). Now let \(i\in I\) be arbitrary and put \(t=s_i-s\in A_{f_i}\). For \(j\in J\) the image of \(t\) in \(A_{f_if_j}\) is \((s_i)|-(s)|=(s_j)|-(s)|=0\) by the hypothesis and the previous step. Since \(A_{f_if_j}=(A_{f_i})_{f_j}\), there is \(k\) with \(f_j^kt=0\) in \(A_{f_i}\) (for all \(j\in J\), with a common \(k\)). As the \(f_j^k\) (\(j\in J\)) generate \((1)\), \(t=0\). So \(s\mapsto s_i\) for every \(i\in I\).

Uniqueness. If \(s,s'\in A\) both map to \(s_i\) for all \(i\), then \(t=s-s'\) maps to \(0\) in each \(A_{f_i}\), so for \(i\in J\) there is \(k\) with \(f_i^kt=0\) in \(A\); the \(f_i^k\) generate \((1)\), so \(t=0\). ∎

Exercise 25

Problem

Let \(f:A\to B\), \(g:A\to C\) be ring homomorphisms and let \(h:A\to B\otimes_AC\) be defined by \(h(x)=f(x)\otimes g(x)\). Let \(X,Y,Z,T\) be the prime spectra of \(A,B,C,B\otimes_AC\) respectively. Then \(h^*(T)=f^*(Y)\cap g^*(Z)\). [Let \(\fp\in X\), and let \(k=k(\fp)\) be the residue field at \(\fp\). By Exercise 21, the fiber \(h^{*-1}(\fp)\) is the spectrum of \((B\otimes_AC)\otimes_Ak\cong(B\otimes_Ak)\otimes_k(C\otimes_Ak)\). Hence \(\fp\in h^*(T)\iff(B\otimes_Ak)\otimes_k(C\otimes_Ak)\ne0\iff B\otimes_Ak\ne0\) and \(C\otimes_Ak\ne0\iff\fp\in f^*(Y)\cap g^*(Z)\).]

Solution

Remark on the formula for \(h\). In \(B\otimes_AC\) one has \(f(x)\otimes1=1\otimes g(x)=x\cdot(1\otimes1)\), so \(f(x)\otimes g(x)=x^2(1\otimes1)\), which is not additive in general (e.g. \((x+y)^2\ne x^2+y^2\) in \(A=\Z\) with \(B=C=\Z\)). The same formula is printed in the text on p. 31. The intended homomorphism is \(h(x)=f(x)\otimes1=1\otimes g(x)\) (this is the structure map of the \(A\)-algebra \(B\otimes_AC\)), and that is what I use.

Let \(\fp\in X\), \(k=k(\fp)\). By Ex. 21(iv) the fibres are

\[ h^{*-1}(\fp)=\Spec\bigl((B\otimes_AC)\otimes_Ak\bigr),\quad f^{*-1}(\fp)=\Spec(B\otimes_Ak),\quad g^{*-1}(\fp)=\Spec(C\otimes_Ak). \]

A spectrum is non-empty iff the ring is non-zero. By the canonical isomorphisms of Ch. 2,

\[ (B\otimes_AC)\otimes_Ak\cong B\otimes_Ak\otimes_AC\cong(B\otimes_Ak)\otimes_k(C\otimes_Ak), \]

a tensor product of two \(k\)-algebras, i.e. of two \(k\)-vector spaces, over the field \(k\); it is non-zero iff both factors are non-zero (\(\dim(V\otimes_kW)=\dim V\dim W\)). Hence \(\fp\in h^*(T)\iff B\otimes_Ak\ne0\) and \(C\otimes_Ak\ne0\iff\fp\in f^*(Y)\cap g^*(Z)\). ∎

Exercise 26

Problem

Let \((B_\alpha,g_{\alpha\beta})\) be a direct system of rings and \(B\) the direct limit. For each \(\alpha\), let \(f_\alpha:A\to B_\alpha\) be a ring homomorphism such that \(g_{\alpha\beta}\circ f_\alpha=f_\beta\) whenever \(\alpha\le\beta\) (i.e. the \(B_\alpha\) form a direct system of \(A\)-algebras). The \(f_\alpha\) induce \(f:A\to B\). Show that

\[ f^*(\Spec(B))=\bigcap_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\Spec(B_\alpha)). \]

[Let \(\fp\in\Spec(A)\). Then \(f^{*-1}(\fp)\) is the spectrum of \(B\otimes_Ak(\fp)\cong\varinjlim(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak(\fp))\) (since tensor products commute with direct limits: Chapter 2, Exercise 20). By Exercise 21 of Chapter 2 it follows that \(f^{*-1}(\fp)=\emptyset\) if and only if \(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak(\fp)=0\) for some \(\alpha\), i.e. if and only if \(f_\alpha^{*-1}(\fp)=\emptyset\).]

Solution

Let \(g_\alpha:B_\alpha\to B\) be the canonical maps, so \(f=g_\alpha f_\alpha\) and \(f^*=f_\alpha^*g_\alpha^*\); hence \(f^*(\Spec B)\subseteq f^*_\alpha(\Spec B_\alpha)\) for all \(\alpha\), giving \(\subseteq\).

For \(\supseteq\) let \(\fp\in\bigcap_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\Spec B_\alpha)\), \(k=k(\fp)\). By Ex. 21(iv), \(f_\alpha^{*-1}(\fp)=\Spec(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak)\ne\emptyset\), i.e. \(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak\ne0\) for every \(\alpha\). The ring homomorphisms \(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak\to B\otimes_Ak\) induce a ring homomorphism \(\varinjlim(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak)\to B\otimes_Ak\), bijective by Ch. 2, Ex. 20; so it is a ring isomorphism. If \(B\otimes_Ak\) were \(0\), then \(\varinjlim(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak)=0\) and by Ch. 2, Ex. 21 some \(B_\alpha\otimes_Ak=0\), a contradiction. So \(B\otimes_Ak\ne0\), i.e. \(f^{*-1}(\fp)\ne\emptyset\) and \(\fp\in f^*(\Spec B)\). ∎

Exercise 27

Problem
  1. (i)

    Let \(f_\alpha:A\to B_\alpha\) be any family of \(A\)-algebras and let \(f:A\to B\) be their tensor product over \(A\) (Chapter 2, Exercise 23). Then \(f^*(\Spec(B))=\bigcap_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\Spec(B_\alpha))\). [Use Examples 25 and 26.]

  2. (ii)

    Let \(f_\alpha:A\to B_\alpha\) be any finite family of \(A\)-algebras and let \(B=\prod_\alpha B_\alpha\). Define \(f:A\to B\) by \(f(x)=(f_\alpha(x))\). Then \(f^*(\Spec(B))=\bigcup_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\Spec(B_\alpha))\).

  3. (iii)

    Hence the subsets of \(X=\Spec(A)\) of the form \(f^*(\Spec(B))\), where \(f:A\to B\) is a ring homomorphism, satisfy the axioms for closed sets in a topological space. The associated topology is the constructible topology on \(X\). It is finer than the Zariski topology (i.e., there are more open sets, or equivalently more closed sets).

  4. (iv)

    Let \(X_C\) denote the set \(X\) endowed with the constructible topology. Show that \(X_C\) is quasi-compact.

Solution

(i) \(B=\varinjlim_JB_J\), where \(J\) runs over the finite subsets of the index set and \(B_J=\bigotimes_{\alpha\in J}B_\alpha\) (Ch. 2, Ex. 23). Let \(f_J:A\to B_J\). By Ex. 26, \(f^*(\Spec B)=\bigcap_Jf_J^*(\Spec B_J)\). By Ex. 25 and induction on \(|J|\) (with \(B_{J\cup\{\alpha\}}=B_J\otimes_AB_\alpha\)), \(f_J^*(\Spec B_J)=\bigcap_{\alpha\in J}f_\alpha^*(\Spec B_\alpha)\) (for \(J=\emptyset\), \(B_\emptyset=A\) and the image is \(X\)). Since every \(\alpha\) lies in some \(J\), \(\bigcap_J\bigcap_{\alpha\in J}=\bigcap_\alpha\).

(ii) \(\Spec(\prod B_\alpha)=\bigsqcup\Spec B_\alpha\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 22): the primes of \(B\) are \(\pi_\alpha^{-1}(\fq)\) for \(\fq\in\Spec B_\alpha\), \(\pi_\alpha:B\to B_\alpha\) the projection. And \(f^{-1}(\pi_\alpha^{-1}\fq)=f_\alpha^{-1}(\fq)\) since \(\pi_\alpha f=f_\alpha\). So \(f^*(\Spec B)=\bigcup_\alpha f_\alpha^*(\Spec B_\alpha)\).

(iii) \(\emptyset=f^*(\Spec0)\) (for \(A\to0\)) and \(X=f^*(\Spec A)\) (identity). Arbitrary intersections are of the required form by (i), finite unions by (ii). So these sets are the closed sets of a topology. Every Zariski closed set \(V(\fa)=f^*(\Spec(A/\fa))\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 21(iv)) is constructible-closed; so the constructible topology is finer.

(iv) The closed sets of \(X_C\) are exactly the sets \(f^*(\Spec B)\), so quasi-compactness means: any family \(E_\alpha=f_\alpha^*(\Spec B_\alpha)\) of closed sets with the finite intersection property has non-empty intersection. By (i), \(\bigcap E_\alpha=f^*(\Spec B)\) with \(B=\varinjlim B_J\). If \(\bigcap E_\alpha=\emptyset\), then \(B=0\), so by Ch. 2, Ex. 21 some \(B_J=0\) and then \(\bigcap_{\alpha\in J}E_\alpha=f_J^*(\Spec B_J)=\emptyset\), contradicting the finite intersection property. ∎

Exercise 28

Problem

(Continuation of Exercise 27.)

  1. (i)

    For each \(g\in A\), the set \(X_g\) (Chapter 1, Exercise 17) is both open and closed in the constructible topology.

  2. (ii)

    Let \(C'\) denote the smallest topology on \(X\) for which the sets \(X_g\) are both open and closed, and let \(X_{C'}\) denote the set \(X\) endowed with this topology. Show that \(X_{C'}\) is Hausdorff.

  3. (iii)

    Deduce that the identity mapping \(X_C\to X_{C'}\) is a homeomorphism. Hence a subset \(E\) of \(X\) is of the form \(f^*(\Spec(B))\) for some \(f:A\to B\) if and only if it is closed in the topology \(C'\).

  4. (iv)

    The topological space \(X_C\) is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected.

Solution

(i) \(X_g\) is Zariski open, hence open in the finer constructible topology. It is also closed there: \(X_g=f^*(\Spec A_g)\) for \(f:A\to A_g\) (Ex. 21(i)).

(ii) Let \(\fp\ne\fq\) be primes. Then there is \(g\) in one but not the other, say \(g\in\fp-\fq\) (or \(\fq-\fp\)). Then \(\fq\in X_g\) and \(\fp\in X\setminus X_g\); both \(X_g\) and its complement are open in \(C'\) (as \(X_g\) is clopen in \(C'\)) and they are disjoint. So \(X_{C'}\) is Hausdorff.

(iii) By (i), \(C'\subseteq C\) (every \(C'\)-open set is \(C\)-open), so the identity \(X_C\to X_{C'}\) is a continuous bijection. \(X_C\) is quasi-compact (Ex. 27(iv)) and \(X_{C'}\) is Hausdorff by (ii); a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is closed, hence a homeomorphism. So \(C=C'\), and the closed sets of \(C'\) are exactly the sets \(f^*(\Spec B)\).

(iv) \(X_C=X_{C'}\) is quasi-compact (Ex. 27(iv)) and Hausdorff (ii), i.e. compact Hausdorff. For \(\fp\ne\fq\) choose \(g\) as in (ii): \(X_g\) is clopen in \(C\) and contains exactly one of the two points. So no connected subset contains two points: \(X_C\) is totally disconnected. ∎

Exercise 29

Problem

Let \(f:A\to B\) be a ring homomorphism. Show that \(f^*:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) is a continuous closed mapping (i.e., maps closed sets to closed sets) for the constructible topology.

Solution

Closed. A closed set of \(Y_C=\Spec(B)_C\) is \(F=h^*(\Spec D')\) with \(h:B\to D'\). Then \(f^*(F)=f^*h^*(\Spec D')=(h\circ f)^*(\Spec D')\) is closed in \(X_C\).

Continuous. Let \(E=h^*(\Spec D)\) be closed in \(X_C\), \(h:A\to D\). Put \(h':B\to B\otimes_AD\), \(y\mapsto y\otimes1\). We claim \(f^{*-1}(E)=h'^*(\Spec(B\otimes_AD))\), which is closed in \(Y_C\). For \(\fq\in\Spec B\), \(\fp=f^*(\fq)\):

\[ \fq\in h'^*(\Spec(B\otimes_AD))\iff k(\fq)\otimes_B(B\otimes_AD)\ne0\iff k(\fq)\otimes_AD\ne0 \]

(Ex. 21(iv) for \(h'\)), and \(k(\fq)\otimes_AD\cong k(\fq)\otimes_{k(\fp)}\bigl(k(\fp)\otimes_AD\bigr)\) because \(k(\fq)\) is a field extension of \(k(\fp)\); this is \(\ne0\) iff \(k(\fp)\otimes_AD\ne0\) (a field extension is faithfully flat: \(V\ne0\Rightarrow k(\fq)\otimes_{k(\fp)}V\ne0\)), i.e. iff \(\fp\in h^*(\Spec D)=E\) (Ex. 21(iv) for \(h\)). ∎

Exercise 30

Problem

Show that the Zariski topology and the constructible topology on \(\Spec(A)\) are the same if and only if \(A/\Nil\) is absolutely flat (where \(\Nil\) is the nilradical of \(A\)). [Use Exercise 11.]

Solution

(\(\Leftarrow\)) If \(A/\Nil\) is absolutely flat, \(\Spec A\) with the Zariski topology is Hausdorff (Ex. 11). The identity \(X_C\to X_Z\) is a continuous bijection (the constructible topology is finer, Ex. 27(iii)) from a compact space \(X_C\) (Ex. 27(iv)) to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism. So the two topologies agree.

(\(\Rightarrow\)) If they agree, the Zariski topology equals \(X_C\), which is Hausdorff by Ex. 28. Then \(\Spec A\) is Hausdorff, so \(A/\Nil\) is absolutely flat by Ex. 11. ∎
