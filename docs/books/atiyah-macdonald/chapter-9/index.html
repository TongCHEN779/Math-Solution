Conventions. \(K\) denotes the field of fractions of the domain \(A\). A Dedekind domain is a Noetherian integrally closed domain of dimension one (Thm. 9.3); in particular it is not a field, every non-zero prime is maximal, and \(A_\fp\) is a discrete valuation ring (DVR) for every non-zero prime \(\fp\). From the text I use: 9.2 (a Noetherian local domain of dimension one with principal maximal ideal is a DVR; in a DVR with maximal ideal \((\pi)\) the non-zero ideals are exactly \((\pi^k)\), \(k\ge0\)); 9.3, 9.4 (unique factorization of non-zero ideals into primes); 9.6–9.9 (a non-zero fractional ideal of a Dedekind domain is invertible; the non-zero fractional ideals form a group \(I\), free on the non-zero primes; \(H=I/P\) is the ideal class group); the localization facts of Ch. 3 (3.3, 3.4, 3.8, 3.9, 3.10, 3.11); integral closure commutes with localization (5.12); the Chinese remainder theorem (1.10, 1.16); and the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain, including uniqueness of the elementary divisors.

A recurring principle (3.8/3.9): if \(X\subseteq Y\) are ideals (or modules) with \(X_\fm=Y_\fm\) for every maximal ideal \(\fm\), then \(X=Y\) (apply 3.8 to \(Y/X\), using exactness of localization). In a Dedekind domain the local rings \(A_\fm\) at maximal ideals are DVRs, so identities can be verified in a DVR.

This chapter has no numbered in-text exercises (the text only says “check that \(v\) is well defined” in the proof of 9.2), and the 9 end-of-chapter exercises are all solved below.

Exercise 1

Problem

Let \(A\) be a Dedekind domain, \(S\) a multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\). Show that \(S^{-1}A\) is either a Dedekind domain or the field of fractions of \(A\).

Suppose that \(S\ne A-\{0\}\), and let \(H,H'\) be the ideal class groups of \(A\) and \(S^{-1}A\) respectively. Show that extension of ideals induces a surjective homomorphism \(H\to H'\).

Solution

We assume \(0\notin S\) (otherwise \(S^{-1}A=0\) is not a domain). Then \(A\subseteq S^{-1}A\subseteq K\) and \(S^{-1}A\) is a domain with field of fractions \(K\).

Noetherian. Every ideal \(\fb\) of \(S^{-1}A\) is extended, \(\fb=(\fb^c)^e\) (3.11(i)); if \(\fb^c\) is generated by \(x_1,\dots,x_n\), then \(\fb\) is generated by \(x_1/1,\dots,x_n/1\).

Integrally closed. \(A\) is integrally closed, so \(S^{-1}A\) is integrally closed in \(K\) (5.12).

Primes. The primes of \(S^{-1}A\) correspond bijectively, preserving inclusion, to the primes of \(A\) not meeting \(S\) (3.11(iv)). The prime \((0)\) always corresponds to \((0)\) (\(0\cap S=\emptyset\)), and every non-zero prime of \(A\) is maximal. Hence every chain of primes of \(S^{-1}A\) has length at most \(1\).

Two cases. If every non-zero prime of \(A\) meets \(S\), then \((0)\) is the only prime of \(S^{-1}A\); a non-unit of \(S^{-1}A\) lies in some maximal ideal, which is a non-zero prime unless the element is \(0\); so every non-zero element is a unit and \(S^{-1}A=K\). Otherwise \(S^{-1}A\) has a non-zero prime, so \(\dim S^{-1}A=1\), and \(S^{-1}A\) is a Noetherian, integrally closed domain of dimension one, i.e. a Dedekind domain.

The map \(H\to H'\). (If \(S^{-1}A=K\) the group \(H'\) is trivial, since the only non-zero fractional ideal of \(K\) is \(K\), and there is nothing to prove; so assume \(S^{-1}A\) is Dedekind.) Let \(I,I'\) be the groups of non-zero fractional ideals of \(A\) and \(S^{-1}A\). For \(M\in I\) put \(\theta(M)=S^{-1}M=S^{-1}A\cdot M\subseteq K\). If \(xM\subseteq A\) with \(0\ne x\in A\) then \(x\,S^{-1}M\subseteq S^{-1}A\), so \(\theta(M)\) is a non-zero fractional ideal. Clearly \(\theta(MN)=\theta(M)\theta(N)\) (products are generated by the products of generators), so \(\theta:I\to I'\) is a group homomorphism (it preserves identities and inverses because \(\theta(A)=S^{-1}A\)). For \(u\in K^*\), \(\theta((u))=(u)\), so \(\theta\) maps the principal fractional ideals \(P\) into \(P'\) and induces \(H=I/P\to H'=I'/P'\).

Surjective. It suffices to show \(\theta:I\to I'\) is surjective. Let \(N'\in I'\), and choose \(0\ne z\in S^{-1}A\) with \(zN'\subseteq S^{-1}A\); writing \(z=a/s\) we may replace \(z\) by \(a=sz\in A-\{0\}\) (\(s\) is a unit of \(S^{-1}A\)). Then \(\fb=aN'\) is a non-zero ideal of \(S^{-1}A\), hence \(\fb=\fa^e=S^{-1}\fa\) with \(\fa=\fb^c\) an ideal of \(A\) (3.11(i)). Therefore \(N'=a^{-1}S^{-1}\fa=S^{-1}(a^{-1}\fa)\), and \(M=a^{-1}\fa\) is a non-zero fractional ideal of \(A\) (\(aM=\fa\subseteq A\)). So \(N'=\theta(M)\). ∎

Exercise 2

Problem

Let \(A\) be a Dedekind domain. If \(f=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_nx^n\) is a polynomial with coefficients in \(A\), the content of \(f\) is the ideal \(c(f)=(a_0,\dots,a_n)\) in \(A\). Prove Gauss's lemma that \(c(fg)=c(f)c(g)\). [Localize at each maximal ideal.]

Solution

Always \(c(fg)\subseteq c(f)c(g)\), because every coefficient of \(fg\) is a sum of products \(a_ib_j\) of coefficients of \(f\) and \(g\). By the principle in the Conventions it suffices to show \(c(fg)_\fm=(c(f)c(g))_\fm\) for every maximal ideal \(\fm\). Since localization commutes with products (3.4), \((c(f)c(g))_\fm=c(f)_\fm c(g)_\fm\), and \(c(f)_\fm\) is the ideal of \(A_\fm\) generated by the (images of the) coefficients of \(f\), i.e. the content of \(f\) viewed in \(A_\fm[x]\); likewise for \(g\) and \(fg\). So we may replace \(A\) by \(A_\fm\) and assume \(A\) is a DVR with maximal ideal \((\pi)\) and valuation \(v\) (recall \(\fm\ne0\) since \(A\) is not a field).

If \(f=0\) or \(g=0\) both sides are \(0\). Otherwise let \(a=\min_iv(a_i)\) and \(b=\min_jv(b_j)\). Then \(c(f)=(\pi^a)\), \(c(g)=(\pi^b)\) (ideals of a DVR are the \((\pi^k)\) and \(\pi^a\) divides every coefficient). Write \(f=\pi^af_1\), \(g=\pi^bg_1\) with \(f_1,g_1\in A[x]\); then some coefficient of \(f_1\) and some coefficient of \(g_1\) is a unit, so \(\bar f_1\ne0\) and \(\bar g_1\ne0\) in \((A/\fm)[x]\). As \((A/\fm)[x]\) is a domain, \(\bar f_1\bar g_1\ne0\), i.e. some coefficient of \(f_1g_1\) is not in \(\fm\), so is a unit, and \(c(f_1g_1)=(1)\). Hence \(c(fg)=\pi^{a+b}c(f_1g_1)=(\pi^{a+b})=c(f)c(g)\). ∎

Exercise 3

Problem

A valuation ring (other than a field) is Noetherian if and only if it is a discrete valuation ring.

Solution

Recall that a valuation ring is an integral domain \(A\) with field of fractions \(K\) such that for every \(x\in K^*\) either \(x\in A\) or \(x^{-1}\in A\).

(\(\Leftarrow\)) A DVR is Noetherian: its ideals are \(0\) and \(\fm^k=(x^k)\), all finitely generated (this is shown in the text before 9.2).

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Let \(A\) be a Noetherian valuation ring which is not a field.

\(A\) is local. Let \(x,y\) be non-units of \(A\), not both \(0\), say \(y\ne0\). Then \(x/y\in A\) or \(y/x\in A\); by symmetry (if \(x\ne0\)) suppose \(a=x/y\in A\). Then \(x+y=y(1+a)\) is a multiple of the non-unit \(y\), hence a non-unit. Also \(ax\) is a non-unit for \(a\in A\), \(x\) a non-unit. So the non-units form an ideal, which is therefore the unique maximal ideal \(\fm\), and \(\fm\ne0\) because \(A\) is not a field.

Finitely generated ideals are principal. For \(a,b\in A\) not both \(0\) say \(b\ne0\) and \(a/b\in A\) (else \(b/a\in A\) and swap the roles); then \(a\in(b)\) and \((a,b)=(b)\). By induction every finitely generated ideal of \(A\) is principal. \(A\) is Noetherian, so \(A\) is a principal ideal domain.

Dimension one. A non-zero prime ideal in a PID is maximal, so the only non-zero prime is \(\fm\), and \(\dim A=1\).

Thus \(A\) is a Noetherian local domain of dimension one whose maximal ideal is principal, so \(A\) is a DVR by 9.2 (iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). ∎

Exercise 4

Problem

Let \(A\) be a local domain which is not a field and in which the maximal ideal \(\fm\) is principal and \(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty\fm^n=0\). Prove that \(A\) is a discrete valuation ring.

Solution

Let \(\fm=(x)\); \(x\ne0\) since \(A\) is not a field. Let \(a\in A\), \(a\ne0\). As \(\bigcap\fm^n=0\) there is a largest \(k\ge0\) with \(a\in\fm^k=(x^k)\); write \(a=x^ku\). Then \(u\notin\fm\) (else \(a\in(x^{k+1})\)), so \(u\) is a unit. The integer \(k=:v(a)\) is uniquely determined by \(a\): if \(x^ku=x^lw\) with \(k<l\) and \(u,w\) units, cancelling \(x^k\) in the domain gives \(u=x^{l-k}w\in\fm\), absurd.

Every element of \(K^*\) is \(a/b\) with \(a,b\in A-\{0\}\); define \(v(a/b)=v(a)-v(b)\). This is well defined: if \(a/b=a'/b'\) then \(ab'=a'b\), so \(v(a)+v(b')=v(a')+v(b)\) (units do not change \(v\), and \(v(ab)=v(a)+v(b)\) on \(A-\{0\}\) since \(x^ku\cdot x^lw=x^{k+l}uw\)). Thus \(v:K^*\to\mathbf Z\) is a homomorphism, and it is onto because \(v(x)=1\).

Ultrametric inequality. Let \(\alpha=a/c\), \(\beta=b/c\) with common denominator \(c\) (put \(v(0)=+\infty\); if \(\alpha+\beta=0\), or if \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) is \(0\), there is nothing to prove). Say \(v(a)\le v(b)\) and \(k=v(a)\). Then \(a,b\in(x^k)\), so \(a+b\in(x^k)\) and \(v(a+b)\ge k\) (if \(a+b\ne0\)). Hence \(v(\alpha+\beta)=v(a+b)-v(c)\ge v(a)-v(c)=\min(v(\alpha),v(\beta))\). So \(v\) is a discrete valuation.

\(A\) is its valuation ring. If \(z=a/b\) with \(v(z)\ge0\), i.e. \(v(a)\ge v(b)\), then \(a=x^ku\), \(b=x^lw\) with \(k\ge l\), so \(z=x^{k-l}uw^{-1}\in A\). Conversely for \(a\in A-\{0\}\), \(v(a)\ge0\). Hence \(A=\{z:v(z)\ge0\}\cup\) (\(0\)) is the valuation ring of \(v\), and \(A\) is a discrete valuation ring by definition. ∎

Exercise 5

Problem

Let \(M\) be a finitely generated module over a Dedekind domain. Prove that \(M\) is flat \(\iff\) \(M\) is torsion-free. [Use Chapter 3, Exercise 13 and Chapter 7, Exercise 16.]

Solution

Let \(A\) be the Dedekind domain. (I give a self-contained argument that does not use Chapter 7, Exercise 16; I only use the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID.)

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Let \(M\) be flat and \(0\ne a\in A\). Multiplication by \(a\), \(A\to A\), is injective; tensoring with the flat module \(M\) gives that \(M\to M\), \(m\mapsto am\), is injective. So no non-zero element of \(M\) is killed by a non-zero element of \(A\): \(M\) is torsion-free. (This direction holds for any domain and any module.)

(\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(M\) be finitely generated and torsion-free. By Ch. 3, Ex. 13, \(M_\fm\) is torsion-free for every maximal ideal \(\fm\). Here \(\fm\ne0\) and \(A_\fm\) is a DVR, hence a PID. \(M_\fm\) is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the PID \(A_\fm\), hence free (structure theorem: \(M_\fm\cong A_\fm^r\oplus T\) with \(T\) the torsion submodule, and \(T=0\)). A free module is flat. Thus \(M_\fm\) is a flat \(A_\fm\)-module for all maximal ideals \(\fm\), and so \(M\) is flat over \(A\) by 3.10. ∎

Exercise 6

Problem

Let \(M\) be a finitely generated torsion module (\(T(M)=M\)) over a Dedekind domain \(A\). Prove that \(M\) is uniquely representable as a finite direct sum of modules \(A/\fp_i^{n_i}\), where \(\fp_i\) are non-zero prime ideals of \(A\). [For each \(\fp\ne0\), \(M_\fp\) is a torsion \(A_\fp\)-module; use the structure theorem for modules over a principal ideal domain.]

Solution

“Uniquely” means: the multiset of pairs \((\fp_i,n_i)\) (with \(n_i\ge1\)) is determined by \(M\).

\(\Ann(M)\ne0\). If \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) generate \(M\) and \(0\ne a_i\in\Ann(x_i)\), then \(a=a_1\cdots a_n\ne0\) kills \(M\). Let \(\fa=\Ann(M)\ne0\). If \(\fa=A\) then \(M=0\) (the empty sum); otherwise by 9.4 \(\fa=\fp_1^{e_1}\cdots\fp_r^{e_r}\) with distinct non-zero primes \(\fp_i\) and \(e_i\ge1\). Put \(\fq_i=\fp_i^{e_i}\) and \(\fb_i=\prod_{j\ne i}\fq_j\).

Existence. The ideals \(\fq_i\) are pairwise coprime (distinct maximal ideals have coprime powers: a maximal ideal containing \(\fq_i+\fq_j\) would contain \(\fp_i\) and \(\fp_j\), forcing \(\fp_i=\fp_j\)), so \(\fb_1+\dots+\fb_r=(1)\) (a maximal ideal containing all \(\fb_i\) would, for \(r\ge2\), contain some \(\fq_j\), hence equal some \(\fp_j\) with \(j\ne1\), and also some \(\fq_k\), \(k\ne j\), hence equal \(\fp_k\ne\fp_j\); for \(r=1\), \(\fb_1=A\)). Write \(1=\sum c_ib_i\) with \(b_i\in\fb_i\), and put \(M_i=\{x\in M:\fq_ix=0\}\). For \(x\in M\), \(x=\sum c_ib_ix\) and \(c_ib_ix\in M_i\) because \(\fq_ib_i\subseteq\fa\). The sum is direct: if \(\sum y_i=0\) with \(y_i\in M_i\), multiply by \(b_i\): \(b_iy_j=0\) for \(j\ne i\) (since \(\fq_j\mid\fb_i\) there) so \(b_iy_i=0\); as also \(\fq_iy_i=0\) and \(\fb_i+\fq_i=A\) (\(\fq_i\) is coprime to \(\fb_i\)), \(y_i=0\). So \(M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^rM_i\), each \(M_i\) finitely generated (a quotient of \(M\)) and killed by \(\fq_i=\fp_i^{e_i}\).

Fix \(i\) and write \(\fp=\fp_i\), \(\fq=\fq_i\). For \(s\in A-\fp\) we have \((s)+\fq=A\) (the only maximal ideal containing \(\fq\) is \(\fp\)), so \(ts\equiv1\pmod\fq\) for some \(t\), and \(s\) acts bijectively on \(M_i\). Hence \(M_i\to(M_i)_\fp\), \(x\mapsto x/1\), is an isomorphism of \(A\)-modules (injective since \(sx=0\Rightarrow x=tsx=0\); surjective since \(x/s=tx/1\)). Now \((M_i)_\fp\) is a finitely generated module over the DVR \(A_\fp\), killed by \(\fp^{e}A_\fp\), so it is a torsion module and by the structure theorem

\[ (M_i)_\fp\cong\bigoplus_jA_\fp/\fp^{k_j}A_\fp\qquad(k_j\ge1), \]

and \(A_\fp/\fp^kA_\fp\cong(A/\fp^k)_\fp\cong A/\fp^k\) (by the same argument as above, elements outside \(\fp\) act invertibly on \(A/\fp^k\)). Therefore \(M_i\cong\bigoplus_jA/\fp_i^{k_{ij}}\) as \(A\)-modules, and \(M\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}A/\fp_i^{k_{ij}}\).

Uniqueness. Suppose \(M\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^mA/\fp_j^{n_j}\) with non-zero primes \(\fp_j\) and \(n_j\ge1\). Fix a non-zero prime \(\fp\). If \(\fp_j\ne\fp\), then \(\fp_j^{n_j}\not\subseteq\fp\) (else \(\fp_j\subseteq\fp\), so \(\fp_j=\fp\) as both are maximal), so there is \(s\in\fp_j^{n_j}-\fp\), which kills \(A/\fp_j^{n_j}\), and hence \((A/\fp_j^{n_j})_\fp=0\). If \(\fp_j=\fp\) then \((A/\fp^{n_j})_\fp\cong A_\fp/\fp^{n_j}A_\fp\) (3.4). Thus

\[ M_\fp\cong\bigoplus_{j:\ \fp_j=\fp}A_\fp/\fp^{n_j}A_\fp . \]

By the uniqueness part of the structure theorem over the PID \(A_\fp\) (the elementary divisors \(\fp^{n_j}A_\fp\), equivalently the exponents \(n_j\), are determined by \(M_\fp\)), the multiset \(\{n_j:\fp_j=\fp\}\) depends only on \(M\). This holds for every non-zero prime \(\fp\), so the multiset of pairs \((\fp_j,n_j)\) is determined by \(M\). ∎

Exercise 7

Problem

Let \(A\) be a Dedekind domain and \(\fa\ne0\) an ideal in \(A\). Show that every ideal in \(A/\fa\) is principal.

Deduce that every ideal in \(A\) can be generated by at most \(2\) elements.

Solution

Let \(\fa=\fp_1^{e_1}\cdots\fp_r^{e_r}\) (9.4; if \(\fa=A\) then \(A/\fa=0\) and there is nothing to prove). Ideals of \(A/\fa\) are of the form \(\fb/\fa\) with \(\fb\supseteq\fa\) an ideal of \(A\). If \(\fb\supseteq\fa\) then \(\fb\ne0\) and \(\fb^{-1}\fa\subseteq\fb^{-1}\fb=A\), so \(\fa=\fb\cdot(\fb^{-1}\fa)\) is a multiple of \(\fb\); by unique factorization \(\fb=\fp_1^{f_1}\cdots\fp_r^{f_r}\) with \(0\le f_i\le e_i\) (only primes dividing \(\fa\) occur in \(\fb\)).

Choice of a generator. For each \(i\), \(\fp_i^{f_i}\ne\fp_i^{f_i+1}\) (unique factorization), so pick \(x_i\in\fp_i^{f_i}-\fp_i^{f_i+1}\). The ideals \(\fp_i^{f_i+1}\) are pairwise coprime, so by the Chinese remainder theorem (1.10) there is \(x\in A\) with \(x\equiv x_i\pmod{\fp_i^{f_i+1}}\) for all \(i\). Then \(x\in\fp_i^{f_i}\) and \(x\notin\fp_i^{f_i+1}\) (since \(x-x_i\in\fp_i^{f_i+1}\) and \(x_i\in\fp_i^{f_i}-\fp_i^{f_i+1}\)).

Claim: \(\fb=(x)+\fa\). Both are ideals, so we compare localizations at every maximal ideal \(\fm\) (Conventions). Recall that \(\fp^{k+1}A_\fp\cap A=\fp^{k+1}\) for a non-zero prime \(\fp\) (the ideal \(\fp^{k+1}\) is \(\fp\)-primary, 4.2, and 4.8(ii)).

  • •

    \(\fm=\fp_i\): in the DVR \(A_{\fp_i}\) the element \(x\) lies in \(\fp_i^{f_i}A_{\fp_i}\) but not in \(\fp_i^{f_i+1}A_{\fp_i}\) (otherwise \(x\in\fp_i^{f_i+1}A_{\fp_i}\cap A=\fp_i^{f_i+1}\)), so \((x)_{\fm}=\fp_i^{f_i}A_{\fm}\). Moreover \(\fa_{\fm}=\fp_i^{e_i}A_\fm\) (\(\fp_j\not\subseteq\fm\) for \(j\ne i\), so \(\fp_j\) becomes the unit ideal), and \(\fp_i^{e_i}A_\fm\subseteq\fp_i^{f_i}A_\fm\). So \(((x)+\fa)_\fm=\fp_i^{f_i}A_\fm=\fb_\fm\).

  • •

    \(\fm\notin\{\fp_1,\dots,\fp_r\}\): then \(\fa_\fm=A_\fm\) and \(\fb_\fm=A_\fm\) (as \(\fb\supseteq\fa\)), so both sides equal \(A_\fm\).

Thus \(\fb=(x)+\fa\), i.e. \(\fb/\fa\) is generated by the class of \(x\) in \(A/\fa\). Every ideal of \(A/\fa\) is principal.

Every ideal is \(2\)-generated. Let \(\fb\) be an ideal of \(A\). If \(\fb=0\) it is principal. Otherwise pick \(0\ne a\in\fb\) and apply the above to \(\fa=(a)\ne0\): the ideal \(\fb/(a)\) of \(A/(a)\) is principal, generated by the class of some \(x\in\fb\); hence \(\fb=(a,x)\). ∎

Exercise 8

Problem

Let \(\fa,\fb,\fc\) be three ideals in a Dedekind domain. Prove that

\begin{align*} \fa\cap(\fb+\fc)&=(\fa\cap\fb)+(\fa\cap\fc),\\ \fa+(\fb\cap\fc)&=(\fa+\fb)\cap(\fa+\fc). \end{align*}

[Localize.]

Solution

In each identity the right-hand side is contained in the left-hand side for the first, and the left-hand side is contained in the right-hand side for the second, in any ring: \((\fa\cap\fb)+(\fa\cap\fc)\subseteq\fa\cap(\fb+\fc)\), and \(\fa+(\fb\cap\fc)\subseteq(\fa+\fb)\cap(\fa+\fc)\). So it suffices to compare localizations at every maximal ideal \(\fm\) (Conventions). Localization commutes with finite sums and intersections (3.4), so we must check both identities for the three ideals \(\fa_\fm,\fb_\fm,\fc_\fm\) of the DVR \(A_\fm\).

In a DVR with maximal ideal \((\pi)\) the ideals are \((\pi^k)\) (\(k\ge0\)) and \(0\), so any two ideals are comparable under inclusion. By symmetry in \(\fb,\fc\) we may assume \(\fb\subseteq\fc\). Then \(\fb+\fc=\fc\) and \(\fb\cap\fc=\fb\). First identity: \(\fa\cap(\fb+\fc)=\fa\cap\fc\), and \(\fa\cap\fb\subseteq\fa\cap\fc\) gives \((\fa\cap\fb)+(\fa\cap\fc)=\fa\cap\fc\). Second identity: \(\fa+(\fb\cap\fc)=\fa+\fb\), and \(\fa+\fb\subseteq\fa+\fc\) gives \((\fa+\fb)\cap(\fa+\fc)=\fa+\fb\). So both identities hold locally, hence globally. ∎

Exercise 9

Problem

(Chinese Remainder Theorem.) Let \(\fa_1,\dots,\fa_n\) be ideals and let \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) be elements in a Dedekind domain \(A\). Then the system of congruences \(x\equiv x_i\pmod{\fa_i}\) \((1\le i\le n)\) has a solution \(x\) in \(A\) \(\iff\) \(x_i\equiv x_j\pmod{\fa_i+\fa_j}\) whenever \(i\ne j\).

[This is equivalent to saying that the sequence of \(A\)-modules

\[ A\xrightarrow{\ \phi\ }\bigoplus_{i=1}^nA/\fa_i\xrightarrow{\ \psi\ }\bigoplus_{i<j}A/(\fa_i+\fa_j) \]

is exact, where \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) are defined as follows: \(\phi(x)=(x+\fa_1,\dots,x+\fa_n)\); \(\psi(x_1+\fa_1,\dots,x_n+\fa_n)\) has \((i,j)\)-component \(x_i-x_j+\fa_i+\fa_j\). To show that this sequence is exact it is enough to show that it is exact when localized at any \(\fp\ne0\): in other words we may assume that \(A\) is a discrete valuation ring, and then it is easy.]

Solution

The reformulation. A tuple \((x_i+\fa_i)_i\) lies in \(\Ker\psi\) iff \(x_i-x_j\in\fa_i+\fa_j\) for all \(i<j\), and lies in \(\operatorname{Im}\phi\) iff there is \(x\in A\) with \(x\equiv x_i\pmod{\fa_i}\) for all \(i\). So the statement says exactly that \(\Ker\psi=\operatorname{Im}\phi\). Always \(\psi\phi=0\): the \((i,j)\)-component of \(\psi\phi(x)\) is \(x-x\) modulo \(\fa_i+\fa_j\). (This is also the easy direction of the equivalence: a solution \(x\) gives \(x_i-x_j\equiv x-x=0\).) Put \(E=\Ker\psi/\operatorname{Im}\phi\); we must show \(E=0\).

Localization. Localization is exact (3.3), so for every maximal ideal \(\fm\) the localized sequence is exact in the middle iff \(E_\fm=0\) (because \((\Ker\psi)_\fm=\Ker\psi_\fm\) and \((\operatorname{Im}\phi)_\fm=\operatorname{Im}\phi_\fm\)). Using \((A/\fa)_\fm\cong A_\fm/\fa_\fm\) and \((\fa_i+\fa_j)_\fm=(\fa_i)_\fm+(\fa_j)_\fm\) (3.4), the localized sequence is the same sequence for the ring \(A_\fm\) and the ideals \((\fa_i)_\fm\). If \(E_\fm=0\) for all maximal \(\fm\), then \(E=0\) (3.8). Every maximal ideal \(\fm\) is non-zero, so \(A_\fm\) is a DVR.

The DVR case. Let \(A\) be a DVR with maximal ideal \((\pi)\). Each \(\fa_i\) is \(0\) or \((\pi^{k_i})\); write \(\fa_i=(\pi^{k_i})\) with \(k_i\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}\) (\(\pi^\infty:=0\)). Then \(\fa_i+\fa_j=(\pi^{\min(k_i,k_j)})\). Suppose \(x_i-x_j\in\fa_i+\fa_j\) for all \(i,j\). Renumber so that \(k_1\le k_2\le\dots\le k_n\) and take \(x=x_n\). For \(i\le n\), \(x_n-x_i\in\fa_i+\fa_n=(\pi^{\min(k_i,k_n)})=(\pi^{k_i})=\fa_i\) (for \(i=n\) this is \(0\)). So \(x\equiv x_i\pmod{\fa_i}\) for all \(i\): a solution exists.

Hence \(E_\fm=0\) for every maximal \(\fm\), so \(E=0\) and the system is solvable iff the compatibility conditions hold. ∎
