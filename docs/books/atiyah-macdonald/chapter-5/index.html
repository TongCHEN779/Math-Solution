Conventions. A ring homomorphism \(f:A\to B\) is integral if \(B\) is integral over \(f(A)\); for a subring \(A\subseteq B\) this is the usual notion. From the text I use: 5.1–5.3 (the elements of \(B\) integral over \(A\) form a subring; \(A[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-module if the \(x_i\) are integral), 5.4 (transitivity), 5.5 (the integral closure is integrally closed), 5.6 (integrality passes to quotients and rings of fractions), 5.7 and 5.8 (for \(A\subseteq B\) integral domains, \(B\) is a field iff \(A\) is; hence \(\fq\) maximal \(\iff\fq^c\) maximal), 5.9 (incomparability), 5.10 (lying over), 5.11 (going-up), 5.12, 5.13, 5.14, 5.15, 5.16 (going-down), 5.18 (valuation rings are local and integrally closed, overrings of valuation rings are valuation rings), 5.21 (a maximal pair \((B,g)\) is a valuation ring), 5.22 (the integral closure is the intersection of the valuation rings containing \(A\)), and 5.23. Also: Ch. 1, Ex. 17, 18, 21; Ch. 3, Ex. 18, 21, 22, 23, 26; 1.11 (prime avoidance); Ch. 2, Ex. 20, 21. A “local subring” of a field is a subring that is a local ring.

Exercise 1

Problem

Let \(f:A\to B\) be an integral homomorphism of rings. Show that \(f^*:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) is a closed mapping, i.e. that it maps closed sets to closed sets. (This is a geometrical equivalent of (5.10).)

Solution

Let \(V(\fb)\) be a closed subset of \(\Spec B\). We show \(f^*(V(\fb))=V(\fb^c)\), which is closed. The inclusion \(\subseteq\) is clear: if \(\fq\supseteq\fb\) then \(f^*(\fq)=\fq^c\supseteq\fb^c\). Conversely let \(\fp\supseteq\fb^c\) be a prime of \(A\). The induced map \(\bar f:A/\fb^c\to B/\fb\) is injective, and \(B/\fb\) is integral over \(\bar f(A/\fb^c)\) by 5.6(i). The prime \(\fp/\fb^c\) of \(A/\fb^c\) is therefore the contraction of a prime of \(B/\fb\) (5.10), i.e. there is a prime \(\fq\supseteq\fb\) of \(B\) with \(\fq^c/\fb^c=\fp/\fb^c\), so \(\fq^c=\fp\). Hence \(\fp\in f^*(V(\fb))\). ∎

Exercise 2

Problem

Let \(A\) be a subring of a ring \(B\) such that \(B\) is integral over \(A\), and let \(f:A\to\Omega\) be a homomorphism of \(A\) into an algebraically closed field \(\Omega\). Show that \(f\) can be extended to a homomorphism of \(B\) into \(\Omega\). [Use (5.10).]

Solution

Let \(\fp=\Ker f\), a prime ideal (the image is a subring of a field). By 5.10 there is a prime \(\fq\) of \(B\) with \(\fq\cap A=\fp\). Then \(A/\fp\hookrightarrow B/\fq\) is an inclusion of integral domains with \(B/\fq\) integral over \(A/\fp\) (5.6). Let \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(A/\fp)\) and \(L=\operatorname{Frac}(B/\fq)\). The injection \(A/\fp\to\Omega\) induced by \(f\) extends to \(K\to\Omega\). Every element of \(B/\fq\) is integral, hence algebraic, over \(K\), so \(L\) is generated by algebraic elements and \(L/K\) is an algebraic extension. Since \(\Omega\) is algebraically closed, the embedding \(K\to\Omega\) extends to an embedding \(L\to\Omega\) (Zorn's lemma, the standard extension theorem). The composite \(B\to B/\fq\to L\to\Omega\) restricts on \(A\) to \(A\to A/\fp\to K\to\Omega\), which is \(f\). ∎

Exercise 3

Problem

Let \(f:B\to B'\) be a homomorphism of \(A\)-algebras, and let \(C\) be an \(A\)-algebra. If \(f\) is integral, prove that \(f\otimes1:B\otimes_AC\to B'\otimes_AC\) is integral. (This includes (5.6) ii) as a special case.)

Solution

Let \(g=f\otimes1\). The ring \(B'\otimes_AC\) is generated as an abelian group by elements \(b'\otimes c=(b'\otimes1)(1\otimes c)\). Now \(1\otimes c=g(1\otimes c)\) lies in \(g(B\otimes C)\). If \(b'\in B'\) satisfies \(b'^n+f(b_1)b'^{n-1}+\dots+f(b_n)=0\) with \(b_i\in B\), then

\[ (b'\otimes1)^n+g(b_1\otimes1)(b'\otimes1)^{n-1}+\dots+g(b_n\otimes1)=0, \]

so \(b'\otimes1\) is integral over \(g(B\otimes C)\). By 5.3 the elements of \(B'\otimes C\) integral over \(g(B\otimes C)\) form a subring; it contains all \(b'\otimes1\) and all \(1\otimes c\), hence all products and sums of these, i.e. everything. ∎

Exercise 4

Problem

Let \(A\) be a subring of a ring \(B\) such that \(B\) is integral over \(A\). Let \(\fn\) be a maximal ideal of \(B\) and let \(\fm=\fn\cap A\) be the corresponding maximal ideal of \(A\). Is \(B_\fn\) necessarily integral over \(A_\fm\)? [Consider the subring \(k[x^2-1]\) of \(k[x]\), where \(k\) is a field, and let \(\fn=(x-1)\). Can the element \(1/(x+1)\) be integral?]

Solution

No. Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(\ne2\) (in characteristic \(2\) one has \(x+1=x-1\), and the element below is not in \(B_\fn\)). Put \(y=x^2-1\), \(A=k[y]\subseteq B=k[x]\). Then \(y\) is transcendental, so \(A\) is a polynomial ring, and \(B\) is integral over \(A\) since \(x^2-y-1=0\). Let \(\fn=(x-1)\); then \(\fm=\fn\cap A=(y)\) (a polynomial \(g(y)\) vanishes at \(x=1\) iff \(g(0)=0\)). \(B_\fn\) is an \(A_\fm\)-algebra because \(A-\fm\subseteq B-\fn\).

The element \(z=1/(x+1)\) lies in \(B_\fn\) since \(x+1\notin\fn\) (its value at \(x=1\) is \(2\ne0\)). We have \(z=(1-x)/(1-x^2)=(x-1)/y\) in \(k(x)\), and \(z\notin k(y)\) because \([k(x):k(y)]=2\) and \(x\notin k(y)\); the conjugate of \(z\) over \(k(y)\) (apply \(x\mapsto-x\)) is \(z'=-(x+1)/y\), so \(z+z'=-2/y\), \(zz'=-1/y\), and the minimal polynomial of \(z\) over \(k(y)\) is

\[ t^2+\tfrac2y\,t-\tfrac1y . \]

\(A_\fm=k[y]_{(y)}\) is a principal ideal domain, hence integrally closed in \(k(y)\). If \(z\) were integral over \(A_\fm\), then by 5.15 (with \(\fa=(1)\), \(B=k(x)\)) the coefficients of its minimal polynomial would lie in \(A_\fm\); but \(1/y\notin A_\fm\). So \(z\) is not integral over \(A_\fm\), and \(B_\fn\) is not integral over \(A_\fm\). ∎

Remark. The hypothesis \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\) is really needed for this example: if \(\operatorname{char}k=2\) then, with \(u=x-1\) and \(y=u^2\), every element \(a(u)/b(u)\) of \(B_\fn\) (\(b(0)\ne0\)) equals \(a(u)b(u)/b(u)^2\) with \(b(u)^2\in k[y]-(y)\), so it lies in \(A_\fm[u]\), which is integral over \(A_\fm\); thus there \(B_\fn\) is integral over \(A_\fm\).

Exercise 5

Problem

Let \(A\subseteq B\) be rings, \(B\) integral over \(A\).

  1. (i)

    If \(x\in A\) is a unit in \(B\) then it is a unit in \(A\).

  2. (ii)

    The Jacobson radical of \(A\) is the contraction of the Jacobson radical of \(B\).

Solution

(i) \(x^{-1}\in B\) is integral over \(A\): \(x^{-m}+a_1x^{-m+1}+\dots+a_m=0\) with \(a_i\in A\). Multiplying by \(x^{m-1}\) gives \(x^{-1}=-(a_1+a_2x+\dots+a_mx^{m-1})\in A\).

(ii) Every maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\) is of the form \(\fn\cap A\) with \(\fn\) a maximal ideal of \(B\): by 5.10 there is a prime \(\fn\) with \(\fn\cap A=\fm\), and it is maximal by 5.8. Conversely \(\fn\cap A\) is maximal for every maximal \(\fn\) of \(B\) (5.8). Hence

\[ \Jac(A)=\bigcap_{\fm}\fm=\bigcap_{\fn}(\fn\cap A)=\Bigl(\bigcap_{\fn}\fn\Bigr)\cap A=\Jac(B)\cap A=\Jac(B)^c. \qquad\square \]

Exercise 6

Problem

Let \(B_1,\dots,B_n\) be integral \(A\)-algebras. Show that \(\prod_{i=1}^nB_i\) is an integral \(A\)-algebra.

Solution

Let \(f_i:A\to B_i\) be the structure maps and \(f=(f_i):A\to B=\prod B_i\). Let \(b=(b_1,\dots,b_n)\in B\). For each \(i\) choose a monic \(P_i\in A[t]\) with \(P_i^{f_i}(b_i)=0\), where \(P^{f_i}\) denotes the polynomial obtained by applying \(f_i\) to the coefficients. Let \(P=P_1P_2\cdots P_n\in A[t]\), monic. The \(i\)-th component of \(P^f(b)\) is \(P^{f_i}(b_i)=P_i^{f_i}(b_i)\cdot\prod_{j\ne i}P_j^{f_i}(b_i)=0\). So \(P^f(b)=0\) and \(b\) is integral over \(f(A)\). ∎

Exercise 7

Problem

Let \(A\) be a subring of a ring \(B\), such that the set \(B-A\) is closed under multiplication. Show that \(A\) is integrally closed in \(B\).

Solution

Suppose \(x\in B\) is integral over \(A\) but \(x\notin A\). Choose a monic equation \(x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+\dots+a_n=0\) (\(a_i\in A\)) of smallest degree \(n\ge1\). If \(n=1\) then \(x=-a_1\in A\), a contradiction, so \(n\ge2\). Put \(y=x^{n-1}+a_1x^{n-2}+\dots+a_{n-1}\in B\). Then \(xy=-a_n\in A\). If \(y\notin A\), then \(x,y\in B-A\) and so \(xy\in B-A\), contradicting \(xy\in A\). Hence \(y\in A\). But then

\[ x^{n-1}+a_1x^{n-2}+\dots+a_{n-2}x+(a_{n-1}-y)=0 \]

is a monic equation of degree \(n-1\ge1\) for \(x\) over \(A\), contradicting the minimality of \(n\). ∎

Exercise 8

Problem
  1. (i)

    Let \(A\) be a subring of an integral domain \(B\), and let \(C\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(B\). Let \(f,g\) be monic polynomials in \(B[x]\) such that \(fg\in C[x]\). Then \(f,g\) are in \(C[x]\). [Take a field containing \(B\) in which the polynomials \(f,g\) split into linear factors: say \(f=\prod(x-\xi_i)\), \(g=\prod(x-\eta_j)\). Each \(\xi_i\) and each \(\eta_j\) is a root of \(fg\), hence is integral over \(C\). Hence the coefficients of \(f\) and \(g\) are integral over \(C\).]

  2. (ii)

    Prove the same result without assuming that \(B\) (or \(A\)) is an integral domain.

Solution

I give one proof, valid for arbitrary rings; it contains (i) (for a domain \(B\) one may of course use a splitting field of \(fg\) over \(\operatorname{Frac}B\) instead of the ring \(B''\) below).

Step 1: a ring \(B''\supseteq B\) over which \(f\) and \(g\) split. If \(h\in B[x]\) is monic of degree \(d\ge1\), then \(B'=B[x]/(h)\) is a free \(B\)-module on \(1,x,\dots,x^{d-1}\), so \(B\to B'\) is injective, and the class \(\xi\) of \(x\) is a root of \(h\). Dividing by the monic polynomial \(x-\xi\) in \(B'[x]\) gives \(h=(x-\xi)h_1\) with \(h_1\) monic of degree \(d-1\). Repeating this with \(h_1\) in place of \(h\), and then with \(g\), we obtain a ring \(B''\supseteq B\) and \(f=\prod(x-\xi_i)\), \(g=\prod(x-\eta_j)\) in \(B''[x]\).

Step 2. \(fg\in C[x]\) is monic and vanishes at each \(\xi_i\) and each \(\eta_j\) (a root of \(f\) or \(g\) is a root of \(fg\)). So these elements of \(B''\) are integral over \(C\). By 5.3 the elements of \(B''\) integral over \(C\) form a subring \(C''\). The coefficients of \(f\) and \(g\) are, up to sign, elementary symmetric functions in the \(\xi_i\) (resp. \(\eta_j\)), hence lie in \(C''\). They also lie in \(B\). So they are elements of \(B\) integral over \(C\), hence integral over \(A\) (5.4, as \(A\subseteq C\subseteq B''\)), hence in \(C\) (definition of \(C\)). Therefore \(f,g\in C[x]\). ∎

Exercise 9

Problem

Let \(A\) be a subring of a ring \(B\) and let \(C\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(B\). Prove that \(C[x]\) is the integral closure of \(A[x]\) in \(B[x]\). [If \(f\in B[x]\) is integral over \(A[x]\), then \(f^m+g_1f^{m-1}+\dots+g_m=0\) (\(g_i\in A[x]\)). Let \(r\) be an integer larger than \(m\) and the degrees of \(g_1,\dots,g_m\), and let \(f_1=f-x^r\), so that \((f_1+x^r)^m+g_1(f+x^r)^{m-1}+\dots+g_m=0\), or say \(f_1^m+h_1f_1^{m-1}+\dots+h_m=0\), where \(h_m=(x^r)^m+g_1(x^r)^{m-1}+\dots+g_m\in A[x]\). Now apply Exercise 8 to the polynomials \(-f_1\) and \(f_1^{m-1}+h_1f_1^{m-2}+\dots+h_{m-1}\).]

Solution

\(C[x]\) is integral over \(A[x]\). A polynomial \(\sum c_ix^i\in C[x]\) is a sum of terms \(c_ix^i\) with \(c_i\) integral over \(A\subseteq A[x]\) and \(x\in A[x]\), so each term is integral over \(A[x]\), and so is the sum (5.3).

Every \(f\in B[x]\) integral over \(A[x]\) lies in \(C[x]\). Let \(f^m+g_1f^{m-1}+\dots+g_m=0\) with \(g_i\in A[x]\). Choose \(r\) larger than \(m\), than \(\deg f\), and than all \(\deg g_i\). (Only \(r>\deg f\) and \(r>\deg g_i\) are used below.) Put \(f_1=f-x^r\). Substituting \(f=f_1+x^r\) and expanding in powers of \(f_1\) gives

\[ f_1^m+h_1f_1^{m-1}+\dots+h_{m-1}f_1+h_m=0,\qquad h_i\in A[x], \]

where \(h_m=x^{rm}+g_1x^{r(m-1)}+\dots+g_m\) (the value of the left side at \(f_1=0\)). Since \(\deg g_i<r\) we have \(\deg(g_ix^{r(m-i)})<rm\) for \(i\ge1\), so \(h_m\) is monic of degree \(rm\). Put \(F=f_1^{m-1}+h_1f_1^{m-2}+\dots+h_{m-1}\in B[x]\). Then

\[ (-f_1)\cdot F=h_m . \]

Since \(r>\deg f\), the polynomial \(-f_1=x^r-f\) is monic of degree \(r\). Dividing the monic polynomial \(h_m\) of degree \(rm\) by the monic \(-f_1\): the product of \(-f_1\) with a polynomial \(F\) has leading coefficient equal to that of \(F\) and degree \(r+\deg F\) (as \(-f_1\) is monic); comparing with \(h_m\) gives \(\deg F=r(m-1)\) and \(F\) monic. Now \(-f_1\) and \(F\) are monic in \(B[x]\) and their product \(h_m\) lies in \(A[x]\subseteq C[x]\). By Exercise 8, \(-f_1\in C[x]\). Hence \(f=x^r+f_1\in C[x]\). ∎

Exercise 10

Problem

A ring homomorphism \(f:A\to B\) is said to have the going-up property (resp. the going-down property) if the conclusion of the going-up theorem (5.11) (resp. the going-down theorem (5.16)) holds for \(B\) and its subring \(f(A)\).

Let \(f^*:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) be the mapping associated with \(f\).

  1. (i)

    Consider the following three statements: (a) \(f^*\) is a closed mapping; (b) \(f\) has the going-up property; (c) let \(\fq\) be any prime ideal of \(B\) and let \(\fp=\fq^c\); then \(f^*:\Spec(B/\fq)\to\Spec(A/\fp)\) is surjective. Prove that (a)\(\Rightarrow\)(b)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(c). (See also Chapter 6, Exercise 11.)

  2. (ii)

    Consider the following three statements: (a\('\)) \(f^*\) is an open mapping; (b\('\)) \(f\) has the going-down property; (c\('\)) for any prime ideal \(\fq\) of \(B\), if \(\fp=\fq^c\), then \(f^*:\Spec(B_\fq)\to\Spec(A_\fp)\) is surjective. Prove that (a\('\))\(\Rightarrow\)(b\('\))\(\Leftrightarrow\)(c\('\)). (See also Chapter 7, Exercise 23.)

[To prove that (a\('\))\(\Rightarrow\)(c\('\)), observe that \(B_\fq\) is the direct limit of the rings \(B_t\) where \(t\in B-\fq\); hence, by Chapter 3, Exercise 26, we have \(f^*(\Spec(B_\fq))=\bigcap_tf^*(\Spec(B_t))=\bigcap_tf^*(Y_t)\). Since \(Y_t\) is an open neighborhood of \(\fq\) in \(Y\), and since \(f^*\) is open, it follows that \(f^*(Y_t)\) is an open neighborhood of \(\fp\) in \(X\) and therefore contains \(\Spec(A_\fp)\).]

Solution

It suffices to treat chains of length \(2\): by induction on the lengths, the going-up (resp. going-down) property holds iff for primes \(\fp_1\subseteq\fp_2\) (resp. \(\fp_1\supseteq\fp_2\)) of \(A\) and a prime \(\fq_1\) of \(B\) with \(\fq_1^c=\fp_1\) there is a prime \(\fq_2\supseteq\fq_1\) (resp. \(\fq_2\subseteq\fq_1\)) with \(\fq_2^c=\fp_2\). (Given a chain \(\fp_1\subseteq\dots\subseteq\fp_n\) and \(\fq_1\subseteq\dots\subseteq\fq_m\) one extends one step at a time.)

(i) (a)\(\Rightarrow\)(b). Let \(\fp_1\subseteq\fp_2\) and \(\fq_1^c=\fp_1\). The set \(f^*(V(\fq_1))\) is closed, and contains \(\fp_1\); hence it contains the closure \(V(\fp_1)\) of \(\{\fp_1\}\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 18), in particular \(\fp_2\). So \(\fp_2=\fq_2^c\) for some \(\fq_2\in V(\fq_1)\).

(b)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(c). The map \(f\) induces \(\bar f:A/\fp\to B/\fq\) (for \(\fp=\fq^c\)), and under the identifications \(\Spec(A/\fp)=V(\fp)\), \(\Spec(B/\fq)=V(\fq)\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 21(iv)) the map \(\bar f^*\) is \(\fq'\mapsto\fq'^c\) (Ch. 3, Ex. 21(iii): \(\bar f^*\) is the restriction of \(f^*\) to \(V(\fq)\)). So (c) says: every prime \(\fp'\supseteq\fp\) of \(A\) is the contraction of a prime \(\fq'\supseteq\fq\) of \(B\). This is exactly the going-up property for \(\fp\subseteq\fp'\) and \(\fq\) over \(\fp\).

(ii) (b\('\))\(\Leftrightarrow\)(c\('\)). Primes of \(A_\fp\) correspond to primes \(\fp'\subseteq\fp\) of \(A\), primes of \(B_\fq\) to primes \(\fq'\subseteq\fq\) of \(B\) (3.13), and under these identifications \(\Spec B_\fq\to\Spec A_\fp\) is \(\fq'\mapsto\fq'^c\) (Ch. 3, Ex. 21(ii)). So (c\('\)) says: every prime \(\fp'\subseteq\fp=\fq^c\) is a contraction of a prime \(\fq'\subseteq\fq\). This is going-down.

(a\('\))\(\Rightarrow\)(c\('\)). Let \(\fq\in Y=\Spec B\), \(\fp=\fq^c\). The rings \(B_t\), \(t\in B-\fq\), with the restriction maps, form a direct system of \(A\)-algebras whose direct limit is \(B_\fq\) (Ch. 3, Ex. 23(v)). By Ch. 3, Ex. 26,

\[ f^*(\Spec B_\fq)=\bigcap_{t\notin\fq}f^*(\Spec B_t)=\bigcap_{t\notin\fq}f^*(Y_t), \]

since the image of \(\Spec B_t\) in \(Y\) is the basic open set \(Y_t\) (Ch. 3, Ex. 21(i)). Each \(Y_t\) is an open neighbourhood of \(\fq\), so as \(f^*\) is open, \(f^*(Y_t)\) is an open neighbourhood of \(\fp\) in \(X=\Spec A\); by Ch. 3, Ex. 22 it contains the image of \(\Spec A_\fp\). Hence the image of \(\Spec B_\fq\) in \(\Spec A\) contains the image of \(\Spec A_\fp\), i.e. the set of primes \(\fp'\subseteq\fp\). It is also contained there (if \(\fq'\subseteq\fq\) then \(\fq'^c\subseteq\fp\)). So \(\Spec B_\fq\to\Spec A_\fp\) is surjective. ∎

Exercise 11

Problem

Let \(f:A\to B\) be a flat homomorphism of rings. Then \(f\) has the going-down property. [Chapter 3, Exercise 18.]

Solution

Let \(\fq\) be a prime of \(B\) and \(\fp=\fq^c\). By Ch. 3, Ex. 18, \(f^*:\Spec(B_\fq)\to\Spec(A_\fp)\) is surjective. This is condition (c\('\)) of Exercise 10, which is equivalent to the going-down property (b\('\)). ∎

Exercise 12

Problem

Let \(G\) be a finite group of automorphisms of a ring \(A\), and let \(A^G\) denote the subring of \(G\)-invariants, that is of all \(x\in A\) such that \(\sigma(x)=x\) for all \(\sigma\in G\). Prove that \(A\) is integral over \(A^G\). [If \(x\in A\), observe that \(x\) is a root of the polynomial \(\prod_{\sigma\in G}(t-\sigma(x))\).]

Let \(S\) be a multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\) such that \(\sigma(S)\subseteq S\) for all \(\sigma\in G\), and let \(S^G=S\cap A^G\). Show that the action of \(G\) on \(A\) extends to an action on \(S^{-1}A\), and that \((S^G)^{-1}A^G\cong(S^{-1}A)^G\).

Solution

Integrality. \(P(t)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}(t-\sigma(x))\in A[t]\) is monic and \(P(x)=0\) (the factor \(\sigma=1\) vanishes). For \(\tau\in G\), \(\tau\) applied to the coefficients permutes the factors (\(\sigma\mapsto\tau\sigma\) is a bijection of \(G\)), so \(P\) has coefficients in \(A^G\).

Extension of the action. Put \(\sigma(a/s)=\sigma(a)/\sigma(s)\); this makes sense since \(\sigma(s)\in S\). It is well defined: if \(a/s=a'/s'\), i.e. \(u(as'-a's)=0\) for some \(u\in S\), apply \(\sigma\) to get \(\sigma(u)(\sigma(a)\sigma(s')-\sigma(a')\sigma(s))=0\) with \(\sigma(u)\in S\). It is a ring homomorphism, and \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma\) is an action (\(\sigma\tau(a/s)=\sigma(\tau(a/s))\)), so each \(\sigma\) is an automorphism.

The isomorphism. Define \(\varphi:(S^G)^{-1}A^G\to S^{-1}A\) by \(a/s\mapsto a/s\); its image lies in \((S^{-1}A)^G\) since \(\sigma(a/s)=a/s\) for \(a\in A^G\), \(s\in S^G\). For \(t\in S\) put \(N(t)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(t)\); then \(N(t)\in S\) (since \(\sigma(S)\subseteq S\) and \(S\) is multiplicatively closed) and \(N(t)\in A^G\), so \(N(t)\in S^G\), and \(N(t)\in tA\).

Injective. If \(a/s=0\) in \(S^{-1}A\) with \(a\in A^G\), \(s\in S^G\), then \(ta=0\) for some \(t\in S\); hence \(N(t)a=0\) with \(N(t)\in S^G\), so \(a/s=0\) in \((S^G)^{-1}A^G\).

Surjective onto \((S^{-1}A)^G\). Let \(\xi=a/s\in(S^{-1}A)^G\). Put \(s'=\prod_{\sigma\ne1}\sigma(s)\in S\) and \(t=ss'=N(s)\in S^G\); then \(\xi=as'/t\). Since \(\sigma(\xi)=\xi\) we get \(\sigma(as')/t=as'/t\), so there is \(u_\sigma\in S\) with \(u_\sigma(\sigma(as')-as')=0\). Let \(u=\prod_\sigma u_\sigma\in S\) and \(u'=N(u)\in S^G\); since \(u\mid u'\) in \(A\), \(u'(\sigma(as')-as')=0\) for all \(\sigma\). Then \(b=u'as'\in A\) satisfies \(\sigma(b)=u'\sigma(as')=u'as'=b\), so \(b\in A^G\) and \(\xi=b/(u't)\) with \(u't\in S^G\). ∎

Exercise 13

Problem

In the situation of Exercise 12, let \(\fp\) be a prime ideal of \(A^G\), and let \(P\) be the set of prime ideals of \(A\) whose contraction is \(\fp\). Show that \(G\) acts transitively on \(P\). In particular, \(P\) is finite. [Let \(\fp_1,\fp_2\in P\) and let \(x\in\fp_1\). Then \(\prod_\sigma\sigma(x)\in\fp_1\cap A^G=\fp\subseteq\fp_2\), hence \(\sigma(x)\in\fp_2\) for some \(\sigma\in G\). Solution

\(G\) acts on \(P\): if \(\mathfrak P\cap A^G=\fp\) then \(\sigma(\mathfrak P)\) is a prime of \(A\) and \(\sigma(\mathfrak P)\cap A^G=\sigma(\mathfrak P\cap A^G)=\fp\) since \(\sigma\) fixes \(A^G\) pointwise.

Let \(\fp_1,\fp_2\in P\) and \(x\in\fp_1\). The element \(\prod_\sigma\sigma(x)\) lies in \(\fp_1\) (the factor for \(\sigma=1\) is \(x\)), and is \(G\)-invariant; so it lies in \(\fp_1\cap A^G=\fp\subseteq\fp_2\). As \(\fp_2\) is prime, \(\sigma(x)\in\fp_2\) for some \(\sigma\), i.e. \(x\in\sigma^{-1}(\fp_2)\). Hence \(\fp_1\subseteq\bigcup_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(\fp_2)\), a finite union of prime ideals. Exercise 14

Problem

Let \(A\) be an integrally closed domain, \(K\) its field of fractions and \(L\) a finite normal separable extension of \(K\). Let \(G\) be the Galois group of \(L\) over \(K\) and let \(B\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(L\). Show that \(\sigma(B)=B\) for all \(\sigma\in G\) and that \(A=B^G\).

Solution

Let \(b\in B\) satisfy a monic equation over \(A\); applying \(\sigma\in G\) (which fixes \(K\supseteq A\)) shows that \(\sigma(b)\) satisfies the same monic equation, so \(\sigma(b)\in B\). Thus \(\sigma(B)\subseteq B\), and applying this to \(\sigma^{-1}\) gives \(B\subseteq\sigma(B)\). So \(\sigma(B)=B\).

Since \(L/K\) is Galois, \(L^G=K\). Hence \(B^G=B\cap L^G=B\cap K\). An element of \(B\cap K\) is an element of \(K\) integral over \(A\), so lies in \(A\) (\(A\) integrally closed). Conversely \(A\subseteq B^G\). So \(A=B^G\). ∎

Exercise 15

Problem

Let \(A,K\) be as in Exercise 14, let \(L\) be any finite extension field of \(K\), and let \(B\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(L\). Show that, if \(\fp\) is any prime ideal of \(A\), then the set of prime ideals \(\fq\) of \(B\) which contract to \(\fp\) is finite (in other words, that \(\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) has finite fibers). [Reduce to the two cases (a) \(L\) separable over \(K\) and (b) \(L\) purely inseparable over \(K\). In case (a), embed \(L\) in a finite normal separable extension of \(K\), and use Exercises 13 and 14. In case (b), if \(\fq\) is a prime ideal of \(B\) such that \(\fq\cap A=\fp\), show that \(\fq\) is the set of all \(x\in B\) such that \(x^{p^m}\in\fp\) for some \(m\ge0\), where \(p\) is the characteristic of \(K\), and hence that \(\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) is bijective in this case.]

Solution

Let \(L_s\) be the set of elements of \(L\) separable over \(K\) (a subfield). Then \(L/K\) factors as \(K\subseteq L_s\subseteq L\) with \(L_s/K\) finite separable and \(L/L_s\) purely inseparable (if \(\operatorname{char}K=0\), \(L_s=L\)). Let \(B_s\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(L_s\); then \(B_s=B\cap L_s\), \(A\subseteq B_s\subseteq B\), and \(B_s\) is integrally closed in \(L_s\) (an element of \(L_s\) integral over \(B_s\) is integral over \(A\) by 5.4), with \(\operatorname{Frac}(B_s)=L_s\) (any \(v\in L_s\) satisfies \(a_0v^r+\dots=0\) with \(a_0\ne0\), so \(a_0v\in B_s\)). A prime \(\fq\) of \(B\) lying over \(\fp\) contracts to a prime \(\fq\cap B_s\) of \(B_s\) lying over \(\fp\). So it suffices to show (a): \(\Spec B_s\to\Spec A\) has finite fibres, and (b): \(\Spec B\to\Spec B_s\) has fibres with at most one point.

(a) Let \(M\supseteq L_s\) be a finite normal separable extension of \(K\) (the normal closure of \(L_s\); it is finite and separable), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\) and \(C\) the integral closure of \(A\) in \(M\). By Exercise 14, \(\sigma(C)=C\) for \(\sigma\in G\) and \(C^G=A\), and \(B_s=C\cap L_s\). Given a prime \(\fq\) of \(B_s\) over \(\fp\), by lying over (5.10) for \(B_s\subseteq C\) there is a prime \(\mathfrak Q\) of \(C\) with \(\mathfrak Q\cap B_s=\fq\); then \(\mathfrak Q\cap A=\fp\). By Exercise 13 (applied to \(G\) acting on \(C\), with \(C^G=A\)) the set of primes of \(C\) over \(\fp\) is finite. Since \(\fq\) is obtained from such a \(\mathfrak Q\) by \(\mathfrak Q\mapsto\mathfrak Q\cap B_s\), the fibre of \(\Spec B_s\to\Spec A\) over \(\fp\) is finite.

(b) Here \(\operatorname{char}=p>0\), and every \(x\in L\) has \(x^{p^m}\in L_s\) for some \(m\ge0\). Let \(\fq\) be a prime of \(B\) with \(\fq\cap B_s=\fp_s\) (a prime of \(B_s\)). If \(x\in\fq\), take \(m\) with \(x^{p^m}\in L_s\); it is integral over \(B_s\) (5.3), hence in \(B_s\), and in \(\fq\), so \(x^{p^m}\in\fq\cap B_s=\fp_s\). Conversely, if \(x\in B\) and \(x^{p^m}\in\fp_s\subseteq\fq\) then \(x\in\fq\). So \(\fq=\{x\in B:x^{p^m}\in\fp_s\text{ for some }m\}\) is determined by \(\fp_s\); there is at most one such \(\fq\) (and exactly one by 5.10).

Combining, the fibre of \(\Spec B\to\Spec A\) over \(\fp\) has at most as many points as the (finite) fibre of \(\Spec B_s\to\Spec A\) over \(\fp\). ∎

Exercise 16

Problem

(Noether's normalization lemma.) Let \(k\) be a field and let \(A\ne0\) be a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra. Then there exist elements \(y_1,\dots,y_r\in A\) which are algebraically independent over \(k\) and such that \(A\) is integral over \(k[y_1,\dots,y_r]\).

[Printed text: We shall assume that \(k\) is infinite. (The result is still true if \(k\) is finite, but a different proof is needed.) Let \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) generate \(A\) as a \(k\)-algebra. We can renumber the \(x_i\) so that \(x_1,\dots,x_r\) are algebraically independent and each of \(x_{r+1},\dots,x_n\) is algebraic over \(k[x_1,\dots,x_r]\). Now proceed by induction on \(n\). If \(n=r\) there is nothing to do, so suppose \(n>r\) and the result true for \(n-1\) generators. The generator \(x_n\) is algebraic over \(k[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}]\), hence there exists a polynomial \(f\ne0\) in \(n\) variables such that \(f(x_1,\dots,x_{n-1},x_n)=0\). Let \(F\) be the homogeneous part of highest degree in \(f\). Since \(k\) is infinite, there exist \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1}\in k\) such that \(F(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1},1)\ne0\). Put \(x_i'=x_i-\lambda_ix_n\) (\(1\le i\le n-1\)). Show that \(x_n\) is integral over the ring \(A'=k[x_1',\dots,x_{n-1}']\), and hence that \(A\) is integral over \(A'\). Then apply the inductive hypothesis to \(A'\).

From the proof it follows that \(y_1,\dots,y_r\) may be chosen to be linear combinations of \(x_1,\dots,x_n\). This has the following geometrical interpretation: if \(k\) is algebraically closed and \(X\) is an affine algebraic variety in \(k^n\) with coordinate ring \(A\ne0\), then there exists a linear subspace \(L\) of dimension \(r\) in \(k^n\) and a linear mapping of \(k^n\) onto \(L\) which maps \(X\) onto \(L\). [Use Exercise 2.]]

Solution

The normalization lemma for arbitrary \(k\). Let \(A\ne0\) be generated by \(x_1,\dots,x_n\). Induct on \(n\). If \(n=0\), \(A=k\cdot1\cong k\) (it is a non-zero quotient of the field \(k\)), and we take \(r=0\). Let \(n\ge1\). If \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) are algebraically independent, take \(y_i=x_i\). Otherwise there is a non-zero polynomial \(f\in k[t_1,\dots,t_n]\) with \(f(x_1,\dots,x_n)=0\). Let \(e\) be an integer larger than every exponent occurring in \(f\). For a monomial \(t^\alpha=t_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots t_n^{\alpha_n}\) put \(N(\alpha)=\alpha_n+\alpha_1e+\alpha_2e^2+\dots+\alpha_{n-1}e^{n-1}\); by uniqueness of base-\(e\) expansion, \(N\) is injective on the exponents occurring in \(f\). Substitute \(t_i=t_i'+t_n^{e^i}\) (\(1\le i\le n-1\)): the monomial \(t^\alpha\) becomes \(t_n^{N(\alpha)}+(\text{terms of lower degree in }t_n\text{ with coefficients in }k[t'])\). Hence

\[ f\bigl(t_1'+t_n^{e},\dots,t_{n-1}'+t_n^{e^{n-1}},t_n\bigr)=c\,t_n^{N^*}+\text{(lower terms in }t_n\text{ with coefficients in }k[t_1',\dots,t'_{n-1}]), \]

where \(N^*\) is the largest value of \(N(\alpha)\) and \(c\in k\) is the (non-zero) coefficient of the corresponding monomial of \(f\). Put \(x_i'=x_i-x_n^{e^i}\) and \(A'=k[x_1',\dots,x_{n-1}']\). Evaluating, \(0=f(x)=c\,x_n^{N^*}+\dots\), so after dividing by \(c\), \(x_n\) is integral over \(A'\). Then \(x_i=x_i'+x_n^{e^i}\in A'[x_n]\), so \(A=A'[x_n]\), which is integral over \(A'\) (5.2, 5.3). Now \(A'\ne0\) (it contains \(1\ne0\)) is generated by \(n-1\) elements, so by induction there are \(y_1,\dots,y_r\in A'\) algebraically independent with \(A'\) integral over \(k[y]\); by transitivity (5.4) \(A\) is integral over \(k[y]\). ∎

The linear version when \(k\) is infinite. Suppose \(k\) is infinite and \(f\ne0\) as above, with \(F\) its homogeneous part of highest degree \(d\). The polynomial \(F(t_1,\dots,t_{n-1},1)\in k[t_1,\dots,t_{n-1}]\) is non-zero (dehomogenization of a non-zero homogeneous polynomial is non-zero) and \(k\) is infinite, so there are \(\lambda_i\in k\) with \(F(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1},1)\ne0\). With \(x_i'=x_i-\lambda_ix_n\), the polynomial \(f(t_1'+\lambda_1t_n,\dots,t_{n-1}'+\lambda_{n-1}t_n,t_n)\) equals \(F(\lambda,1)t_n^d+(\text{terms of degree}<d\text{ in }t_n)\), whose leading coefficient is a non-zero element of \(k\). As before \(x_n\) is integral over \(A'=k[x_1',\dots,x_{n-1}']\) and \(A\) is integral over \(A'\). By induction the \(y_i\) can be taken to be \(k\)-linear combinations of \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) (each \(x_i'\) is one).

Geometric interpretation. Let \(k\) be algebraically closed (so infinite), \(X\subseteq k^n\) an affine variety with coordinate ring \(A=k[t_1,\dots,t_n]/I(X)\ne0\) generated by the coordinate functions \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n\). By the above, there are \(y_i=\sum_jc_{ij}\xi_j\) (\(1\le i\le r\)) algebraically independent with \(A\) integral over \(k[y_1,\dots,y_r]\). Let \(\pi:k^n\to k^r\), \(\pi(x)=(\ell_1(x),\dots,\ell_r(x))\) with \(\ell_i=\sum_jc_{ij}t_j\). The \(\ell_i\) are linearly independent forms (a linear relation among them would give an algebraic relation among the \(y_i\)), so \(\pi\) is a surjective linear map. We claim \(\pi(X)=k^r\). Let \(a\in k^r\). The homomorphism \(k[y_1,\dots,y_r]\to k\), \(y_i\mapsto a_i\), extends to a homomorphism \(g:A\to k\) by Exercise 2 (applied to the subring \(k[y]\subseteq A\) over which \(A\) is integral, with \(\Omega=k\)). It restricts to the identity on \(k\). Put \(x_0=(g(\xi_1),\dots,g(\xi_n))\). For a polynomial \(h\in I(X)\), \(h(x_0)=g(h(\xi))=g(0)=0\); so \(x_0\in X\) (as \(X\) is defined by equations lying in \(I(X)\)). And \(\ell_i(x_0)=g(y_i)=a_i\), so \(\pi(x_0)=a\). Composing \(\pi\) with an embedding \(\iota:k^r\to k^n\) identifies the image with a linear subspace \(L=\iota(k^r)\) of dimension \(r\) and gives a linear map \(k^n\to L\) mapping \(X\) onto \(L\). ∎

Problem

(Nullstellensatz, weak form.) For \(h\in\fa\), \(h(x_0)=g(h(\xi))=g(0)=0\); so \(x_0\in V(\fa)\), which is therefore non-empty.

Problem

Let \(k\) be a field and let \(B\) be a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra. Suppose that \(B\) is a field. Then \(B\) is a finite algebraic extension of \(k\). (This is another version of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz. The following proof is due to Zariski. For other proofs, see (5.24), (7.9).)

[Let \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) generate \(B\) as a \(k\)-algebra. The proof is by induction on \(n\). If \(n=1\) the result is clearly true, so assume \(n>1\). Suppose \(x_1\) is transcendental over \(k\). Then \(A\) is integrally closed, because it is a unique factorization domain. Hence \(A_f\) is integrally closed (5.12), and therefore \(A_f=K\), which is clearly absurd. Hence \(x_1\) is algebraic over \(k\), hence \(K\) (and therefore \(B\)) is a finite extension of \(k\).]

Solution

Induct on the number \(n\) of generators \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) of the \(k\)-algebra \(B\), which is a field.

\(n=1\). \(B=k[x_1]\) is a field. If \(x_1\) were transcendental, \(B\) would be a polynomial ring, not a field. So \(x_1\) is algebraic and \(B=k[x_1]\) is a finite extension of \(k\).

\(n>1\). Let \(A=k[x_1]\), \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)=k(x_1)\subseteq B\) (as \(B\) is a field). \(B\) is generated over \(K\) by \(x_2,\dots,x_n\), \(n-1\) elements; by the inductive hypothesis \(B\) is a finite algebraic extension of \(K\). So each \(x_i\) (\(i\ge2\)) satisfies a monic polynomial over \(K\), whose coefficients are quotients of elements of \(A\). Let \(f\in A-\{0\}\) be the product of all the denominators; then \(x_2,\dots,x_n\) are integral over \(A_f\). Let \(v\in B\), \(v\ne0\). Then \(B_v\) is a domain containing \(A\), finitely generated as an \(A\)-algebra (by the generators of \(B\) and \(1/v\)). By Exercise 21 applied to \(A\subseteq B_v\) there is \(s\ne0\) in \(A\) with the stated extension property. Since \(\Jac(A)=0\) and \(s\ne0\), \(s\) is not in some maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\). Let \(k=A/\fm\), \(\Omega\) an algebraic closure of \(k\), and \(f:A\to k\to\Omega\) the canonical map; \(f(s)\ne0\), and \(\Ker f=\fm\). By Exercise 21, \(f\) extends to \(g:B_v\to\Omega\). Since \(v\) is a unit of \(B_v\), \(g(v)g(v^{-1})=1\), so \(g(v)\ne0\).

Let \(g_0=g|_B\) and \(\fn=\Ker g_0\). The image \(g_0(B)\) is a subring of \(\Omega\) containing \(g(A)=k\). It is generated as a \(k\)-algebra by the images of generators of \(B\) over \(A\), which are algebraic over \(k\) (all elements of \(\Omega\) are), hence integral over the field \(k\). So \(g_0(B)\supseteq k\) is an integral domain integral over the field \(k\), hence a field (5.7). Thus \(B/\fn\cong g_0(B)\) is a field and \(\fn\) is a maximal ideal of \(B\); and \(v\notin\fn\) since \(g(v)\ne0\). As \(v\ne0\) was arbitrary, \(\Jac(B)=0\). ∎

Exercise 23

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring. Show that the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    Every prime ideal in \(A\) is an intersection of maximal ideals.

  2. (ii)

    In every homomorphic image of \(A\) the nilradical is equal to the Jacobson radical.

  3. (iii)

    Every prime ideal in \(A\) which is not maximal is equal to the intersection of the prime ideals which contain it strictly.

[The only hard part is iii)\(\Rightarrow\)ii). Suppose ii) false; then there is a prime ideal which is not an intersection of maximal ideals. Passing to the quotient ring, we may assume that \(A\) is an integral domain whose Jacobson radical \(\Jac\) is not zero. Let \(f\) be a non-zero element of \(\Jac\). Then \(A_f\ne0\), hence \(A_f\) has a maximal ideal, whose contraction in \(A\) is a prime ideal \(\fp\) such that \(f\notin\fp\), and which is maximal with respect to this property. Then \(\fp\) is not maximal and is not equal to the intersection of the prime ideals strictly containing \(\fp\).]

A ring \(A\) with the three equivalent properties above is called a Jacobson ring.

Solution

Recall that the primes of a quotient \(A/\fa\) correspond to the primes of \(A\) containing \(\fa\), maximal ones to maximal ones, and \(\Nil\subseteq\Jac\) always.

(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). Property (i) passes to quotients: a prime of \(A/\fa\) is \(\fp/\fa\) with \(\fp\supseteq\fa\) prime in \(A\), \(\fp=\bigcap\fm_i\) with \(\fm_i\supseteq\fp\supseteq\fa\) maximal, so \(\fp/\fa=\bigcap(\fm_i/\fa)\). So it suffices to show \(\Nil=\Jac\) in \(A\) itself. Each prime of \(A\) is an intersection of maximal ideals, hence contains \(\Jac\); so \(\Nil=\bigcap\fp\supseteq\Jac\) and \(\Nil=\Jac\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). Let \(\fp\) be prime. In \(A/\fp\), \(\Jac=\Nil=0\) (a domain). So \(\fp=\bigcap\) of the maximal ideals containing \(\fp\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii). Let \(\fp\) be a prime which is not maximal and \(f\in A\) lie in every prime strictly containing \(\fp\). In the domain \(D=A/\fp\), which is not a field, every maximal ideal is non-zero, hence corresponds to a prime strictly containing \(\fp\), so the image \(\bar f\) lies in all maximal ideals of \(D\): \(\bar f\in\Jac(D)=\Nil(D)=0\) by (ii). So \(f\in\fp\). Hence \(\fp\) is the intersection of the primes strictly containing it (and there is at least one, a maximal ideal).

(iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). Suppose (ii) fails: there is an ideal \(\fa\) and \(x\in\Jac(A/\fa)\) not nilpotent. Then some prime of \(A/\fa\) does not contain \(x\); passing to the quotient by it, we get a domain \(D=A/\fp_0\) and a non-zero element \(\bar x\in\Jac(D)\) (the image of \(\Jac(R)\) in a quotient \(R/I\) lies in \(\Jac(R/I)\), because maximal ideals of \(R/I\) pull back to maximal ideals of \(R\)). Take \(f\ne0\) in \(\Jac(D)\); \(D_f\ne0\) has a maximal ideal, whose contraction \(\fp\) in \(D\) is a prime with \(f\notin\fp\), maximal with respect to this property (primes of \(D_f\) correspond to primes of \(D\) not containing \(f\)). \(\fp\) is not maximal (a maximal ideal of \(D\) contains \(f\in\Jac(D)\)), and every prime strictly containing \(\fp\) contains \(f\) (by the maximality), so the intersection of the primes strictly containing \(\fp\) contains \(f\notin\fp\) and differs from \(\fp\). Pulling \(\fp\) back to \(A\) gives a prime of \(A\) violating (iii) (primes of \(D\) correspond to primes of \(A\) containing \(\fp_0\), preserving inclusions). ∎

Exercise 24

Problem

Let \(A\) be a Jacobson ring (Exercise 23) and \(B\) an \(A\)-algebra. Show that if \(B\) is either (i) integral over \(A\) or (ii) finitely generated as an \(A\)-algebra, then \(B\) is a Jacobson ring. [Use Exercise 22 for (ii).]

In particular, every finitely generated ring, and every finitely generated algebra over a field, is a Jacobson ring.

Solution

Let \(f:A\to B\) be the structure map and let \(\fq\) be a prime of \(B\), \(\fp=\fq^c\). By Exercise 23(i)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(ii) it suffices to show that \(\fq\) is an intersection of maximal ideals, i.e. that \(\Jac(B/\fq)=0\). Now \(A'=A/\fp\hookrightarrow B/\fq=B'\) (the kernel of \(A\to B/\fq\) is \(\fq^c=\fp\)), and \(A'\) is a Jacobson domain (a quotient of a Jacobson ring is Jacobson, see the proof of (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii) in Exercise 23), so \(0\) is an intersection of maximal ideals of \(A'\), i.e. \(\Jac(A')=0\).

(i) If \(B\) is integral over \(A\), then \(B'\) is integral over \(A'\) (5.6), and \(\Jac(A')=\Jac(B')\cap A'\) (Exercise 5(ii)). Hence \(\Jac(B')\cap A'=0\). Now \(B'\) is a domain integral over the domain \(A'\); suppose \(\Jac(B')\ne0\) and take a non-zero \(b\in\Jac(B')\); it satisfies a monic equation \(b^m+a_1b^{m-1}+\dots+a_m=0\) of least degree; then \(a_m\ne0\) (else we divide by \(b\) in the domain and reduce the degree), and \(a_m=-b(b^{m-1}+\dots+a_{m-1})\in\Jac(B')\cap A'\) (as \(\Jac(B')\) is an ideal). This contradicts \(\Jac(B')\cap A'=0\). So \(\Jac(B')=0\).

(ii) If \(B\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-algebra, \(B'\) is a domain finitely generated over \(A'\subseteq B'\) with \(\Jac(A')=0\). By Exercise 22, \(\Jac(B')=0\).

In both cases every prime of \(B\) is an intersection of maximal ideals, so \(B\) is Jacobson. Finally \(\Z\) is a Jacobson ring (its primes are \((0)\), which is the intersection of the infinitely many maximal ideals \((p)\), and the maximal ideals \((p)\)), and a field is Jacobson (its only prime is maximal). A finitely generated ring is a finitely generated \(\Z\)-algebra, and a finitely generated algebra over a field is a finitely generated algebra over that field, so both are Jacobson by (ii). ∎

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring. Show that the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    \(A\) is a Jacobson ring.

  2. (ii)

    Every finitely generated \(A\)-algebra \(B\) which is a field is finite over \(A\).

[(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). Reduce to the case where \(A\) is a subring of \(B\), and use Exercise 21. If \(s\in A\) is as in Exercise 21, then there exists a maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\) not containing \(s\), and the homomorphism \(A\to A/\fm=k\) extends to a homomorphism \(g\) of \(B\) into the algebraic closure of \(k\). Since \(B\) is a field, \(g\) is injective, and \(g(B)\) is algebraic over \(k\), hence finite over \(k\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). Use criterion iii) of Exercise 23. Let \(\fp\) be a prime ideal of \(A\) which is not maximal, and let \(B=A/\fp\). Let \(f\) be a non-zero element of \(B\). Then \(B_f\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-algebra. If it is a field it is finite over \(A\) (by ii)) hence integral over \(B\) and therefore \(B\) is a field by (5.7). Hence \(B_f\) is not a field and therefore has a non-zero prime ideal, whose contraction in \(B\) is a non-zero ideal \(\fp'\) such that \(f\notin\fp'\).]

Solution

(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). Let \(B\) be a finitely generated \(A\)-algebra which is a field, \(\fp=\Ker(A\to B)\), a prime ideal. Then \(A'=A/\fp\subseteq B\) is a Jacobson domain (a quotient of a Jacobson ring), so \(\Jac(A')=0\); and \(B\) is a finitely generated \(A'\)-algebra, and finiteness over \(A\) is finiteness over \(A'\). Apply Exercise 21 to \(A'\subseteq B\): we get \(s\ne0\) in \(A'\). Since \(\Jac(A')=0\) there is a maximal ideal \(\fm'\) of \(A'\) with \(s\notin\fm'\). Let \(k=A'/\fm'\), \(\Omega\) an algebraic closure of \(k\), and \(f:A'\to k\to\Omega\) the canonical map; \(f(s)\ne0\). By Exercise 21, \(f\) extends to \(g:B\to\Omega\). As \(B\) is a field, \(g\) is injective (\(g(1)=1\ne0\)). Then \(g|_{A'}\) is injective, but its kernel is \(\fm'\), so \(\fm'=0\) and \(A'=k\) is a field. Now \(g(B)\) is generated over \(g(A')=k\) by finitely many elements of \(\Omega\), which are algebraic over \(k\), so \(g(B)\) is a finite extension of \(k\). Therefore \(B\cong g(B)\) is finite over \(A'\), hence finite over \(A\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). We verify Exercise 23(iii). Let \(\fp\) be a prime of \(A\) which is not maximal, \(B=A/\fp\), and \(0\ne f\in B\). \(B_f=B[1/f]\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-algebra. If \(B_f\) were a field, then by (ii) it would be finite over \(A\), hence integral over the image \(B=A/\fp\) of \(A\) in it (note \(B\subseteq B_f\) as \(B\) is a domain); then \(B\) would be a field by 5.7, contradicting that \(\fp\) is not maximal. So the domain \(B_f\) is not a field, and has a non-zero maximal ideal \(\mathfrak Q\). Let \(\fp'=\mathfrak Q\cap B\), a prime of \(B\). Then \(f\notin\fp'\) (as \(f\) is a unit of \(B_f\)), and \(\fp'\ne0\): a non-zero element \(b/f^k\in\mathfrak Q\) gives \(b\in\mathfrak Q\cap B\), \(b\ne0\). So the prime of \(A\) corresponding to \(\fp'\) strictly contains \(\fp\) and does not contain (a lift of) \(f\). As \(f\in B-\{0\}\) was arbitrary, \(\fp\) equals the intersection of the primes strictly containing it. Hence \(A\) is Jacobson by Exercise 23. ∎

Exercise 26

Problem

Let \(X\) be a topological space. A subset of \(X\) is locally closed if it is the intersection of an open set and a closed set, or equivalently if it is open in its closure.

The following conditions on a subset \(X_0\) of \(X\) are equivalent:

  1. (1)

    Every non-empty locally closed subset of \(X\) meets \(X_0\);

  2. (2)

    For every closed set \(E\) in \(X\) we have \(\overline{E\cap X_0}=E\);

  3. (3)

    The mapping \(U\mapsto U\cap X_0\) of the collection of open sets of \(X\) onto the collection of open sets of \(X_0\) is bijective.

A subset \(X_0\) satisfying these conditions is said to be very dense in \(X\).

If \(A\) is a ring, show that the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    \(A\) is a Jacobson ring;

  2. (ii)

    The set of maximal ideals of \(A\) is very dense in \(\Spec(A)\);

  3. (iii)

    Every locally closed subset of \(\Spec(A)\) consisting of a single point is closed.

[(ii) and (iii) are geometrical formulations of conditions ii) and iii) of Exercise 23.]

Solution

The three conditions on \(X_0\) are equivalent.

(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2): Let \(E\) be closed and \(F=\overline{E\cap X_0}\subseteq E\). If \(F\ne E\), then \(E-F=E\cap(X-F)\) is a non-empty locally closed set, so by (1) it contains a point of \(X_0\). This point lies in \(E\cap X_0\subseteq F\), a contradiction.

(2)\(\Rightarrow\)(3): The map is onto the open sets of \(X_0\) by the definition of the subspace topology. If \(U\cap X_0=U'\cap X_0\), put \(E=X-U\), \(E'=X-U'\); then \(E\cap X_0=X_0-U\cap X_0=E'\cap X_0\), so by (2) \(E=\overline{E\cap X_0}=\overline{E'\cap X_0}=E'\), and \(U=U'\).

(3)\(\Rightarrow\)(1): Let \(L=U\cap E\ne\emptyset\) (\(U\) open, \(E\) closed) with \(L\cap X_0=\emptyset\). Then \(U\cap X_0=(U\cap(X-E))\cap X_0\), and \(U\cap(X-E)\) is open, so by injectivity in (3) \(U=U-E\), i.e. \(U\cap E=\emptyset\), contradicting \(L\ne\emptyset\).

Spectra. Let \(X=\Spec A\), \(X_0=\operatorname{Max}A\). Recall that the closure of \(S\subseteq X\) is \(V(\bigcap_{\fp\in S}\fp)\).

(i)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(ii). Condition (2) for \(X_0\) and the closed set \(E=V(\fa)\) says \(V\bigl(\bigcap_{\fm\supseteq\fa}\fm\bigr)=V(\fa)\), which holds iff \(\bigcap_{\fm\supseteq\fa}\fm=r(\fa)\) (the left side is a radical ideal, and \(V(\fb)=V(\fa)\) iff \(r(\fb)=r(\fa)\), Ch. 1, Ex. 15). Writing this in \(A/\fa\), it says \(\Jac(A/\fa)=\Nil(A/\fa)\). So (ii) holds iff this is true for all ideals \(\fa\), i.e. iff Exercise 23(ii) holds, i.e. iff \(A\) is Jacobson.

(i)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii). Let \(\{\fp\}=U\cap V(\fa)\) be locally closed (\(U\) open). Since \(U\ni\fp\), \(U\supseteq X_f\) for some \(f\notin\fp\) (basic opens, Ch. 1, Ex. 17), and then \(\{\fp\}=X_f\cap V(\fa)\) (it contains \(\fp\) and is contained in \(U\cap V(\fa)\)). Suppose \(\fp\) is not maximal. Every prime strictly containing \(\fp\) lies in \(V(\fa)\) (as \(\fa\subseteq\fp\)) and is not in \(X_f\) (else it would be an element of \(\{\fp\}\)), so it contains \(f\). Then \(f\in\bigcap\{\fp'\supsetneq\fp\}=\fp\) by Exercise 23(iii), a contradiction. So \(\fp\) is maximal, and \(\{\fp\}\) is closed.

(iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). We verify Exercise 23(iii). Let \(\fp\) be non-maximal and suppose some \(f\notin\fp\) lies in every prime strictly containing \(\fp\). Then \(V(\fp)\cap X_f=\{\fp\}\), a locally closed single point, which is closed by (iii); so \(\fp\) is maximal, contradiction. Hence \(\fp\) is the intersection of the primes strictly containing it. ∎

Problem

Let \(A,B\) be two local rings. \(B\) is said to dominate \(A\) if \(A\) is a subring of \(B\) and the maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\) is contained in the maximal ideal \(\fn\) of \(B\) (or, equivalently, if \(\fm=\fn\cap A\)). Let \(K\) be a field and let \(\Sigma\) be the set of all local subrings of \(K\). If \(\Sigma\) is ordered by the relation of domination, show that \(\Sigma\) has maximal elements and that \(A\in\Sigma\) is maximal if and only if \(A\) is a valuation ring of \(K\). [Use (5.21).]

Solution

Domination is a partial order on \(\Sigma\) (reflexive and transitive clearly; if \(A\) dominates \(B\) and \(B\) dominates \(A\) then \(A=B\)). (\(\Sigma\ni K\) is non-empty.)

Existence of maximal elements. Let \((A_\alpha)\) be a chain in \(\Sigma\), \(C=\bigcup A_\alpha\) (a subring of \(K\)), and \(\fm_C=\bigcup\fm_\alpha\). If \(A_\beta\) dominates \(A_\alpha\) then \(\fm_\beta\cap A_\alpha=\fm_\alpha\), so \(\fm_\alpha\subseteq\fm_\beta\) along the chain and \(\fm_C\) is an ideal of \(C\); it is proper (\(1\notin\fm_\alpha\) for all \(\alpha\)). An element \(x\in C-\fm_C\) lies in some \(A_\alpha\) and not in \(\fm_\alpha\), hence is a unit of \(A_\alpha\subseteq C\). So \(C\) is local with maximal ideal \(\fm_C\); and \(\fm_C\cap A_\alpha=\fm_\alpha\) (an element of \(A_\alpha\) in \(\fm_\beta\), \(\beta\ge\alpha\), lies in \(\fm_\beta\cap A_\alpha=\fm_\alpha\)). So \(C\) dominates each \(A_\alpha\). By Zorn's lemma \(\Sigma\) has maximal elements.

Maximal \(\Rightarrow\) valuation ring. Let \(A\in\Sigma\) be maximal, \(\fm\) its maximal ideal, \(\Omega\) an algebraic closure of \(A/\fm\) and \(g:A\to A/\fm\to\Omega\), so \(\Ker g=\fm\). We show \((A,g)\) is maximal in the set of pairs of 5.21. Let \((B,h)\) be a pair with \(A\subseteq B\subseteq K\) and \(h|_A=g\). The kernel \(\mathfrak P=\Ker h\) is a prime ideal of \(B\) (the image lies in a field), \(0\in\mathfrak P\), so \(B'=B_\mathfrak P\) is a local subring of \(K\) with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak P B'\), and \(\mathfrak P\cap A=\Ker g=\fm\). So \(\fm=\mathfrak P B'\cap A\subseteq\mathfrak P B'\), i.e. \(B'\) dominates \(A\). By maximality of \(A\), \(B'=A\), so \(B\subseteq B'=A\), whence \(B=A\) and \(h=g\). So \((A,g)\) is a maximal pair and, by Theorem 5.21, \(A\) is a valuation ring of \(K\).

Valuation ring \(\Rightarrow\) maximal. Let \(A\) be a valuation ring of \(K\) and let \(B\in\Sigma\) dominate \(A\), with maximal ideals \(\fm_A\subseteq\fm_B\). Let \(x\in B\). If \(x\notin A\) then \(x^{-1}\in A\) (valuation ring), and \(x^{-1}\) is not a unit of \(A\) (else \(x\in A\)), so \(x^{-1}\in\fm_A\subseteq\fm_B\); then \(1=x\cdot x^{-1}\in\fm_B\) (as \(x\in B\)), absurd. So \(B\subseteq A\), and since \(A\subseteq B\), \(A=B\). Hence \(A\) is maximal. ∎

If \(\fp\) is a prime ideal of \(A\), what are the value groups of the valuation rings \(A/\fp\), \(A_\fp\)?

Solution

Write \(\Gamma\) additively with \(v:K^*\to\Gamma\) surjective (Exercise 30 in additive notation) and \(A=\{0\}\cup\{x:v(x)\ge0\}\). Note: if \(x,y\in A-\{0\}\) and \(v(x)\le v(y)\), then \(y/x\in A\), so \(y\in xA\).

The subgroup \(\Delta_\fp\). Let \(\fp\) be prime, \(T=v(A-\fp)=\{v(x):x\in A-\fp\}\subseteq\Gamma_{\ge0}\) (note \(0\in\fp\), so only \(x\ne0\) occur). \(0=v(1)\in T\); \(T\) is closed under \(+\) since \(A-\fp\) is multiplicatively closed. \(T\) is an initial segment of \(\Gamma_{\ge0}\): if \(0\le\beta\le\alpha=v(x)\), \(x\in A-\fp\), and \(\beta=v(y)\) (\(y\in A\) since \(\beta\ge0\)), then \(v(y)\le v(x)\) gives \(x\in yA\); if \(y\in\fp\) we would have \(x\in\fp\); so \(y\in A-\fp\) and \(\beta\in T\). Put \(\Delta_\fp=T\cup(-T)\). It is a subgroup: \(-\Delta=\Delta\), and for \(\alpha,\beta\in T\), \(\alpha-\beta\in[0,\alpha]\subseteq T\) if \(\alpha\ge\beta\), and \(\alpha-\beta\in-[0,\beta]\subseteq-T\) if \(\alpha<\beta\). It is isolated: if \(0\le\beta\le\alpha\in\Delta_\fp\) then \(\alpha\in T\) (as \(\alpha\ge0\) and \(\Delta_\fp\cap\Gamma_{\ge0}=T\)), hence \(\beta\in T\). And \(\Delta_\fp\cap\Gamma_{\ge0}=T=v(A-\fp)\), as claimed.

Bijectivity. The map is \(\fp\mapsto\Delta_\fp\). If \(x\in A-\{0\}\) then \(x\notin\fp\iff v(x)\in\Delta_\fp\) (“\(\Rightarrow\)” by definition; “\(\Leftarrow\)”: \(v(x)=v(y)\) with \(y\in A-\fp\) gives \(x/y\in A^\times\), so \(x\in y A^\times\not\subseteq\fp\)). So \(\fp=\{0\}\cup\{x\in A-\{0\}:v(x)\notin\Delta_\fp\}\), whence the map is injective. For surjectivity let \(\Delta\) be an isolated subgroup and put \(\fp_\Delta=\{0\}\cup\{x\in A-\{0\}:v(x)\notin\Delta\}\). It is an ideal: if \(x\in\fp_\Delta\) and \(a\in A\), \(a\ne0\), then \(0\le v(x)\le v(ax)\), and \(v(ax)\in\Delta\) would give \(v(x)\in\Delta\) (isolated); and if \(x,y\in\fp_\Delta\) with \(x+y\ne0\) and, say, \(v(x)\le v(y)\), then \(v(x+y)\ge v(x)\), so \(v(x+y)\in\Delta\) would give \(v(x)\in\Delta\). It is proper (\(v(1)=0\in\Delta\)) and prime: if \(x,y\in A-\fp_\Delta\) then \(v(x),v(y)\in\Delta\), so \(v(xy)\in\Delta\), so \(xy\notin\fp_\Delta\). Finally \(v(A-\fp_\Delta)=\{\alpha\in\Delta:\alpha\ge0\}\) (every such \(\alpha\) is \(v(x)\) for some \(x\in A\) since \(v\) is onto \(\Gamma\)), whence \(\Delta_{\fp_\Delta}=\Delta\). Thus \(\fp\mapsto\Delta_\fp\) is a bijection from \(\Spec A\) onto the set of isolated subgroups, with inverse \(\Delta\mapsto\fp_\Delta\); it reverses inclusions (\(\fp\subseteq\fq\iff\Delta_\fq\subseteq\Delta_\fp\)).

Value groups of \(A/\fp\) and \(A_\fp\).

\(A_\fp\). \(A_\fp=\{a/s:a\in A,s\in A-\fp\}=\{0\}\cup\{x\in K^*:v(x)\ge-\delta\text{ for some }\delta\in T\}\). Its units are the \(x\) with \(x\) and \(x^{-1}\in A_\fp\), i.e. \(-\delta\le v(x)\le\delta'\) with \(\delta,\delta'\in T\), i.e. (by isolation) \(v(x)\in\Delta_\fp\). So \(U(A_\fp)=v^{-1}(\Delta_\fp)\) and the value group of \(A_\fp\) is \(K^*/v^{-1}(\Delta_\fp)\cong\Gamma/\Delta_\fp\), with the order \(\gamma+\Delta_\fp\ge0\) iff \(\gamma\ge\delta\) for some \(\delta\in\Delta_\fp\) (well defined because \(\Delta_\fp\) is isolated).

\(A/\fp\). The fraction field of \(A/\fp\) is the residue field \(k(\fp)=A_\fp/\fp A_\fp\) (Remark after 3.13). Now \(\fp A_\fp=\{x:v(x)>\Delta_\fp\}\) (greater than every element of \(\Delta_\fp\)), and the units of \(A_\fp\) are \(\{x:v(x)\in\Delta_\fp\}\), so \(v\) induces a map \(w:k(\fp)^*\to\Delta_\fp\), \(w(\bar x)=v(x)\) for \(x\) a unit of \(A_\fp\); it is well defined because \(\bar x=\bar x'\) gives \(x'/x\in1+\fp A_\fp\), which has value \(0\). It is a valuation (properties (1),(2) of Exercise 31 follow from those of \(v\)), surjective onto \(\Delta_\fp\), and its valuation ring \(\{\bar x:w(\bar x)\ge0\}\cup\{0\}\) is the image of \(A\), i.e. \(A/\fp\). Hence the value group of \(A/\fp\) is \(\Delta_\fp\) (a subgroup of \(\Gamma\)), and the value group of \(A_\fp\) is \(\Gamma/\Delta_\fp\). ∎

Exercise 33

Problem

Let \(\Gamma\) be a totally ordered abelian group. We shall show how to construct a field \(K\) and a valuation \(v\) of \(K\) with \(\Gamma\) as value group. Let \(k\) be any field and let \(A=k[\Gamma]\) be the group algebra of \(\Gamma\) over \(k\). By definition, \(A\) is freely generated as a \(k\)-vector space by elements \(x_\alpha\) (\(\alpha\in\Gamma\)) such that \(x_\alpha x_\beta=x_{\alpha+\beta}\). Show that \(A\) is an integral domain.

If \(u=\lambda_1x_{\alpha_1}+\dots+\lambda_nx_{\alpha_n}\) is any non-zero element of \(A\), where the \(\lambda_i\) are all \(\ne0\) and \(\alpha_1<\dots<\alpha_n\), define \(v_0(u)\) to be \(\alpha_1\). Show that the mapping \(v_0:A-\{0\}\to\Gamma\) satisfies conditions (1) and (2) of Exercise 31.

Let \(K\) be the field of fractions of \(A\). Show that \(v_0\) can be uniquely extended to a valuation \(v\) of \(K\), and that the value group of \(v\) is precisely \(\Gamma\).

Solution

Let \(u=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_ix_{\alpha_i}\), \(w=\sum_{j=1}^m\mu_jx_{\beta_j}\) be non-zero, with non-zero coefficients and \(\alpha_1<\dots<\alpha_n\), \(\beta_1<\dots<\beta_m\).

Domain and (1). In \(uw=\sum\lambda_i\mu_jx_{\alpha_i+\beta_j}\), a pair \((i,j)\ne(1,1)\) has \(\alpha_i+\beta_j>\alpha_1+\beta_1\) (strict, since \(\alpha_i\ge\alpha_1\), \(\beta_j\ge\beta_1\) with one strict, and the order is compatible with addition). So the coefficient of \(x_{\alpha_1+\beta_1}\) in \(uw\) is exactly \(\lambda_1\mu_1\ne0\), all other terms having larger exponent. Hence \(uw\ne0\) and \(v_0(uw)=\alpha_1+\beta_1=v_0(u)+v_0(w)\). So \(A\) is an integral domain and \(v_0\) satisfies (1).

(2). If \(u+w\ne0\), its support is contained in \(\{\alpha_i\}\cup\{\beta_j\}\), so its smallest exponent is \(\ge\min(\alpha_1,\beta_1)\): \(v_0(u+w)\ge\min(v_0(u),v_0(w))\).

Extension. Define \(v(a/c)=v_0(a)-v_0(c)\) for \(a,c\in A-\{0\}\). It is well defined: if \(a/c=a'/c'\) then \(ac'=a'c\), so \(v_0(a)+v_0(c')=v_0(a')+v_0(c)\) by (1). Property (1) for \(v\) is clear. For (2): \(a/c+b/d=(ad+bc)/cd\), so if the sum is \(\ne0\),

\[ v\Bigl(\tfrac ac+\tfrac bd\Bigr)=v_0(ad+bc)-v_0(c)-v_0(d)\ge\min\bigl(v_0(a)+v_0(d),\,v_0(b)+v_0(c)\bigr)-v_0(c)-v_0(d)=\min\bigl(v(\tfrac ac),v(\tfrac bd)\bigr). \]

Uniqueness. Any valuation \(v'\) of \(K\) extending \(v_0\) satisfies \(v'(a/c)=v'(a)-v'(c)=v_0(a)-v_0(c)\) by (1), so \(v'=v\).

Value group. \(v(x_\alpha)=\alpha\), so \(v(K^*)\supseteq\Gamma\); and every \(v(a/c)=v_0(a)-v_0(c)\in\Gamma\). Hence \(v(K^*)=\Gamma\). ∎

Exercise 34

Problem

Let \(A\) be a valuation ring and \(K\) its field of fractions. Let \(f:A\to B\) be a ring homomorphism such that \(f^*:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)\) is a closed mapping. Then if \(g:B\to K\) is any \(A\)-algebra homomorphism (i.e., if \(g\circ f\) is the embedding of \(A\) in \(K\)) we have \(g(B)=A\). [Let \(C=g(B)\); obviously \(C\supseteq A\). Let \(\fn\) be a maximal ideal of \(C\). Since \(f^*\) is closed, \(\fm=\fn\cap A\) is the maximal ideal of \(A\), whence \(A_\fm=A\). Also the local ring \(C_\fn\) dominates \(A_\fm\). Hence by Exercise 27 we have \(C_\fn=A\) and therefore \(C\subseteq A\).]

Solution

Let \(C=g(B)\); since \(gf\) is the inclusion, \(A\subseteq C\subseteq K\). By Exercise 29, \(C\) is a local ring (indeed a local ring of \(A\)); let \(\fn\) be its unique maximal ideal. Then \(\mathfrak N=g^{-1}(\fn)\) is a prime of \(B\), in fact maximal (\(B/\mathfrak N\cong C/\fn\)), so \(\{\mathfrak N\}\) is closed in \(\Spec B\). As \(f^*\) is closed, \(f^*(\{\mathfrak N\})=\{f^{-1}(\mathfrak N)\}=\{(gf)^{-1}(\fn)\}=\{\fn\cap A\}\) is closed in \(\Spec A\), i.e. \(\fm=\fn\cap A\) is a maximal ideal of \(A\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 18(i)). Since \(A\) is local, \(\fm\) is its maximal ideal and \(A_\fm=A\). Now \(C=C_\fn\) (local) contains \(A\) and \(\fn\cap A=\fm\), i.e. \(C\) dominates \(A\). By Exercise 27, a valuation ring is maximal for domination, so \(C=A\). Hence \(g(B)=A\). ∎

Exercise 35

Problem

From Exercises 1 and 3 it follows that, if \(f:A\to B\) is integral and \(C\) is any \(A\)-algebra, then the mapping \((f\otimes1)^*:\Spec(B\otimes_AC)\to\Spec(C)\) is a closed map.

Conversely, suppose that \(f:A\to B\) has this property and that \(B\) is an integral domain. Then \(f\) is integral. [Replacing \(A\) by its image in \(B\), reduce to the case where \(A\subseteq B\) and \(f\) is the injection. Let \(K\) be the field of fractions of \(B\) and let \(A'\) be a valuation ring of \(K\) containing \(A\). By (5.22) it is enough to show that \(A'\) contains \(B\). By hypothesis \(\Spec(B\otimes_AA')\to\Spec(A')\) is a closed map. Apply the result of Exercise 34 to the homomorphism \(B\otimes_AA'\to K\) defined by \(b\otimes a'\mapsto ba'\). It follows that \(ba'\in A'\) for all \(b\in B\) and all \(a'\in A'\); taking \(a'=1\), we have what we want.]

Show that the result just proved remains valid if \(B\) is a ring with only finitely many minimal prime ideals (e.g., if \(B\) is Noetherian). [Let \(\fp_i\) be the minimal prime ideals. Then each composite homomorphism \(A\to B\to B/\fp_i\) is integral, hence \(A\to\prod(B/\fp_i)\) is integral, hence \(A\to B/\Nil\) is integral (where \(\Nil\) is the nilradical of \(B\)), hence finally \(A\to B\) is integral.]

Solution

The direct statement. The ring homomorphism \(C\to B\otimes_AC\), \(c\mapsto1\otimes c\), is the base change of \(f\). Apply Exercise 3 to the integral homomorphism \(f:A\to B\) of \(A\)-algebras and the \(A\)-algebra \(C\) (with \(A\) itself in the role of the first algebra): \(f\otimes1:A\otimes_AC=C\to B\otimes_AC\) is integral. By Exercise 1, \((f\otimes1)^*:\Spec(B\otimes_AC)\to\Spec(C)\) is closed.

The converse for \(B\) a domain. Suppose \(f:A\to B\) has the property: for every \(A\)-algebra \(C\), \(\Spec(B\otimes_AC)\to\Spec C\) is closed. Let \(A_1=f(A)\cong A/\Ker f\). The property is inherited by \(A_1\subseteq B\): an \(A_1\)-algebra \(C\) is an \(A\)-algebra and \(B\otimes_{A_1}C=B\otimes_AC\) (since \(A\to A_1\) is onto). And \(f\) integral means \(B\) integral over \(A_1\). So we may assume \(A\subseteq B\).

Let \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(B)\) and let \(A'\) be any valuation ring of \(K\) containing \(A\). Regard \(A'\) as an \(A\)-algebra. By hypothesis the map \(\phi^*:\Spec(B\otimes_AA')\to\Spec(A')\) associated with \(\phi:A'\to B\otimes_AA'\), \(a'\mapsto1\otimes a'\), is closed. Define \(g:B\otimes_AA'\to K\) by \(b\otimes a'\mapsto ba'\) (a well-defined ring homomorphism: the map \((b,a')\mapsto ba'\) is \(A\)-bilinear). Then \(g\circ\phi(a')=g(1\otimes a')=a'\), the embedding of \(A'\) in \(K\). By Exercise 34 (applied to the valuation ring \(A'\), the homomorphism \(\phi\) and \(g\)), \(g(B\otimes_AA')=A'\). Since \(b=g(b\otimes1)\) for \(b\in B\), \(B\subseteq A'\). As \(A'\) was an arbitrary valuation ring of \(K\) containing \(A\), \(B\subseteq\bigcap A'\) = the integral closure of \(A\) in \(K\) (5.22). So every element of \(B\) is integral over \(A\), i.e. \(f\) is integral.

\(B\) with finitely many minimal primes. Let \(\fp_1,\dots,\fp_r\) be the minimal primes of \(B\), so \(\Nil(B)=\bigcap\fp_i\) (every prime contains a minimal prime, Ch. 1, Ex. 8). For each \(i\) the composite \(f_i:A\to B/\fp_i\) again has the property: \((B/\fp_i)\otimes_AC=(B\otimes_AC)/\fp_i^e\), so \(\Spec((B/\fp_i)\otimes_AC)\) is the closed subset \(V(\fp_i^e)\) of \(\Spec(B\otimes_AC)\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 21(iv)), and the restriction of a closed map to a closed subset is closed. Since \(B/\fp_i\) is a domain, \(f_i\) is integral by the case just proved. Therefore \(A\to\prod_i B/\fp_i\) is integral (Exercise 6). The map \(B/\Nil\to\prod B/\fp_i\) is injective, and the image of \(A\) in \(\prod B/\fp_i\) lies in \(B/\Nil\) (it is the image of \(A\) under \(A\to B\to B/\Nil\hookrightarrow\prod\)); so each element of \(B/\Nil\) is integral, in \(\prod B/\fp_i\), over the image of \(A\), with a monic equation having coefficients in that image, which lies in \(B/\Nil\). Hence \(A\to B/\Nil\) is integral. Finally let \(x\in B\); its class satisfies \(\bar x^n+\bar f(a_1)\bar x^{n-1}+\dots+\bar f(a_n)=0\), i.e. \(P(x)=x^n+f(a_1)x^{n-1}+\dots+f(a_n)\in\Nil(B)\), so \(P(x)^N=0\) for some \(N\); \(P^N\) is monic with coefficients in \(f(A)\), so \(x\) is integral over \(f(A)\). Thus \(f\) is integral. ∎
