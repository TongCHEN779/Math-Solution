Conventions. Rings are commutative with \(1\); \(\operatorname{ht}\) denotes height of a prime ideal and \(\dim\) the Krull dimension. From the text I use: Krull's principal ideal theorem in the form 11.16 (in a Noetherian ring every minimal prime ideal of \((x_1,\dots,x_r)\) has height \(\le r\)); 11.12 (heights are finite in a Noetherian ring); 11.13 (a Noetherian local ring of dimension \(d\) has an \(\fm\)-primary ideal generated by \(d\) elements); 11.14 (dimension theorem), together with the example after it (\(\dim k[x_1,\dots,x_n]_{(x_1,\dots,x_n)}=n\)); 11.18 (\(\dim A/(x)=\dim A-1\) for a non-zero-divisor \(x\in\fm\)); 11.20 and 11.21; 11.22 (regularity: \(\dim_k\fm/\fm^2=\dim A\)); 11.26 (integral extension of integrally closed domains preserves local dimension). Exercise 1

Problem

Let \(f\in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) be an irreducible polynomial over an algebraically closed field \(k\). A point \(P\) on the variety \(f(x)=0\) is non-singular \(\iff\) not all the partial derivatives \(\partial f/\partial x_i\) vanish at \(P\). Let \(A=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(f)\), and let \(\fm\) be the maximal ideal of \(A\) corresponding to the point \(P\). Prove that \(P\) is non-singular \(\iff A_\fm\) is a regular local ring. [By (11.18) we have \(\dim A_\fm=n-1\). Now \(\fm/\fm^2\cong(x_1,\dots,x_n)/\bigl((x_1,\dots,x_n)^2+(f)\bigr)\) and has dimension \(n-1\) if and only if \(f\notin(x_1,\dots,x_n)^2\).]

Solution

Write \(P=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\), \(B=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\), \(M=(x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n)\) (the maximal ideal of \(P\) in \(B\)). Since \(P\) lies on the variety, \(f\in M\), and \(f\ne0\). The ring \(A=B/(f)\) is a domain (\(f\) irreducible, \(B\) a UFD), and \(\fm=M/(f)\).

Dimension. \(B_M\) is a Noetherian local domain of dimension \(n\) (translate \(P\) to the origin and use the example after 11.14). \(f\in M B_M\) is a non-zero element of a domain, hence a non-zero-divisor in the maximal ideal. By 11.18, \(\dim A_\fm=\dim B_M/(f)=n-1\).

Cotangent space. \(\fm/\fm^2\) is a vector space over \(k=A/\fm\) killed by \(\fm\), so it is unchanged by localization at \(\fm\): \(\fm A_\fm/\fm^2A_\fm\cong\fm/\fm^2\). Moreover \(\fm^2=(M^2+(f))/(f)\), so

\[ \fm/\fm^2\cong M/(M^2+(f))=\bigl(M/M^2\bigr)\big/\bigl(\text{image of }f\bigr). \]

\(M/M^2\) has \(k\)-basis the classes of \(x_i-a_i\) (\(1\le i\le n\)), so \(\dim_kM/M^2=n\). Expand \(f\) in powers of \(y_i=x_i-a_i\) (Taylor expansion at \(P\), valid for formal derivatives in every characteristic): \(f=f(P)+\sum_i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(P)\,y_i+(\text{terms in }M^2)\), with \(f(P)=0\). Hence the image of \(f\) in \(M/M^2\) is \(\sum_i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(P)\,\bar y_i\), which is non-zero iff some partial derivative does not vanish at \(P\). So

\[ \dim_k\fm/\fm^2=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{if }P\text{ is non-singular},\\ n&\text{if }P\text{ is singular.}\end{cases} \]

By 11.22, \(A_\fm\) is regular iff \(\dim_k\fm/\fm^2=\dim A_\fm=n-1\), i.e. iff \(P\) is non-singular. ∎

Exercise 2

Problem

In (11.21) assume that \(A\) is complete. Prove that the homomorphism \(k[[t_1,\dots,t_d]]\to A\) given by \(t_i\mapsto x_i\) (\(1\le i\le d\)) is injective and that \(A\) is a finitely-generated module over \(k[[t_1,\dots,t_d]]\). [Use (10.24).]

Solution

Recall the setting of 11.21: \(A\) is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal \(\fm\), \(k\subseteq A\) a field mapping isomorphically onto \(A/\fm\), \(x_1,\dots,x_d\) a system of parameters (\(d=\dim A\)), and \(\fq=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\) is \(\fm\)-primary. Put \(B=k[[t_1,\dots,t_d]]\).

The homomorphism. Since \(\fm^r\subseteq\fq\subseteq\fm\) for some \(r\), the \(\fq\)-adic and \(\fm\)-adic topologies on \(A\) agree, and \(A\) is complete for them. For \(g=\sum c_\alpha t^\alpha\in B\) (\(c_\alpha\in k\)) the series \(\sum c_\alpha x^\alpha\) converges in \(A\) (\(x^\alpha\in\fq^{|\alpha|}\)), and \(\phi(g)=\sum c_\alpha x^\alpha\) defines a \(k\)-algebra homomorphism \(\phi:B\to A\) with \(\phi((t)^n)\subseteq\fq^n\), where \((t)=(t_1,\dots,t_d)\).

Step 1: \(G_{(t)}(B)=k[t_1,\dots,t_d]\to G_\fq(A)\), \(t_i\mapsto\bar x_i\), is injective and makes \(G_\fq(A)\) a finitely generated module. This is the map induced by \(\phi\) on associated graded rings. Injective: the map is graded, so it suffices to show that no non-zero homogeneous \(h\in k[t]\) of degree \(s\) is in its kernel, i.e. that \(h(x)\in\fq^{s+1}\) forces \(h=0\). By 11.20, if \(h(x)\in\fq^{s+1}\) then all coefficients of \(h\) lie in \(\fm\); but they lie in \(k\) and \(k\cap\fm=0\) (\(k\to A/\fm\) is injective). So \(h=0\). Finite generation: \(A/\fq\) has finite length (it is Artin), so it is a finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector space (every composition factor is \(A/\fm\cong k\)), say spanned by \(e_1,\dots,e_N\). \(G_\fq(A)_n=\fq^n/\fq^{n+1}\) is spanned over \(A/\fq\) by the classes of the monomials of degree \(n\) in \(x_i\), hence over \(k\) by \(e_j\cdot\bar x^\alpha\). So the \(e_j\in G_\fq(A)_0\) generate \(G_\fq(A)\) as a \(k[t]\)-module.

Finite generation of \(A\) over \(B\). Regard \(A\) as a \(B\)-module via \(\phi\) with the filtration \(A_n=\fq^n\); then \((t)A_n\subseteq A_{n+1}\), so this is a \((t)\)-filtration. \(B\) is complete in the \((t)\)-adic topology, \(A\) is Hausdorff in the filtration topology (\(\bigcap\fq^n=0\) by Krull's theorem, 10.17, as \(A\) is Noetherian local), and \(G(A)\) is a finitely generated \(G(B)\)-module by Step 1. By 10.24, \(A\) is a finitely generated \(B\)-module.

Injectivity of \(\phi\). Let \(0\ne g\in B\) with \(\phi(g)=0\). Write \(g=g_s+g_{s+1}+\cdots\) with \(g_j\) homogeneous of degree \(j\) and \(g_s\ne0\). Every monomial \(x^\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|>s\) can be written \(x^\beta x^{\alpha-\beta}\) with \(|\beta|=s+1\) (choose \(\beta\) for each \(\alpha\)), so grouping the convergent series \(\sum_{|\alpha|>s}c_\alpha x^\alpha=\sum_{|\beta|=s+1}x^\beta u_\beta\) with \(u_\beta\in A\); this lies in \(\fq^{s+1}\). Then \(0=\phi(g)=g_s(x)+(\text{element of }\fq^{s+1})\) gives \(g_s(x)\in\fq^{s+1}\), hence \(g_s=0\) by Step 1 (again 11.20), a contradiction. So \(\phi\) is injective. ∎

Exercise 3

Problem

Extend (11.25) to non-algebraically-closed fields. [If \(\bar k\) is the algebraic closure of \(k\), then \(\bar k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) is integral over \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\).]

Solution

Statement. Let \(k\) be any field, \(\fp\) a prime ideal of \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\), \(A=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\fp\), \(K\) its field of fractions and \(d=\operatorname{tr.deg}_kK\). Then \(\dim A_\fm=d\) for every maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\); consequently \(\dim A=d\). (For \(k\) algebraically closed, maximal ideals are the points of the variety, and this is 11.25 and 11.27.)

Step 1: for \(B=k[y_1,\dots,y_d]\) (independent indeterminates) and every maximal ideal \(\fn\) of \(B\), \(\dim B_\fn=d\). Let \(\bar k\) be an algebraic closure of \(k\) and \(\bar B=\bar k[y_1,\dots,y_d]\supseteq B\). Every element of \(\bar k\) is algebraic over \(k\), hence integral over \(B\); together with the \(y_i\in B\) this shows \(\bar B\) is integral over \(B\) (the integral closure of \(B\) in \(\bar B\) is a subring containing \(\bar k\) and \(B\)). \(B\) is a UFD, hence integrally closed (5.12). By lying over (5.10) there is a prime \(\bar\fm\) of \(\bar B\) with \(\bar\fm\cap B=\fn\), and \(\bar\fm\) is maximal by 5.8. By Lemma 11.26, \(\dim B_\fn=\dim\bar B_{\bar\fm}\). As \(\bar k\) is algebraically closed, \(\bar\fm=(y_1-c_1,\dots,y_d-c_d)\) for some \(c_i\in\bar k\) (Nullstellensatz, 7.9), and after translating \(c\) to the origin the example after 11.14 gives \(\dim\bar B_{\bar\fm}=d\).

Step 2: the general case. By the Normalization Lemma (Ch. 5, Ex. 16; the substitution \(x_i\mapsto x_i-x_n^{N^i}\) works over any field) there are \(y_1,\dots,y_r\in A\) algebraically independent over \(k\) with \(A\) integral over \(B=k[y_1,\dots,y_r]\). Then \(K\) is algebraic over \(k(y_1,\dots,y_r)\), so \(r=\operatorname{tr.deg}_kK=d\). \(B\) is integrally closed (UFD) and \(A\) is an integral domain integral over \(B\). For a maximal ideal \(\fm\) of \(A\), \(\fn=\fm\cap B\) is maximal (5.8) and Lemma 11.26 gives \(\dim A_\fm=\dim B_\fn=d\) by Step 1. Hence \(\dim A=\sup_\fm\dim A_\fm=d\) too. ∎

Exercise 4

Problem

(An example of a Noetherian domain of infinite dimension, Nagata.) Let \(k\) be a field and let \(A=k[x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n,\dots]\) be a polynomial ring over \(k\) in a countably infinite set of indeterminates. Let \(m_1,m_2,\dots\) be an increasing sequence of positive integers such that \(m_{i+1}-m_i>m_i-m_{i-1}\) for all \(i>1\). Let \(\fp_i=(x_{m_i+1},\dots,x_{m_{i+1}})\) and let \(S\) be the complement in \(A\) of the union of the ideals \(\fp_i\). Each \(\fp_i\) is a prime ideal and therefore the set \(S\) is multiplicatively closed. The ring \(S^{-1}A\) is Noetherian by Chapter 7, Exercise 9. Each \(S^{-1}\fp_i\) has height equal to \(m_{i+1}-m_i\), hence \(\dim S^{-1}A=\infty\).

Solution

Put \(r_i=m_{i+1}-m_i\). Call \(X_i=\{x_{m_i+1},\dots,x_{m_{i+1}}\}\) the \(i\)-th block of variables; the blocks are pairwise disjoint.

\(\fp_i\) is prime and \(S\) is multiplicatively closed. \(A/\fp_i\) is the polynomial ring in all variables outside \(X_i\), a domain. The complement of a union of primes is multiplicatively closed (\(1\in S\)). \(A\) is a domain, so \(S^{-1}A\) is a domain.

(a) A non-zero \(a\in A\) lies in only finitely many \(\fp_i\). A polynomial involves finitely many variables. If no variable of \(X_i\) occurs in \(a\), then \(a\in\fp_i\) would force \(a=0\) (a polynomial lies in \(\fp_i=(X_i)\) iff each of its monomials contains a variable of \(X_i\)).

(b) Every prime \(\fq\) of \(A\) with \(\fq\cap S=\emptyset\) (i.e. \(\fq\subseteq\bigcup\fp_i\)) is contained in some \(\fp_i\). Suppose not; we construct an element of \(\fq\) outside every \(\fp_j\), contradicting \(\fq\subseteq\bigcup\fp_j\). Clearly \(\fq\ne0\) (as \(0\subseteq\fp_1\)); take \(0\ne x\in\fq\) and let \(I=\{i:x\in\fp_i\}\), finite by (a); \(I\ne\emptyset\) since \(x\notin S\). As \(\fq\not\subseteq\fp_i\) for \(i\in I\), prime avoidance (1.11) gives \(z\in\fq\) with \(z\notin\fp_i\) for all \(i\in I\). Choose a variable \(x_N\) not occurring in \(x\) and not belonging to any block \(X_i\) with \(i\in I\) (possible: infinitely many variables). Put \(u=x+zx_N\in\fq\).

  • •

    For \(i\in I\): \(x\in\fp_i\) while \(z,x_N\notin\fp_i\), so \(zx_N\notin\fp_i\) (prime) and \(u\notin\fp_i\).

  • •

    For \(j\notin I\): \(x\notin\fp_j\), so \(x\) has a monomial \(\mu\) containing no variable of \(X_j\). No monomial of \(zx_N\) equals \(\mu\), because every monomial of \(zx_N\) is divisible by \(x_N\) while \(\mu\) is not. So \(\mu\) occurs in \(u\) with its coefficient unchanged, and \(\mu\) has no variable of \(X_j\); hence \(u\notin\fp_j\).

Thus \(u\in\fq\cap S\), a contradiction. So \(\fq\subseteq\fp_i\) for some \(i\).

(c) Maximal ideals of \(R=S^{-1}A\). Primes of \(R\) are \(S^{-1}\fq\) with \(\fq\) a prime of \(A\) disjoint from \(S\) (3.11), inclusion-preserving. By (b) each such \(\fq\) lies in some \(\fp_i\); and \(\fp_i\subseteq\fp_j\) forces \(i=j\) (the blocks are disjoint, so \(x\in X_i\) is not in \(\fp_j\) for \(j\ne i\)). Hence the maximal ideals of \(R\) are exactly the \(\fn_i=S^{-1}\fp_i\).

(d) \(R_{\fn_i}\) is Noetherian and \(\dim R_{\fn_i}=r_i\). Since \(S\subseteq A\setminus\fp_i\), \(R_{\fn_i}\cong A_{\fp_i}\) (Ch. 3, Ex. 3). Let \(T=k[x_j:x_j\notin X_i]\setminus\{0\}\subseteq A\setminus\fp_i\) and let \(F\) be the field of fractions of \(k[x_j:x_j\notin X_i]\). Then \(T^{-1}A=F[X_i]\) is the polynomial ring in \(r_i\) variables over \(F\), and \(A_{\fp_i}=(T^{-1}A)_{T^{-1}\fp_i}=F[X_i]_{(X_i)}\), the localization of a polynomial ring at the ideal generated by all its variables. This is a Noetherian local ring of dimension \(r_i\) (example after 11.14).

(e) \(R\) is Noetherian. By Ch. 7, Ex. 9 (a ring in which every localization at a maximal ideal is Noetherian and every non-zero element lies in only finitely many maximal ideals is Noetherian) it suffices to check these two conditions. The first is (d). For the second let \(0\ne a/s\in R\); then \(a\ne0\) and \(a/s\in\fn_i\iff a\in\fp_i\) (as \(\fp_i\cap S=\emptyset\)), which holds for only finitely many \(i\) by (a).

(f) Dimension. \(\operatorname{ht}\fn_i=\dim R_{\fn_i}=r_i=m_{i+1}-m_i\) by (d). The hypothesis \(m_{i+1}-m_i>m_i-m_{i-1}\) says the \(r_i\) form a strictly increasing sequence of integers, so \(r_i\to\infty\). Therefore \(\dim S^{-1}A\ge\sup_ir_i=\infty\): \(S^{-1}A\) is a Noetherian domain of infinite Krull dimension. (Each local ring \(R_{\fn_i}\) has finite dimension, in agreement with Cor. 11.11, but these dimensions are unbounded.) ∎

Exercise 5

Problem

Reformulate (11.1) in terms of the Grothendieck group \(K(A_0)\) (Chapter 7, Exercise 26).

(Recall: for a Noetherian ring \(A_0\), \(K(A_0)\) is the quotient of the free abelian group on isomorphism classes \((M)\) of finitely generated \(A_0\)-modules by the subgroup generated by \((M')-(M)+(M'')\) for all short exact sequences \(0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0\); \(\gamma(M)\) denotes the class of \(M\). By Ch. 7, Ex. 26(i), \(\gamma\) is the universal additive function: for every additive function \(\lambda\) on finitely generated \(A_0\)-modules with values in an abelian group \(G\) there is a unique homomorphism \(\lambda_0:K(A_0)\to G\) with \(\lambda=\lambda_0\circ\gamma\).)

Solution

Reformulation. Let \(A=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}A_n\) be a Noetherian graded ring (so \(A_0\) is Noetherian by 10.7), generated over \(A_0\) by homogeneous elements \(x_1,\dots,x_s\) of degrees \(k_1,\dots,k_s>0\), and let \(M=\bigoplus M_n\) be a finitely generated graded \(A\)-module. Each \(M_n\) is a finitely generated \(A_0\)-module, so its class \(\gamma(M_n)\in K(A_0)\) is defined. Define the universal Poincaré series

\[ P_\gamma(M,t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\gamma(M_n)\,t^n\ \in K(A_0)[[t]]. \]

Theorem 11.1\('\). \(P_\gamma(M,t)=\dfrac{f(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^s(1-t^{k_i})}\) with \(f(t)\in K(A_0)[t]\); that is, \(\prod_{i=1}^s(1-t^{k_i})\cdot P_\gamma(M,t)=f(t)\) in \(K(A_0)[[t]]\). (For every additive function \(\lambda\) with values in \(\Z\) (or any abelian group), \(P(M,t)=\sum\lambda(M_n)t^n\) is the image of \(P_\gamma(M,t)\) under the coefficientwise map \(\lambda_0:K(A_0)[[t]]\to\Z[[t]]\), so Theorem 11.1 follows by applying \(\lambda_0\) to \(f\).)

Proof (the proof of 11.1 verbatim, with \(\gamma\) in place of \(\lambda\); it only uses that \(\lambda\) is additive and takes values in an abelian group). Induction on \(s\). If \(s=0\) then \(A=A_0\); \(M\) is generated by finitely many homogeneous elements of degrees \(\le r\), so \(M_n=0\) for \(n>r\) and \(P_\gamma(M,t)\) is a polynomial.

Let \(s>0\) and assume the result for rings generated by \(s-1\) elements. Multiplication by \(x_s\) is an \(A_0\)-linear map \(M_n\to M_{n+k_s}\); let \(K_n=\Ker\) and \(L_{n+k_s}=\Coker\), giving exact sequences of finitely generated \(A_0\)-modules

\[ 0\to K_n\to M_n\xrightarrow{x_s}M_{n+k_s}\to L_{n+k_s}\to0 . \]

Put \(K=\bigoplus K_n\), \(L=\bigoplus L_n\) (with \(L_n=M_n\) for \(n<k_s\)). \(K\) is a submodule and \(L=M/x_sM\) a quotient of \(M\), so they are finitely generated \(A\)-modules (as \(A\) is Noetherian), and both are annihilated by \(x_s\); they are therefore finitely generated graded modules over the Noetherian graded ring \(A/(x_s)\), which is generated over \(A_0\) by the images of \(x_1,\dots,x_{s-1}\). Since \(\gamma\) is additive, (2.11) gives \(\gamma(K_n)-\gamma(M_n)+\gamma(M_{n+k_s})-\gamma(L_{n+k_s})=0\). Multiply by \(t^{n+k_s}\) and sum over \(n\ge0\):

\[ t^{k_s}P_\gamma(K,t)-t^{k_s}P_\gamma(M,t)+\bigl(P_\gamma(M,t)-g(t)\bigr)-\bigl(P_\gamma(L,t)-h(t)\bigr)=0, \]

where \(g=\sum_{j<k_s}\gamma(M_j)t^j\) and \(h=\sum_{j<k_s}\gamma(L_j)t^j\) are polynomials. Hence \((1-t^{k_s})P_\gamma(M,t)=P_\gamma(L,t)-t^{k_s}P_\gamma(K,t)+g(t)-h(t)\). By the inductive hypothesis \(P_\gamma(L,t)\) and \(P_\gamma(K,t)\) have the form (polynomial)\(/\prod_{i<s}(1-t^{k_i})\), and the result follows. ∎

Exercise 6

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring (not necessarily Noetherian). Prove that

\[ 1+\dim A\le\dim A[x]\le1+2\dim A. \]

[Let \(f:A\to A[x]\) be the embedding and consider the fiber of \(f^*:\Spec(A[x])\to\Spec(A)\) over a prime ideal \(\fp\) of \(A\). This fiber can be identified with the spectrum of \(k\otimes_AA[x]\cong k[x]\), where \(k\) is the residue field at \(\fp\) (Chapter 3, Exercise 21), and \(\dim k[x]=1\). Now use Exercise 7(ii) of Chapter 4.]

Solution

If \(\dim A=\infty\) both inequalities say \(\dim A[x]=\infty\), which follows from the lower bound below; so assume the dimension finite where needed.

Lower bound. Let \(\fp_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\fp_r\) be a chain of primes of \(A\). By Ch. 4, Ex. 7(ii) the ideals \(\fp_i[x]\) are prime in \(A[x]\), and they form a strictly increasing chain (compare constant polynomials). Moreover \(\fp_r[x]\subsetneq\fp_r[x]+(x)\), which is prime since \(A[x]/(\fp_r[x]+(x))\cong A/\fp_r\) is a domain. This is a chain of length \(r+1\), so \(\dim A[x]\ge1+\dim A\).

Upper bound. Let \(Q_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq Q_m\) be a chain of primes of \(A[x]\) and \(\fp_i=Q_i\cap A\), so \(\fp_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\fp_m\). Fix a prime \(\fp\) of \(A\) and consider the primes \(Q\) of \(A[x]\) with \(Q\cap A=\fp\). They contain \(\fp[x]\), so correspond to primes of \((A/\fp)[x]\) whose contraction to \(A/\fp\) is \(0\), i.e. which do not meet \(S=(A/\fp)\setminus\{0\}\); by 3.11(iv) these correspond, inclusion-preservingly, to primes of \(S^{-1}((A/\fp)[x])=k(\fp)[x]\) (this is the identification of the fibre of Ch. 3, Ex. 21(iv)). Now \(k(\fp)[x]\) is a principal ideal domain of dimension \(1\), so it has no chain of three distinct primes. Hence at most two of the \(Q_i\) have the same contraction \(\fp\). The distinct values among \(\fp_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\fp_m\) form a strictly increasing chain of primes of \(A\), so there are at most \(\dim A+1\) of them. Therefore \(m+1\le2(\dim A+1)\), i.e. \(m\le1+2\dim A\). Hence \(\dim A[x]\le1+2\dim A\). ∎

Exercise 7

Problem

Let \(A\) be a Noetherian ring. Then

\[ \dim A[x]=1+\dim A, \]

and hence, by induction on \(n\),

\[ \dim A[x_1,\dots,x_n]=n+\dim A. \]

[Let \(\fp\) be a prime ideal of height \(m\) in \(A\). Then there exist \(a_1,\dots,a_m\in\fp\) such that \(\fp\) is a minimal prime ideal belonging to the ideal \(\fa=(a_1,\dots,a_m)\). By Exercise 7 of Chapter 4, \(\fp[x]\) is a minimal prime ideal of \(\fa[x]\) and therefore \(\operatorname{height}\fp[x]\le m\). On the other hand, a chain of prime ideals \(\fp_0\subset\fp_1\subset\cdots\subset\fp_m=\fp\) gives rise to a chain \(\fp_0[x]\subset\cdots\subset\fp_m[x]=\fp[x]\), hence \(\operatorname{height}\fp[x]\ge m\). Hence \(\operatorname{height}\fp[x]=\operatorname{height}\fp\). Now use the argument of Exercise 6.]

Solution

By Ex. 6, \(\dim A[x]\ge1+\dim A\). For the reverse inequality it suffices to show:

\[ (\ast)\qquad\text{for every prime }Q\text{ of }A[x]\text{ with }\fp=Q\cap A:\quad\operatorname{ht}Q\le\operatorname{ht}\fp+1 , \]

because then \(\dim A[x]=\sup_Q\operatorname{ht}Q\le\sup_\fp\operatorname{ht}\fp+1=\dim A+1\) (heights are finite by 11.12 since \(A\) and, by the Hilbert basis theorem, \(A[x]\) are Noetherian).

Reduction to \(A\) local. Let \(T=A\setminus\fp\). Every prime \(Q'\subseteq Q\) of \(A[x]\) has \(Q'\cap A\subseteq\fp\), so it does not meet \(T\); by 3.11 and 3.13, \(\operatorname{ht}Q=\operatorname{ht}(T^{-1}Q)\) in \(T^{-1}(A[x])=A_\fp[x]\), and \(T^{-1}Q\cap A_\fp=\fp A_\fp\). So we may replace \(A\) by the Noetherian local ring \(A_\fp\) and assume \(A\) local with maximal ideal \(\fp=\fm\), \(d=\operatorname{ht}\fm=\dim A\) and \(Q\cap A=\fm\).

The case \(A\) local. By 11.13 there are \(a_1,\dots,a_d\in\fm\) with \(\fa=(a_1,\dots,a_d)\) \(\fm\)-primary. By Ch. 4, Ex. 7(iii), \(\fa[x]=\fa A[x]\) is \(\fm[x]\)-primary, so \(r(\fa A[x])=\fm[x]\). Now \(Q\supseteq\fm[x]\) and \(Q/\fm[x]\) is a prime of \((A/\fm)[x]\) whose contraction to \(A/\fm\) is \(0\).

  • •

    If \(Q=\fm[x]\), then \(Q\) is minimal over \(\fa A[x]\) (its radical), an ideal generated by \(d\) elements, so \(\operatorname{ht}Q\le d\) by 11.16.

  • •

    If \(Q\supsetneq\fm[x]\), then \(Q/\fm[x]\) is a non-zero prime of the PID \((A/\fm)[x]\), hence principal: \(Q=\fm[x]+(f)\) for some \(f\in A[x]\). The ideal \(\fb=(a_1,\dots,a_d,f)\) is contained in \(Q\), so \(r(\fb)\subseteq Q\); and \(r(\fb)\supseteq r(\fa A[x])=\fm[x]\) and \(f\in r(\fb)\), so \(r(\fb)\supseteq Q\). Thus \(r(\fb)=Q\) and \(Q\) is a minimal prime of \(\fb\), which has \(d+1\) generators; by 11.16, \(\operatorname{ht}Q\le d+1\).

In either case \(\operatorname{ht}Q\le d+1=\operatorname{ht}\fm+1\), proving \((\ast)\). Hence \(\dim A[x]=1+\dim A\) (if \(\dim A=\infty\), Ex. 6 gives \(\dim A[x]=\infty\) as well).

Induction. \(A[x_1,\dots,x_n]=A[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}][x_n]\) and \(A[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}]\) is Noetherian (7.5), so \(\dim A[x_1,\dots,x_n]=1+\dim A[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}]=n+\dim A\).

Remark (the hint's route). The argument above also shows \(\operatorname{ht}\fp[x]=\operatorname{ht}\fp\): the inequality \(\ge\) comes from the chain \(\fp_0[x]\subset\cdots\subset\fp[x]\), and \(\le\) from the case \(Q=\fm[x]\) treated above (after localizing at \(\fp\), where \(\fp[x]\mapsto\fm[x]\)). ∎
