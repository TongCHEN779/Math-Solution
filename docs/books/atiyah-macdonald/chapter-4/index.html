Conventions. For an ideal \(\fa\) and multiplicatively closed \(S\), \(S(\fa)=(S^{-1}\fa)^c=\bigcup_{s\in S}(\fa:s)\) is the contraction of \(S^{-1}\fa\) (3.11(ii)), and \(S_\fp(\fa)\) means \(S(\fa)\) for \(S=A-\fp\). A decomposable ideal is one with a primary decomposition; minimal (irredundant) decompositions have distinct radicals \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\) and no superfluous component. From the text I use: \(r(\fq)\) is prime for \(\fq\) primary (4.1); powers of a maximal ideal are primary (4.2); 4.3 and 4.4; the first uniqueness theorem 4.5 (the prime ideals \(r(\fa:x)\) that occur are exactly the \(\fp_i\)) and 4.6; 4.7, 4.8, 4.9 and the second uniqueness theorem 4.10–4.11; also 1.11 (prime avoidance), 1.13, 1.14 and Ch. 3.

Exercise 1

Problem

If an ideal \(\fa\) has a primary decomposition, then \(\Spec(A/\fa)\) has only finitely many irreducible components.

Solution

Let \(\fa=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\fq_i\) be a minimal primary decomposition, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\). \(\Spec(A/\fa)\cong V(\fa)\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 21(iv)) and the irreducible components of \(\Spec(A/\fa)\) are the closed sets \(V(\bar\fp)\) with \(\bar\fp\) a minimal prime of \(A/\fa\) (Ch. 1, Ex. 20(iv)), i.e. \(V(\fp)\) with \(\fp\) minimal among the primes containing \(\fa\). By 4.6 these are among \(\fp_1,\dots,\fp_n\). So there are at most \(n\) components. ∎

Exercise 2

Problem

If \(\fa=r(\fa)\), then \(\fa\) has no embedded prime ideals.

Solution

(We assume \(\fa\) is decomposable, so that associated primes make sense.) Let \(\fa=\bigcap\fq_i\) be a minimal decomposition, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\), so \(r(\fa)=\bigcap\fp_i\). Every embedded \(\fp_j\) contains a minimal \(\fp_i\) (4.6 and the definition), so we may drop the embedded ones from the intersection: \(\fa=r(\fa)=\bigcap_{\fp_i\text{ minimal}}\fp_i\). This is a primary decomposition by prime ideals (primes are primary), with pairwise incomparable (so irredundant) distinct radicals, i.e. a minimal primary decomposition. By the first uniqueness theorem 4.5, the associated primes of \(\fa\) are the \(\fp_i\) appearing in any minimal decomposition; hence they are all minimal, and there are no embedded primes. ∎

Exercise 3

Problem

If \(A\) is absolutely flat, every primary ideal is maximal.

Solution

Let \(\fq\) be primary and \(\fp=r(\fq)\). \(A/\Nil\) is absolutely flat (a homomorphic image, Ch. 2, Ex. 28), so every prime of \(A\) is maximal (Ch. 3, Ex. 11); in particular \(\fp\) is maximal. Let \(x\in\fp\). As \(A\) is absolutely flat, \(x=ax^2\) for some \(a\) (Ch. 2, Ex. 27), so \(x(1-ax)=0\in\fq\). Now \(1-ax\notin\fp\) (else \(1\in\fp\)), hence \((1-ax)^n\notin\fq\) for all \(n\); since \(\fq\) is primary, \(x\in\fq\). Thus \(\fp\subseteq\fq\subseteq\fp\) and \(\fq=\fp\) is maximal. ∎

Exercise 4

Problem

In the polynomial ring \(\Z[t]\), the ideal \(\fm=(2,t)\) is maximal and the ideal \(\fq=(4,t)\) is \(\fm\)-primary, but is not a power of \(\fm\).

Solution

\(\Z[t]/(2,t)\cong\Z/2=\mathbf F_2\), so \(\fm\) is maximal. We have \(\fm^2=(4,2t,t^2)\subseteq\fq=(4,t)\subseteq\fm\), hence \(r(\fq)=\fm\) and \(\fq\) is \(\fm\)-primary by 4.2.

\(\fq\) is not a power of \(\fm\): \(\fq\ne\fm\) since \(2\notin\fq\) (note \(\fq\cap\Z=4\Z\)); \(\fq\ne\fm^2\) since \(t\in\fq\) but \(t\notin\fm^2\) (the coefficient of \(t\) in an element \(4f+2tg+t^2h\) of \(\fm^2\) is even). Since \(\fm^n\subseteq\fm^2\subsetneq\fq\) for \(n\ge2\), \(\fq\) equals no \(\fm^n\). So \(\fm^2\subsetneq\fq\subsetneq\fm\). ∎

Exercise 5

Problem

In the polynomial ring \(K[x,y,z]\) where \(K\) is a field and \(x,y,z\) are independent indeterminates, let \(\fp_1=(x,y)\), \(\fp_2=(x,z)\), \(\fm=(x,y,z)\); \(\fp_1\) and \(\fp_2\) are prime, and \(\fm\) is maximal. Let \(\fa=\fp_1\fp_2\). Show that \(\fa=\fp_1\cap\fp_2\cap\fm^2\) is a reduced primary decomposition of \(\fa\). Which components are isolated and which are embedded?

Solution

\(\fa=\fp_1\fp_2=(x^2,xz,xy,yz)\). We have \(\fp_1\cap\fp_2=(x,yz)\) (monomial ideals: a monomial lies in both iff it is divisible by \(x\) or by both \(y\) and \(z\)), and \(\fm^2=(x^2,y^2,z^2,xy,xz,yz)\). Intersecting monomial ideals: \((x,yz)\cap\fm^2=(x)\cap\fm^2+(yz)\cap\fm^2=(x^2,xy,xz)+(yz)=\fa\). So \(\fa=\fp_1\cap\fp_2\cap\fm^2\).

Primary: \(\fp_1,\fp_2\) are prime, \(\fm^2\) is \(\fm\)-primary (4.2). The radicals \(\fp_1,\fp_2,\fm\) are distinct.

Irredundant: \(z^2\in\fp_2\cap\fm^2\) but \(z^2\notin\fp_1\), so \(\fp_2\cap\fm^2\not\subseteq\fp_1\); \(y^2\in\fp_1\cap\fm^2\), \(y^2\notin\fp_2\); \(x\in\fp_1\cap\fp_2\), \(x\notin\fm^2\). So no component can be omitted: the decomposition is reduced (minimal).

Isolated vs. embedded: \(\fp_1,\fp_2\) are incomparable and minimal; \(\fm\supsetneq\fp_1\). So \(\fp_1,\fp_2\) are the isolated primes, and the component \(\fm^2\) (belonging to \(\fm=(x,y,z)\)) is embedded. ∎

Exercise 6

Problem

Let \(X\) be an infinite compact Hausdorff space, \(C(X)\) the ring of real-valued continuous functions on \(X\) (Chapter 1, Exercise 26). Is the zero ideal decomposable in this ring?

Solution

No.

Step 1: \(X\) contains infinitely many pairwise disjoint non-empty open sets \(U_1,U_2,\dots\). An infinite subset \(E\subseteq X\) has an accumulation point \(x_0\) (compactness). Choose \(y_1\in E-\{x_0\}\) and disjoint open \(U_1\ni y_1\), \(V_1\ni x_0\). \(V_1\) contains infinitely many points of \(E\), so choose \(y_2\in E\cap V_1-\{x_0\}\) and disjoint open \(U_2\ni y_2\), \(V_2\ni x_0\) with \(U_2,V_2\subseteq V_1\); and so on. Then \(U_n\subseteq V_{n-1}\subseteq V_k\) for \(k<n\), and \(U_k\cap V_k=\emptyset\), so the \(U_n\) are pairwise disjoint.

Step 2: functions. \(X\) is normal, so by Urysohn there are continuous \(f_n:X\to[0,1]\) with \(f_n(y_n)=1\) and \(f_n=0\) outside \(U_n\). Then \(f_n\ne0\) and \(f_nf_m=0\) for \(n\ne m\).

Step 3. Suppose \(0=\fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_r\) with \(\fq_i\) primary, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\) prime. Then \(\bigcap\fp_i=r(0)=0\) (\(C(X)\) has no non-zero nilpotents). Fix \(i\). If \(n\ne m\) and neither \(f_n\) nor \(f_m\) lies in \(\fp_i\), then \(f_nf_m=0\in\fp_i\) contradicts primality. So at most one \(n\) has \(f_n\notin\fp_i\); i.e. \(\{n:f_n\in\fp_i\}\) is cofinite. A finite intersection of cofinite sets is cofinite, hence non-empty: some \(f_n\) lies in every \(\fp_i\), so \(f_n\in\bigcap\fp_i=0\), contradicting \(f_n\ne0\).

Hence \(0\) is not decomposable in \(C(X)\). ∎

Exercise 7

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring and let \(A[x]\) denote the ring of polynomials in one indeterminate over \(A\). For each ideal \(\fa\) of \(A\), let \(\fa[x]\) denote the set of all polynomials in \(A[x]\) with coefficients in \(\fa\).

  1. (i)

    \(\fa[x]\) is the extension of \(\fa\) to \(A[x]\).

  2. (ii)

    If \(\fp\) is a prime ideal in \(A\), then \(\fp[x]\) is a prime ideal in \(A[x]\).

  3. (iii)

    If \(\fq\) is a \(\fp\)-primary ideal in \(A\), then \(\fq[x]\) is a \(\fp[x]\)-primary ideal in \(A[x]\). [Use Chapter 1, Exercise 2.]

  4. (iv)

    If \(\fa=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\fq_i\) is a minimal primary decomposition in \(A\), then \(\fa[x]=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\fq_i[x]\) is a minimal primary decomposition in \(A[x]\).

  5. (v)

    If \(\fp\) is a minimal prime ideal of \(\fa\), then \(\fp[x]\) is a minimal prime ideal of \(\fa[x]\).

Solution

(i) The extension is the ideal \(A[x]\fa\); its elements \(\sum g_ia_i\) have all coefficients in \(\fa\), and conversely \(\sum a_jx^j=\sum x^j a_j\in A[x]\fa\).

(ii) \(\fp[x]=\Ker(A[x]\to(A/\fp)[x])\) and \((A/\fp)[x]\) is a domain.

(iii) \(A[x]/\fq[x]\cong(A/\fq)[x]\) and \(\fq[x]\ne A[x]\). By Ch. 1, Ex. 2(iii), a zero-divisor \(f=\sum\bar a_jx^j\) of \((A/\fq)[x]\) is killed by some \(\bar a\ne0\) in \(A/\fq\); then \(\bar a\bar a_j=0\) for all \(j\), so each \(\bar a_j\) is a zero-divisor of \(A/\fq\) (or \(0\)), hence nilpotent, since \(\fq\) is primary; by Ch. 1, Ex. 2(ii), \(f\) is nilpotent. So \(\fq[x]\) is primary. Its radical: \(f\in r(\fq[x])\iff\bar f\) nilpotent in \((A/\fq)[x]\iff\) all \(\bar a_j\) nilpotent (Ex. 2(ii)) \(\iff a_j\in r(\fq)=\fp\), i.e. \(r(\fq[x])=\fp[x]\).

(iv) A polynomial has all coefficients in \(\bigcap\fq_i\) iff it has all coefficients in each \(\fq_i\), so \(\fa[x]=\bigcap\fq_i[x]\); the \(\fq_i[x]\) are \(\fp_i[x]\)-primary by (iii) and \(\fp_i\ne\fp_j\Rightarrow\fp_i[x]\ne\fp_j[x]\) (compare constants). Irredundance: if \(\bigl(\bigcap_{j\ne i}\fq_j\bigr)[x]\subseteq\fq_i[x]\), comparing constant polynomials gives \(\bigcap_{j\ne i}\fq_j\subseteq\fq_i\), a contradiction.

(v) The minimal primes of \(\fa\) are the minimal elements of \(\{\fp_1,\dots,\fp_n\}\) (4.6), and the minimal primes of \(\fa[x]\) are the minimal elements of \(\{\fp_1[x],\dots,\fp_n[x]\}\) by (iv) and 4.6. Since \(\fp_i\subseteq\fp_j\iff\fp_i[x]\subseteq\fp_j[x]\), a minimal \(\fp_i\) gives a minimal \(\fp_i[x]\). ∎

Exercise 8

Problem

Let \(k\) be a field. Show that in the polynomial ring \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) the ideals \(\fp_i=(x_1,\dots,x_i)\) \((1\le i\le n)\) are prime and all their powers are primary. [Use Exercise 7.]

Solution

\(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/\fp_i\cong k[x_{i+1},\dots,x_n]\) is a domain, so \(\fp_i\) is prime. We show by induction on \(n\) that every power \(\fp_i^m\) is \(\fp_i\)-primary in \(A_n=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\). For \(i=n\), \(\fp_n\) is maximal and its powers are primary (4.2); this covers \(n=1\). Let \(i<n\) and \(A_n=A_{n-1}[x_n]\). The extension of \(\fp_i^m\subseteq A_{n-1}\) to \(A_n\) is \((\fp_i^m)[x_n]\) (Ex. 7(i)), and it equals the \(m\)-th power of the extension \(\fp_i[x_n]=(x_1,\dots,x_i)A_n\). By induction \(\fp_i^m\) is \(\fp_i\)-primary in \(A_{n-1}\), so by Ex. 7(iii) \((\fp_i^m)[x_n]\) is \(\fp_i[x_n]\)-primary in \(A_n\), i.e. the \(m\)-th power of \(\fp_i\) in \(A_n\) is \(\fp_i\)-primary. ∎

Exercise 9

Problem

In a ring \(A\), let \(D(A)\) denote the set of prime ideals \(\fp\) which satisfy the following condition: there exists \(a\in A\) such that \(\fp\) is minimal in the set of prime ideals containing \((0:a)\). Show that \(x\in A\) is a zero-divisor \(\iff x\in\fp\) for some \(\fp\in D(A)\).

Let \(S\) be a multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\), and identify \(\Spec(S^{-1}A)\) with its image in \(\Spec(A)\) (Chapter 3, Exercise 21). Show that \(D(S^{-1}A)=D(A)\cap\Spec(S^{-1}A)\).

If the zero ideal has a primary decomposition, show that \(D(A)\) is the set of associated prime ideals of \(0\).

Solution

Zero-divisors. (\(\Rightarrow\)) If \(xa=0\), \(a\ne0\), then \(x\in(0:a)\ne(1)\). \(r(0:a)\) is the intersection of the primes containing \((0:a)\), hence of the minimal ones among them; so \(x\) lies in each such minimal prime, which belongs to \(D(A)\) (and at least one exists).

(\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(\fp\) be minimal over \(\fb=(0:a)\) and \(x\in\fp\). The only prime of \(A_\fp\) containing \(\fb_\fp\) is \(\fp A_\fp\), so \(\fp A_\fp=r(\fb_\fp)\), and \((x/1)^n\in\fb_\fp\) for some \(n\): there is \(s\notin\fp\) with \(sx^n\in(0:a)\), i.e. \(b:=sa\) satisfies \(x^nb=0\). Moreover \(b\ne0\): otherwise \(s\in(0:a)\subseteq\fp\). Let \(k\ge1\) be minimal with \(x^kb=0\); then \(x^{k-1}b\ne0\) and \(x\cdot x^{k-1}b=0\). So \(x\) is a zero-divisor.

Localization. Let \(\fq\in\Spec(S^{-1}A)\), \(\fp=\fq^c\) (\(\fp\cap S=\emptyset\)). For \(a\in A\), \(s\in S\) we have \(\Ann_{S^{-1}A}(a/s)=\Ann_{S^{-1}A}(a/1)=S^{-1}(0:a)\) (3.14 applied to the cyclic module \(Aa\)). Primes of \(S^{-1}A\) containing \(S^{-1}(0:a)\) correspond (order-preservingly, 3.11) to primes of \(A\) disjoint from \(S\) containing \((0:a)\), and any prime contained in \(\fp\) is disjoint from \(S\). Hence \(\fq\) is minimal over \(S^{-1}(0:a)\) iff \(\fp\) is minimal over \((0:a)\) among all primes of \(A\) (for “\(\Leftarrow\)”: take \(\fp\) minimal over \((0:a)\), then \(\fq\) is minimal over \(S^{-1}(0:a)=(0:a/1)\); for “\(\Rightarrow\)”: every element of \(S^{-1}A\) has the form \(a/s\) and its annihilator is \(S^{-1}(0:a)\)). So \(D(S^{-1}A)=D(A)\cap\Spec(S^{-1}A)\).

Decomposable zero ideal. Let \(0=\bigcap\fq_i\) be minimal, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\). (\(\supseteq\)) By 4.5 and its remark, \(\fp_i=r(0:x_i)\) with \((0:x_i)\) \(\fp_i\)-primary; so \(\fp_i\) is the unique minimal prime over \((0:x_i)\), hence \(\fp_i\in D(A)\). (\(\subseteq\)) Let \(\fp\in D(A)\) be minimal over \((0:a)\), \(a\ne0\). As in the proof of 4.5, \(r(0:a)=\bigcap_{a\notin\fq_i}\fp_i\) (since \((0:a)=\bigcap(\fq_i:a)\), which is \((1)\) if \(a\in\fq_i\) and has radical \(\fp_i\) otherwise). The prime \(\fp\supseteq(0:a)\) contains this intersection, hence some \(\fp_i\) with \(a\notin\fq_i\), and \(\fp_i\supseteq r(0:a)\supseteq(0:a)\). By minimality of \(\fp\), \(\fp=\fp_i\). ∎

Exercise 10

Problem

For any prime ideal \(\fp\) in a ring \(A\), let \(S_\fp(0)\) denote the kernel of the homomorphism \(A\to A_\fp\). Prove that

  1. (i)

    \(S_\fp(0)\subseteq\fp\).

  2. (ii)

    \(r(S_\fp(0))=\fp\iff\fp\) is a minimal prime ideal of \(A\).

  3. (iii)

    If \(\fp\supseteq\fp'\), then \(S_\fp(0)\subseteq S_{\fp'}(0)\).

  4. (iv)

    \(\bigcap_{\fp\in D(A)}S_\fp(0)=0\), where \(D(A)\) is defined in Exercise 9.

Solution

(i) If \(x/1=0\) in \(A_\fp\), then \(sx=0\in\fp\) for some \(s\notin\fp\), so \(x\in\fp\).

(ii) \(S_\fp(0)\) is the contraction of \(0\subseteq A_\fp\), and \(r(0)=\Nil(A_\fp)=\bigcap_{\fp'\subseteq\fp}\fp'A_\fp\) (3.13, 3.12); contracting (1.18: \(r(\fb)^c=r(\fb^c)\)): \(r(S_\fp(0))=\bigcap_{\fp'\subseteq\fp}\fp'\). This equals \(\fp\) iff \(\fp\subseteq\fp'\) for every prime \(\fp'\subseteq\fp\), i.e. iff \(\fp\) is minimal.

(iii) If \(sx=0\) with \(s\notin\fp\), then \(s\notin\fp'\) (as \(\fp'\subseteq\fp\)), so \(x/1=0\) in \(A_{\fp'}\).

(iv) Let \(x\ne0\), so \((0:x)\ne(1)\), and let \(\fp\) be a prime minimal over \((0:x)\); then \(\fp\in D(A)\). If \(x\in S_\fp(0)\) there is \(s\notin\fp\) with \(sx=0\), i.e. \(s\in(0:x)\subseteq\fp\), a contradiction. So \(x\notin\bigcap_{D(A)}S_\fp(0)\). ∎

Exercise 11

Problem

If \(\fp\) is a minimal prime ideal of a ring \(A\), show that \(S_\fp(0)\) (Exercise 10) is the smallest \(\fp\)-primary ideal.

Let \(\fa\) be the intersection of the ideals \(S_\fp(0)\) as \(\fp\) runs through the minimal prime ideals of \(A\). Show that \(\fa\) is contained in the nilradical of \(A\).

Suppose that the zero ideal is decomposable. Prove that \(\fa=0\) if and only if every prime ideal of \(0\) is isolated.

Solution

\(S_\fp(0)\) is \(\fp\)-primary. \(A_\fp\) has a unique prime ideal \(\fp A_\fp\) (3.13), which is therefore its nilradical. In \(A_\fp\) every non-unit is nilpotent, so every zero-divisor is nilpotent and \(0\) is a primary ideal of \(A_\fp\), with radical \(\fp A_\fp\). Its contraction \(S_\fp(0)\) is primary with radical \((\fp A_\fp)^c=\fp\).

Smallest. If \(\fq\) is \(\fp\)-primary, then \(S_\fp(\fq)=\fq\) by 4.8(ii) (as \((A-\fp)\cap\fp=\emptyset\)), and \(S_\fp(0)\subseteq S_\fp(\fq)=\fq\).

\(\fa\subseteq\Nil\). By Ex. 10(i) \(\fa\subseteq\bigcap_{\fp\text{ min}}\fp=\Nil\) (every prime contains a minimal prime).

Decomposable zero ideal. Let \(0=\bigcap\fq_i\) be minimal, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\). Minimal primes of \(A\) are the minimal \(\fp_i\) (4.6). For \(\fp_i\) minimal, \(S=A-\fp_i\) meets \(\fp_j\) iff \(\fp_j\not\subseteq\fp_i\) iff \(j\ne i\) (by minimality, \(\fp_j\subseteq\fp_i\Rightarrow\fp_j=\fp_i\)), so by 4.9 \(S_{\fp_i}(0)=\fq_i\). Hence \(\fa=\bigcap_{\fp_i\text{ min}}\fq_i\). If every \(\fp_i\) is isolated, then \(\fa=\bigcap_{\text{all }i}\fq_i=0\). Conversely if \(\fa=0\), then \(0=\bigcap_{\fp_i\text{ min}}\fq_i\) is a primary decomposition which (after removing redundant terms) is minimal and has only minimal primes as associated primes; by 4.5 the associated primes of \(0\) do not depend on the decomposition, so there are no embedded primes. ∎

Exercise 12

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring, \(S\) a multiplicatively closed subset of \(A\). For any ideal \(\fa\), let \(S(\fa)\) denote the contraction in \(A\) of \(S^{-1}\fa\). The ideal \(S(\fa)\) is called the saturation of \(\fa\) with respect to \(S\). Prove that

  1. (i)

    \(S(\fa)\cap S(\fb)=S(\fa\cap\fb)\);

  2. (ii)

    \(S(r(\fa))=r(S(\fa))\);

  3. (iii)

    \(S(\fa)=(1)\iff\fa\) meets \(S\);

  4. (iv)

    \(S_1(S_2(\fa))=(S_1S_2)(\fa)\).

If \(\fa\) has a primary decomposition, prove that the set of ideals \(S(\fa)\) (where \(S\) runs through all multiplicatively closed subsets of \(A\)) is finite.

Solution

Recall \(S(\fa)=\{x:sx\in\fa\text{ for some }s\in S\}\) (3.11(ii)).

(i) \(S^{-1}(\fa\cap\fb)=S^{-1}\fa\cap S^{-1}\fb\) (3.4) and contraction commutes with intersection.

(ii) \(S^{-1}r(\fa)=r(S^{-1}\fa)\) (3.11(v)), and \(r(\fc)^c=r(\fc^c)\) (1.18); apply with \(\fc=S^{-1}\fa\).

(iii) \(S(\fa)=(1)\iff1\in S(\fa)\iff1/1\in S^{-1}\fa\iff S^{-1}\fa=(1)\iff\fa\cap S\ne\emptyset\) (3.11(ii)).

(iv) \(x\in S_1(S_2(\fa))\iff s_1x\in S_2(\fa)\) for some \(s_1\in S_1\iff s_2s_1x\in\fa\) for some \(s_1\in S_1\), \(s_2\in S_2\iff x\in(S_1S_2)(\fa)\).

Finiteness. Let \(\fa=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\fq_i\) be a minimal decomposition, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\). By 4.9, \(S(\fa)=\bigcap_{i:\fp_i\cap S=\emptyset}\fq_i\) (the empty intersection being \((1)\)). So \(S(\fa)\) depends only on the subset \(\{i:\fp_i\cap S=\emptyset\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\); there are at most \(2^n\) such ideals. ∎

Exercise 13

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring and \(\fp\) a prime ideal of \(A\). The \(n\)th symbolic power of \(\fp\) is defined to be the ideal (in the notation of Exercise 12) \(\fp^{(n)}=S_\fp(\fp^n)\), where \(S_\fp=A-\fp\). Show that

  1. (i)

    \(\fp^{(n)}\) is a \(\fp\)-primary ideal;

  2. (ii)

    if \(\fp^n\) has a primary decomposition, then \(\fp^{(n)}\) is its \(\fp\)-primary component;

  3. (iii)

    if \(\fp^{(m)}\fp^{(n)}\) has a primary decomposition, then \(\fp^{(m+n)}\) is its \(\fp\)-primary component;

  4. (iv)

    \(\fp^{(n)}=\fp^n\iff\fp^n\) is \(\fp\)-primary.

Solution

(i) In \(A_\fp\), \(r(\fp^nA_\fp)=\fp A_\fp\) is maximal, so \(\fp^nA_\fp\) is primary (4.2); its contraction \(\fp^{(n)}\) is primary with radical \((\fp A_\fp)^c=\fp\).

(ii) \(r(\fp^n)=\fp\) (1.13(vi)); every prime containing \(\fp^n\) contains \(\fp\), so \(\fp\) is the unique minimal prime of \(\fp^n\), and the \(\fp\)-component of any minimal decomposition is isolated. By 4.9/4.11 the isolated component is \(S(\fp^n)\) for \(S=A-\fp\), i.e. \(\fp^{(n)}\).

(iii) Put \(\fb=\fp^{(m)}\fp^{(n)}\). \(r(\fb)=r(\fp^{(m)})\cap r(\fp^{(n)})=\fp\) (1.13), so as in (ii), \(\fp\) is the unique minimal prime of \(\fb\) and its component is \(S_\fp(\fb)\). Now \(S_\fp^{-1}\fp^{(m)}=\fp^mA_\fp\) (since \(\fp^mA_\fp\) is an extended ideal, \((\fp^mA_\fp)^{ce}=\fp^mA_\fp\)), so \(S_\fp^{-1}\fb=\fp^mA_\fp\cdot\fp^nA_\fp=\fp^{m+n}A_\fp\) and \(S_\fp(\fb)=\fp^{(m+n)}\).

(iv) (\(\Leftarrow\)) If \(\fp^n\) is \(\fp\)-primary, then \(S_\fp(\fp^n)=\fp^n\) by 4.8(ii). (\(\Rightarrow\)) \(\fp^n=\fp^{(n)}\) is \(\fp\)-primary by (i). ∎

Exercise 14

Problem

Let \(\fa\) be a decomposable ideal in a ring \(A\) and let \(\fp\) be a maximal element of the set of ideals \((\fa:x)\), where \(x\in A\) and \(x\notin\fa\). Show that \(\fp\) is a prime ideal belonging to \(\fa\).

Solution

Let \(\fp=(\fa:x)\) with \(x\notin\fa\) (so \(\fp\ne(1)\)) maximal in the set. Suppose \(yz\in\fp\) with \(z\notin\fp\). Then \(yzx\in\fa\), so \(y\in(\fa:zx)\). Also \(zx\notin\fa\) (else \(z\in(\fa:x)=\fp\)) and \((\fa:zx)\supseteq(\fa:x)=\fp\). By maximality \((\fa:zx)=\fp\), so \(y\in\fp\). Hence \(\fp\) is prime. Then \(r(\fa:x)=\fp\) is a prime of the form \(r(\fa:x)\), so by the first uniqueness theorem (4.5) \(\fp\) is one of the primes belonging to \(\fa\). ∎

Problem

Let \(A\) be a ring with the following property.

(L1) For every ideal \(\fa\ne(1)\) in \(A\) and every prime ideal \(\fp\), there exists \(x\notin\fp\) such that \(S_\fp(\fa)=(\fa:x)\), where \(S_\fp=A-\fp\).

Then every ideal in \(A\) is an intersection of (possibly infinitely many) primary ideals. [Let \(\fa\) be an ideal \(\ne(1)\) in \(A\), and let \(\fp_1\) be a minimal element of the set of prime ideals containing \(\fa\). Then \(\fq_1=S_{\fp_1}(\fa)\) is \(\fp_1\)-primary (by Exercise 11), and \(\fq_1=(\fa:x)\) for some \(x\notin\fp_1\). Show that \(\fa=\fq_1\cap(\fa+(x))\). Now let \(\fa_1\) be a maximal element of the set of ideals \(\fb\supseteq\fa\) such that \(\fq_1\cap\fb=\fa\), and choose \(\fa_1\) so that \(x\in\fa_1\), and therefore \(\fa_1\not\subseteq\fp_1\). Repeat the construction starting with \(\fa_1\), and so on. At the \(n\)th stage we have \(\fa=\fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_n\cap\fa_n\) where the \(\fq_i\) are primary ideals, \(\fa_n\) is maximal among the ideals \(\fb\supseteq\fa_{n-1}=\fa_n\cap\fq_n\) such that \(\fa=\fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_n\cap\fb\), and \(\fa_n\not\subseteq\fp_n\). If at any stage we have \(\fa_n=(1)\), the process stops, and \(\fa\) is a finite intersection of primary ideals. If not, continue by transfinite induction, observing that each \(\fa_n\) strictly contains \(\fa_{n-1}\).]

Solution

Let \(\fa\ne(1)\). We construct, by transfinite recursion, ideals \(\fa_\alpha\) (with \(\fa_0=\fa\)) increasing strictly with \(\alpha\), and primary ideals \(\fq_{\alpha}\) (\(\alpha\ge1\)), such that

\[ (\ast_\alpha)\qquad \fa=\bigcap_{1\le\beta\le\alpha}\fq_\beta\ \cap\ \fa_\alpha . \]

Key step. Suppose \((\ast_\alpha)\) holds and \(\fa_\alpha\ne(1)\). Let \(\fp\) be a prime minimal over \(\fa_\alpha\). Passing to \(A/\fa_\alpha\), Ex. 11 shows \(S_\fp(\fa_\alpha)\) (the preimage of \(S_{\bar\fp}(0)\)) is a \(\fp\)-primary ideal \(\fq\). By (L1), \(\fq=(\fa_\alpha:x)\) for some \(x\notin\fp\). We claim \(\fa_\alpha=\fq\cap(\fa_\alpha+(x))\). The inclusion \(\subseteq\) is clear. Let \(y=a'+cx\in\fq\) with \(a'\in\fa_\alpha\). Then \(yx\in\fa_\alpha\), so \(cx^2\in\fa_\alpha\), i.e. \(c\in(\fa_\alpha:x^2)\subseteq S_\fp(\fa_\alpha)=(\fa_\alpha:x)\) (as \(x^2\notin\fp\)). Hence \(cx\in\fa_\alpha\) and \(y\in\fa_\alpha\).

Successor stage. Put \(\fq_{\alpha+1}=\fq\) and \(x_{\alpha+1}=x\). Let \(M\) be the set of ideals \(\fb\supseteq\fa_\alpha+(x)\) with \(\fq\cap\fb=\fa_\alpha\); it contains \(\fa_\alpha+(x)\), and the union of a chain in \(M\) lies in \(M\), so by Zorn \(M\) has a maximal element \(\fa_{\alpha+1}\). Then \(x\in\fa_{\alpha+1}\), \(\fa_\alpha\subseteq\fp\) and \(x\notin\fp\) give \(\fa_{\alpha+1}\supsetneq\fa_\alpha\), and \((\ast_{\alpha+1})\) holds since \(\fa=\bigcap_{\beta\le\alpha}\fq_\beta\cap(\fq\cap\fa_{\alpha+1})\). (Note \(\fa_{\alpha+1}\not\subseteq\fp\).)

Limit stage \(\lambda\). Put \(\fa_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}\fa_\beta\). If \(y\in\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}\fq_\beta\cap\fa_\lambda\) then \(y\in\fa_\beta\) for some \(\beta<\lambda\), so \(y\in\bigcap_{\gamma\le\beta}\fq_\gamma\cap\fa_\beta=\fa\); hence \(\fa=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}\fq_\beta\cap\fa_\lambda\).

Termination. The \(\fa_\alpha\) are strictly increasing, and there is only a set of ideals, so at some stage \(\fa_\alpha=(1)\) (a limit stage has \(\fa_\lambda\ne(1)\) only if the process continues). Then \((\ast_\alpha)\) reads \(\fa=\bigcap_{\beta\le\alpha}\fq_\beta\), an intersection of primary ideals. ∎

Problem

Consider the following condition on a ring \(A\):

(L2) Given an ideal \(\fa\) and a descending chain \(S_1\supseteq S_2\supseteq\cdots\supseteq S_n\supseteq\cdots\) of multiplicatively closed subsets of \(A\), there exists an integer \(n\) such that \(S_n(\fa)=S_{n+1}(\fa)=\cdots\).

Prove that the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    Every ideal in \(A\) has a primary decomposition.

  2. (ii)

    \(A\) satisfies (L1) and (L2).

[For (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii), use Exercises 12 and 15. For (ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i) show, with the notation of the proof of Exercise 17, that if \(S_n=S_{\fp_1}\cap\dots\cap S_{\fp_n}\) then \(S_n\) meets \(\fa_n\), hence \(S_n(\fa_n)=(1)\), and therefore \(S_n(\fa)=\fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_n\). (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). (L2). If \(S_1\supseteq S_2\supseteq\cdots\) then \(S_1(\fa)\supseteq S_2(\fa)\supseteq\cdots\) (since \(S(\fa)=\bigcup_{s\in S}(\fa:s)\)), and by Ex. 12 the set of all ideals \(S(\fa)\) is finite, so the chain stabilizes.

(L1). Let \(\fa\ne(1)\) and \(\fp\) prime. Let \(\fa=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\fq_i\) be a minimal decomposition, \(\fp_i=r(\fq_i)\), and \(\Sigma=\{\fp_i:\fp_i\subseteq\fp\}\). \(\Sigma\) is isolated (if \(\fp_k\subseteq\fp_j\in\Sigma\) then \(\fp_k\subseteq\fp\)). Let \(\fb=\bigcap_{\fp_i\notin\Sigma}\fp_i\) (\(=A\) if \(\Sigma\) is everything). We claim \(\fb\not\subseteq\fp\) and \(\fb\not\subseteq\fp_j\) for \(\fp_j\in\Sigma\): if \(\fb\subseteq\fp\) (resp. \(\fp_j\)), then since \(\prod_{\fp_i\notin\Sigma}\fp_i\subseteq\fb\) and \(\fp\) is prime, some \(\fp_i\notin\Sigma\) satisfies \(\fp_i\subseteq\fp\) (resp. \(\fp_i\subseteq\fp_j\subseteq\fp\)), contradicting \(\fp_i\notin\Sigma\). By prime avoidance (1.11) there is \(f\in\fb\) with \(f\notin\fp\) and \(f\notin\fp_j\) for all \(\fp_j\in\Sigma\). Then for each \(\fp_i\): \(f\in\fp_i\iff\fp_i\notin\Sigma\). By Ex. 15, \((\fa:f^n)=\fq_\Sigma\) for \(n\) large. By 4.9 applied to \(S=A-\fp\) (which meets \(\fp_i\) iff \(\fp_i\not\subseteq\fp\)), \(S_\fp(\fa)=\bigcap_{\fp_i\subseteq\fp}\fq_i=\fq_\Sigma\). So \(x=f^n\notin\fp\) satisfies \(S_\fp(\fa)=(\fa:x)\).

(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). Let \(\fa\ne(1)\) and run the construction of Ex. 17 (with the following precise choice in the successor step: \(\fa_n\) is maximal in \(M_n=\{\fb\supseteq\fa_{n-1}+(x_n):\ \fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_n\cap\fb=\fa\}\) — this set contains \(\fa_{n-1}+(x_n)\) because \(\fq_n\cap(\fa_{n-1}+(x_n))=\fa_{n-1}\)). Also \(S_n\cap\fp_i=\emptyset\) for \(i\le n\), so \(S_n(\fq_i)=\fq_i\) (4.8). Hence by Ex. 12(i), \(S_n(\fa)=S_n(Q_n)\cap S_n(\fa_n)=Q_n\).

So \(\fa_{N+1}\in M_N\), and maximality of \(\fa_N\) in \(M_N\) forces \(\fa_{N+1}=\fa_N\). But \(x_{N+1}\in\fa_{N+1}\) while \(x_{N+1}\notin\fp_{N+1}\supseteq\fa_N\), so \(\fa_{N+1}\ne\fa_N\): contradiction. Hence \(\fa_n=(1)\) for some finite \(n\), and then \(\fa=Q_n=\fq_1\cap\dots\cap\fq_n\) is a primary decomposition. ∎

Exercise 21

Problem

An element \(x\in A\) defines an endomorphism \(\phi_x\) of \(M\), namely \(m\mapsto xm\). The element \(x\) is said to be a zero-divisor (resp. nilpotent) in \(M\) if \(\phi_x\) is not injective (resp. is nilpotent). \((Q:M)\) is primary. \((Q:M)\ne(1)\) since \(Q\ne M\). Suppose \(xy\in(Q:M)\) and \(x\notin(Q:M)\). Then there is \(m\in M\) with \(xm\notin Q\), while \(y(xm)=(xy)m\in Q\). So \(y\) kills the non-zero class \(xm+Q\in M/Q\), i.e. \(y\) is a zero-divisor in \(M/Q\), hence nilpotent there: \(y^qM\subseteq Q\), i.e. \(y^q\in(Q:M)\). Thus \((Q:M)\) is primary, and \(\fp=r_M(Q)=r(Q:M)\) is prime (4.1).

Analogue of 4.3. If \(Q_1,\dots,Q_n\) are \(\fp\)-primary in \(M\), then \(Q=\bigcap Q_i\) is \(\fp\)-primary in \(M\). Proof: \(Q\ne M\) as \(Q\subseteq Q_1\); \(r_M(Q)=\bigcap r_M(Q_i)=\fp\) by Ex. 20(iii). Let \(x\) be a zero-divisor in \(M/Q\): \(xm\in Q\) for some \(m\notin Q\). Pick \(i\) with \(m\notin Q_i\); then \(xm\in Q_i\), so \(x\) is a zero-divisor in \(M/Q_i\), hence nilpotent there, so \(x\in r_M(Q_i)=\fp\). Then \(x^{q_j}M\subseteq Q_j\) for each \(j\), and with \(q=\max q_j\), \(x^qM\subseteq Q\): \(x\) is nilpotent in \(M/Q\).

Analogue of 4.4. Let \(Q\) be \(\fp\)-primary in \(M\), \(m\in M\), and \((Q:m)=\{a\in A:am\in Q\}\). Then:

  1. (i)

    if \(m\in Q\) then \((Q:m)=(1)\);

  2. (ii)

    if \(m\notin Q\) then \((Q:m)\) is \(\fp\)-primary (in \(A\)), hence \(r(Q:m)=\fp\);

  3. (iii)

    if \(x\in A-\fp\) then \((Q:_Mx)=\{m\in M:xm\in Q\}=Q\).

Proofs: (i) clear. (iii) \(x\notin\fp=r_M(Q)\) means \(x\) is not nilpotent in \(M/Q\), hence not a zero-divisor there (zero-divisors are nilpotent), so \(xm\in Q\Rightarrow m\in Q\). If \(a\in(Q:m)\) then \(a\) kills the non-zero class \(m+Q\), so \(a\) is a zero-divisor in \(M/Q\), hence in \(\fp\); so \((Q:M)\subseteq(Q:m)\subseteq\fp\), and taking radicals \(r(Q:m)=\fp\). If instead \(z\in\fp\), then \(z^q\in(Q:M)\subseteq(Q:m)\). So \((Q:m)\) is primary. ∎

Exercise 22

Problem

A primary decomposition of \(N\) in \(M\) is a representation of \(N\) as an intersection \(N=Q_1\cap\dots\cap Q_n\) of primary submodules of \(M\); it is a minimal primary decomposition if the ideals \(\fp_i=r_M(Q_i)\) are all distinct and if none of the components \(Q_i\) can be omitted from the intersection, that is if \(Q_i\not\supseteq\bigcap_{j\ne i}Q_j\) (\(1\le i\le n\)).

Let \(N=\bigcap Q_i\) be minimal, \(\fp_i=r_M(Q_i)\). For \(m\in M\), \((N:m)=\bigcap_i(Q_i:m)\) (Ex. 20), and by the analogue of 4.4, \((Q_i:m)=(1)\) if \(m\in Q_i\) and \(\fp_i\)-primary (radical \(\fp_i\)) if not. Hence

\[ r(N:m)=\bigcap_{i:\ m\notin Q_i}\fp_i . \]

So this is a minimal primary decomposition of \(0\) in \(M/N\) with the same radicals; the \(\fp_i\) are the primes belonging to \(0\) in \(M/N\). ∎

Problem

State and prove the analogues of (4.6)–(4.11) inclusive. (There is no loss of generality in taking \(N=0\).)

Solution

Let \(M\) be an \(A\)-module in which \(0\) is decomposable: \(0=Q_1\cap\dots\cap Q_n\), a minimal primary decomposition, \(\fp_i=r_M(Q_i)\). A prime \(\fp_i\) is isolated (minimal) if it is minimal in \(\{\fp_1,\dots,\fp_n\}\), otherwise embedded. Let \(S(N)\) denote the contraction in \(M\) of \(S^{-1}N\subseteq S^{-1}M\) (i.e. \(\{m:sm\in N\text{ for some }s\in S\}\)), for a submodule \(N\).

(4.6\('\)) Any prime ideal \(\fp\supseteq\Ann(M)\) contains a minimal prime belonging to \(0\), so the minimal primes of \(\Ann(M)\) (minimal among primes containing \(\Ann M\)) are exactly the minimal elements of \(\{\fp_1,\dots,\fp_n\}\). Proof: by Ex. 20, \(r(\Ann M)=r_M(0)=\bigcap\fp_i\). If \(\fp\supseteq\Ann(M)\) then \(\fp\supseteq r(\Ann M)=\bigcap\fp_i\supseteq\prod\fp_i\), so \(\fp\supseteq\fp_i\) for some \(i\) (1.11). Also each \(\fp_i\supseteq\bigcap\fp_j=r(\Ann M)\) contains \(\Ann M\). Hence the minimal primes over \(\Ann M\) are the minimal \(\fp_i\).

(4.7\('\)) The set of zero-divisors in \(M\) is \(\bigcup_{i=1}^n\fp_i=\{x\in A:(0:_Mx)\ne0\}\), i.e. \(\bigcup_{m\ne0}r(0:m)\). Proof: if \(x\) is a zero-divisor, \(xm=0\) with \(m\ne0\), so \(x\in(0:m)\subseteq r(0:m)=\bigcap_{m\notin Q_i}\fp_i\subseteq\fp_j\) for some \(j\) (there is at least one \(i\) with \(m\notin Q_i\) since \(m\ne0\)). Conversely, by Ex. 22, \(\fp_i=r(0:m_i)\) with \(m_i\ne0\) and \((0:m_i)\) \(\fp_i\)-primary; if \(x\in\fp_i\) then \(x^km_i=0\) for some \(k\); with \(k\) minimal, \(x^{k-1}m_i\ne0\) is killed by \(x\), so \(x\) is a zero-divisor. (Applied to \(M=A\) this is 4.7.)

(4.8\('\)) Let \(S\) be multiplicatively closed and \(Q\) a \(\fp\)-primary submodule of \(M\). (i) If \(S\cap\fp\ne\emptyset\) then \(S^{-1}Q=S^{-1}M\). (ii) If \(S\cap\fp=\emptyset\) then \(S^{-1}Q\) is \(S^{-1}\fp\)-primary in \(S^{-1}M\) and its contraction in \(M\) is \(Q\). Proof: (i) Let \(s\in S\cap\fp\), \(s^kM\subseteq Q\). Then \(m/t=s^km/s^kt\in S^{-1}Q\). (ii) Contraction. If \(m/1=q/s\) (\(q\in Q\)), then \(u(sm-q)=0\) for some \(u\in S\), so \(usm\in Q\) and \(us\notin\fp\), hence \(m\in Q\) by (4.4\('\))(iii). So the contraction is \(Q\); in particular \(S^{-1}Q\ne S^{-1}M\). Primary: let \(a/s\) be a zero-divisor in \(S^{-1}M/S^{-1}Q=S^{-1}(M/Q)\): \((a/s)(m/t)=0\) with \(m/t\ne0\), i.e. \(uam\in Q\) for some \(u\in S\) while \(um\notin Q\) (else \(m/t=0\)). So \(a\) is a zero-divisor on \(M/Q\), hence \(a^kM\subseteq Q\), and \((a/s)^k\) kills \(S^{-1}M/S^{-1}Q\). Radical: if \((x/s)^kS^{-1}M\subseteq S^{-1}Q\), then for each \(m\), \(x^km/s^k=q/t\), so \(u\,t\,x^km\in Q\) for some \(u\in S\); with \(ut\notin\fp\) we get \(utx^k\in(Q:M)\subseteq\fp\), so \(x\in\fp\). Conversely \(x\in\fp\Rightarrow x^kM\subseteq Q\). So \(r_{S^{-1}M}(S^{-1}Q)=S^{-1}\fp\).

(4.9\('\)) Let \(0=\bigcap Q_i\) be minimal and \(S\) meet \(\fp_{m+1},\dots,\fp_n\) but not \(\fp_1,\dots,\fp_m\). Then \(S^{-1}0=\bigcap_{i\le m}S^{-1}Q_i\) and \(S(0)=\bigcap_{i\le m}Q_i\), and these are minimal primary decompositions. Proof: \(0=S^{-1}0=\bigcap_iS^{-1}Q_i\) (3.4) \(=\bigcap_{i\le m}S^{-1}Q_i\) by (4.8\('\))(i), and each \(S^{-1}Q_i\) (\(i\le m\)) is \(S^{-1}\fp_i\)-primary by (4.8\('\))(ii); the \(S^{-1}\fp_i\) are distinct since the \(\fp_i\) are. Irredundant: if \(\bigcap_{j\ne i,j\le m}S^{-1}Q_j\subseteq S^{-1}Q_i\), contracting gives \(\bigcap_{j\ne i,j\le m}Q_j\subseteq Q_i\), hence \(\bigcap_{j\ne i}Q_j\subseteq Q_i\), contradicting minimality of the original decomposition. Contracting the decomposition gives \(S(0)=\bigcap_{i\le m}Q_i\) by (4.8\('\))(ii).

(4.10\('\)) (Second uniqueness theorem.) Let \(\{\fp_{i_1},\dots,\fp_{i_m}\}\) be an isolated set of primes belonging to \(0\) (a set \(\Sigma\) such that \(\fp'\) belonging to \(0\), \(\fp'\subseteq\fp\in\Sigma\) implies \(\fp'\in\Sigma\)). Then \(Q_{i_1}\cap\dots\cap Q_{i_m}\) is independent of the decomposition. Proof: let \(S=A-\bigcup_{\fp\in\Sigma}\fp\), multiplicatively closed. For \(\fp'\) belonging to \(0\): \(\fp'\in\Sigma\Rightarrow\fp'\cap S=\emptyset\); and \(\fp'\notin\Sigma\Rightarrow\fp'\not\subseteq\bigcup_{\fp\in\Sigma}\fp\) (by 1.11 and isolatedness), so \(\fp'\cap S\ne\emptyset\). By (4.9\('\)), \(Q_{i_1}\cap\dots\cap Q_{i_m}=S(0)\), which depends only on \(0\) and \(\Sigma\) (the \(\fp_i\) being intrinsic by Ex. 22).

(4.11\('\)) The isolated primary components (those \(Q_i\) with \(\fp_i\) minimal) are uniquely determined by \(M\). Proof: the set of minimal \(\fp_i\) is isolated, and for a single minimal \(\fp_i\) the set \(\{\fp_i\}\) is isolated; by (4.10\('\)) \(Q_i=S(0)\) with \(S=A-\fp_i\) (which meets \(\fp_j\) iff \(\fp_j\not\subseteq\fp_i\)), which is independent of the decomposition.

Remark. Taking \(M=A\) and \(N\) an ideal recovers 4.6–4.11 (via \(M=A/\fa\)). ∎
